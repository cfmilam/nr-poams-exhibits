📐 Mathematical Derivation

Click each step to reveal the complete mathematical derivation from basic orbital mechanics to the varying G* formula.

Step 1: Basic Orbital Angular Momentum
$$L = mvr$$

For any orbiting system, angular momentum equals mass times velocity times radius.

Step 2: Gravitational Orbital Condition
$$\frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{GmM}{r^2}$$
$$v^2 = \frac{GM}{r}$$

Centripetal force equals gravitational force for stable circular orbits.

Step 3: Angular Momentum in Terms of G
$$L = mvr = m\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} \cdot r = m\sqrt{GMr}$$
$$L^2 = m^2GMr$$
$$r = \frac{L^2}{m^2GM}$$

Expressing orbit radius in terms of angular momentum and the gravitational parameter GM.

Step 4: The POAMS Insight
$$L_{total} = \hbar = L_{orbital} + L_{spin}$$

In quantum systems, total angular momentum is conserved at ℏ (reduced Planck constant). When we add spin energy, the orbital component must adjust to maintain this total.

Step 5: Spin Energy Effects
$$K_{spin} = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} \text{ J (spin energy of the pattern QM calls the electron)}$$
$$v_{orbital} = \sqrt{v^2 - \frac{2K_{spin}}{m}}$$

Spin kinetic energy affects the orbital velocity. The system must maintain energy and angular momentum conservation simultaneously.

Step 6: Variable G* Derivation
$$G^* = \frac{\hbar v}{mM}$$

From Einstein's Lost Legacy Appendix 5: G becomes a function of orbital velocity, which depends on spin contribution.

This is exactly the Bohr radius! No fitting parameters, no assumptions about "electromagnetic forces" - just angular momentum conservation with spin.
