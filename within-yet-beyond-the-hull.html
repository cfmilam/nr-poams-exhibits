Abstract

Reports that a recovered craft is “larger inside than outside” appear contradictory only if one assumes that the exterior hull and the experienced interior must occupy one pre-existing Euclidean container. Normal Realism and POAMS do not make that assumption. Space is not a substance or stage. It is an observer’s stable projection of completed relations among retained organizations. The exterior and interior dimensions of a craft are therefore two operational reconstructions from two sets of completed exchanges. They may be locally regular without admitting one global Euclidean embedding.

This paper supplies a constructive mathematical account. Reality is represented first as a discrete network of completion addresses, not a continuum. Each stable adjacency has an operational cost obtained by clocked, co-signed exchanges. Shortest accumulated costs define an intrinsic metric. A small exterior interface can be joined to an arbitrarily large internal network; neither the diameter nor the number of internal addresses is bounded by the interface’s diameter. This result is elementary and exact. It removes the logical contradiction but does not, by itself, build a craft.

The POAMS physical proposal is that a sufficiently organized circulation may maintain two locally coherent completion networks and a threshold map between them. A crossing body is not compressed into a hidden box. Its retained whole-turn organization is re-addressed: local internal relations remain intact while its permitted external adjacencies change from the exterior network to the interior network. Metric volume is not a conserved book because metric volume is not substance. Angular momentum and whole-turn closure remain conserved; action-rate, energy, momentum, and every completed boundary exchange still require explicit receipts.

The construction yields distinct observational regimes. A pure adjacency transformation can produce greater internal extent with no clock-rate anomaly and no local scale change. A rate-weighted transformation adds clock and ranging offsets. A dynamically maintained threshold can make the geometry depend on craft state, loading, phase, or entry order. Each regime has different measurements and failure conditions. The proposal is therefore not “extra dimensions” under a new name. It is a specific account of how spatial appearance can change when the relational organization from which space is projected changes.

The stronger conjecture developed below treats the craft as a fibered completion system. Exterior observers resolve a compact base address; entrants gain access to a large internal fiber of completion relations. The threshold supplies a closure-preserving connection between the two. Its measurable signatures are holonomy, finite cut capacity, spectral stability, phase-matching tolerances, and a state-dependent internal metric. These additions do not make the proposal established. They make it sufficiently definite to calculate, criticize, and compare with hardware.

1. The alleged impossibility

Let an exterior survey assign a craft a hull diameter \(D_{\mathrm{out}}\). Let an entrant assign its interior a traversable diameter \(D_{\mathrm{in}}\), with

\[ D_{\mathrm{in}} > D_{\mathrm{out}}. \]

In ordinary container language, every interior point must be a point in the bounded Euclidean region enclosed by the hull. Under that assumption,

\[ \operatorname{diam}(\text{interior}) \leq \operatorname{diam}(\text{hull}), \]

and the report is impossible, mistaken, or deceptive.

But the inequality is not a measurement law. It follows from a prior ontological choice: both surveys are assumed to be restrictions of one ambient Euclidean metric. The contradiction therefore proves only this conditional:

If the exterior and interior observations inherit one common Euclidean embedding, a larger operational interior cannot lie inside the smaller surveyed hull.

The reported phenomenon, if sound, denies the antecedent. The proper question is not how a large Euclidean room was squeezed into a small Euclidean shell. It is how two locally coherent spatial reconstructions could meet at one operational threshold without belonging to one global Euclidean embedding.

That is a geometry problem before it is a materials problem.

2. Ontological ground: no container called space

2.1 One substance

POAMS admits one physical substance: organized angular momentum—turning, circulation, rotational time. Retained bodies, clocks, rulers, signals, observers, walls, and instruments are organizations of that same substance. None sits in a second substance called space.

The foundational relation

\[ L=\frac{dS}{d\theta} \]

is the smooth representation of a discrete physical book: persistent organizations close in whole turns. The continuum is the far-field drawing through an integer staircase, not the substrate beneath it.

2.2 Space as projection

An observer does not receive “space.” The observer receives completed differences: this return followed that emission; this surface closed an optical transaction before that surface; this rigid body required so many local steps to carry from one retained organization to another; these two paths returned equal counts; this loop closed.

From stable ratios among such completions, the observer constructs a spatial map. Distance is therefore an organized comparison, not a cord of substance stretched through a void. In the corpus translation ledger, the metre is read as a time-projection calibrated through \(c\); \(r/c\) is a projection-ledger entry, not the travel time of a resident pellet called a photon.

2.3 The observer is also relational

The entrant is not a container of private experience. The entrant is a retained census of closures: biological, mechanical, vestibular, optical, acoustic, and mnemonic. Crossing a threshold can alter the entrant’s permitted relations without requiring a second stage behind the first. “Inside” names the network in which the entrant’s continuing exchanges close.

2.4 What must remain conserved

A change of spatial projection does not license missing books. The following remain non-negotiable:

  • whole-turn closure of retained organizations;
  • angular-momentum accounting;
  • co-signing of every completed exchange;
  • action and rate receipts at the declared boundary;
  • continuity, or a precisely identified discontinuity, of the entering body’s retained organization;
  • a balancing entry for every repeatable energetic or momentum change.

Spatial volume is absent from this list. Volume is a derived property of a metric reconstruction. It is not a conserved substance.

3. The discrete relational construction

The mathematics begins with the least structure needed to reconstruct geometry.

3.1 Completion network

Let

\[ G=(V,E) \]

be a connected graph.

  • \(V\) is a set of retained completion addresses.
  • \(E\) is a set of repeatable, co-signed adjacencies.
  • Each edge \(e\in E\) has a positive operational price \(w(e)>0\).

The price is not assumed to be a little length residing on the edge. It is the reproducible count assigned by a declared ranging procedure: clock count, rigid-step count, phase count, or another disclosed comparison. Different procedures may initially supply different edge-price families and must be shown to agree before they are called one geometry.

For a path \(P=(e_1,\ldots,e_k)\), define its accumulated price

\[ W(P)=\sum_{j=1}^{k} w(e_j). \]

The intrinsic operational distance is

\[ d_G(u,v)=\inf_{P:u\leadsto v} W(P). \]

Because the graph is discrete and the weights are positive, this defines a metric on connected addresses. Continuous curves and metric tensors may approximate this book when the address census is dense. They are not required at foundation.

3.2 Exterior and interior books

Let

\[ G_{\mathrm{out}}=(V_{\mathrm{out}},E_{\mathrm{out}},w_{\mathrm{out}}) \]

be the network from which exterior observers reconstruct the craft and its surroundings. Let

\[ G_{\mathrm{in}}=(V_{\mathrm{in}},E_{\mathrm{in}},w_{\mathrm{in}}) \]

be the network from which entrants reconstruct the craft’s interior.

Let \(A_{\mathrm{out}}\subset V_{\mathrm{out}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{in}}\subset V_{\mathrm{in}}\) be the addresses participating in the doorway or threshold. A threshold rule

\[ T:A_{\mathrm{out}}\longleftrightarrow A_{\mathrm{in}} \]

specifies which completion addresses on one side co-sign crossings with which addresses on the other.

The total operational network is the gluing

\[ G=G_{\mathrm{out}}\sqcup_T G_{\mathrm{in}}, \]

meaning the disjoint networks plus the declared threshold adjacencies. This notation does not place two spaces in a larger space. It defines one relational book assembled from two locally coherent address families.

3.3 The elementary theorem

Theorem — interface size does not bound intrinsic extent. For every positive interface diameter \(a\), every desired internal diameter \(R\), and every desired finite internal address count \(N\), there exists a weighted connected network \(G_{\mathrm{in}}\) joined to an exterior network through an interface of diameter no greater than \(a\), while

\[ \operatorname{diam}(G_{\mathrm{in}})\ge R \]

and

\[ |V_{\mathrm{in}}|\ge N. \]

Construction. Choose one threshold address \(q\). Attach to \(q\) a path of \(m\) edges, each of weight \(\ell\), with \(m\ell\ge R\). If at least \(N\) addresses are required, choose \(m+1\ge N\), or attach an \(n\times n\times n\) lattice beyond \(q\). The interface may remain the single address \(q\), hence diameter zero in the induced interface metric, while the internal diameter and address census grow without bound. ∎

The theorem is mathematically modest and physically consequential. A boundary aperture does not determine the intrinsic extent of the network reached through it. The familiar bound arises only when the reached network is required to embed isometrically inside the exterior hull.

3.4 Why this is not a trick with labels

A relabeled database can also contain many nodes behind one icon. That alone is not a physical room. A physical interior requires the graph prices to be instantiated by repeatable, mutually consistent completions among matter, instruments, and observers. If an entrant walks ten rigidly calibrated metres, sends and receives ranging exchanges, places objects at separated addresses, and later retrieves them with the same local standards, then the internal network has operational extent. The proposal does not reduce that extent to imagination. It denies only that operational extent must inherit the exterior hull’s embedding.

4. Three distinct meanings of “larger inside”

The phrase can conceal different physical claims.

4.1 Greater capacity

The internal address census supports more retained objects than an exterior Euclidean volume estimate permits. This is a cardinality or occupancy claim.

4.2 Greater intrinsic distance

Rigid-step, acoustic, optical, or clocked ranging produces internal path lengths larger than the hull’s exterior diameter. This is a metric claim.

4.3 Non-embeddable layout

The internal adjacency graph cannot be placed inside the exterior hull while preserving measured local distances and adjacencies. This is stronger than either visual spaciousness or one long corridor.

A scientifically valuable account must say which of these occurred. All three together would be far stronger than any one alone.

5. The physical threshold: re-addressing without destruction

The graph theorem removes contradiction. It does not explain how matter crosses. POAMS must now pay the physical bill.

5.1 A body as a retained closure complex

Represent an entering body (B) not as material points occupying coordinates but as a finite retained subnetwork

\[ B=(V_B,E_B,n_B,\phi_B), \]

where:

  • \(V_B,E_B\) encode its persistent internal adjacencies;
  • \(n_B\in\mathbb Z^k\) records relevant whole-turn closure grades;
  • \(\phi_B\) records phase relations needed for continuation.

The body persists if its defining internal closure relations remain within their permitted tolerances through the crossing. Its identity is not the list of ambient coordinates assigned to it.

5.2 External address is not internal constitution

Separate two books:

  1. Constitution book: relations by which the body remains that body.
  2. Custody book: relations by which the body continues with its surroundings.

Ordinary motion changes the custody book continuously while largely preserving the constitution book. The proposed threshold performs a more radical custody update: external adjacencies to \(G_{\mathrm{out}}\) close out and adjacencies to \(G_{\mathrm{in}}\) open, while the body’s defining internal closures remain co-signed.

Schematically,

\[ (B,\,C_{\mathrm{out}})\xrightarrow{\;T\;}(B',\,C_{\mathrm{in}}), \]

with a successful non-destructive crossing requiring an isomorphism of retained constitution,

\[ B'\cong B, \]

up to ordinary reversible deformations and rate-state changes.

The body is not miniaturized. Its environmental address changes.

5.3 Whole-turn threshold condition

Let \(n_k^-\) and \(n_k^+\) be the closure grades of each protected organizational channel immediately before and after crossing. A minimal continuity condition is

\[ n_k^+=n_k^- \]

for every protected channel (k), together with phase matching

\[ \phi_k^+ = \phi_k^- + 2\pi m_k,\qquad m_k\in\mathbb Z. \]

This says the threshold may advance whole turns but may not strand the body at an unclosed fractional organization. A real mechanism would have to identify the protected channels, tolerances, and crossing cadence. The equations state the debt; they do not pretend it has already been paid.

5.4 Co-signed boundary accounting

For the complete body-plus-threshold-plus-craft boundary, write the discrete angular-momentum receipt for crossing event (j) as

\[ \Delta \mathbf L_B^{(j)}+ \Delta \mathbf L_T^{(j)}+ \Delta \mathbf L_C^{(j)}+ \Delta \mathbf L_R^{(j)}=0, \]

where \(T\) is the threshold organization, \(C\) the craft, and \(R\) all remaining receiving seats included in the declared boundary. Corresponding energy/action-rate and linear-recoil receipts must close under their disclosed calibrations.

If an entrant acquires orientation, momentum, heat, or a rate change, the opposite posting must appear somewhere in the complete apparatus. Re-addressing is not permission for conservation failure.

6. Where the large interior comes from

The larger interior is not manufactured by stretching empty space. It follows from the adjacency book maintained after the threshold.

6.1 Exterior observation

Exterior observers close exchanges with the hull and doorway addresses available in \(G_{\mathrm{out}}\). Their reconstruction yields a compact boundary with diameter \(D_{\mathrm{out}}\). Unless the craft exposes internal addresses to exterior completion channels, the exterior survey contains no measurement of the interior network’s diameter.

6.2 Crossing

At the threshold, the entrant’s local constitution is maintained while its permitted custody relations change. The doorway is therefore not principally a hole through material. It is the physical organization that enforces the map \(T\) and closes the corresponding receipts.

6.3 Interior observation

After crossing, the entrant’s optical, acoustic, mechanical, and locomotor exchanges close predominantly with \(G_{\mathrm{in}}\). If those completions are stable and mutually calibrated, the entrant reconstructs a regular interior. Its measured diameter is

\[ D_{\mathrm{in}}=sup_{u,v\in V_{\mathrm{in}}}d_{G_{\mathrm{in}}}(u,v), \]

which has no mathematical dependence on \(D_{\mathrm{out}}\) unless an additional embedding law imposes one.

6.4 Exit

An exit event reverses the custody transition while preserving the retained body’s protected closures. Memory and carried artifacts can persist because they are part of the entrant’s retained constitution, not data stored in exterior coordinates.

6.5 Why the hull does not secretly contain the room

The statement “the room is inside the craft” is operational shorthand: the room is reached by crossing the craft’s threshold and is controlled by the craft’s maintained organization. It does not mean the room’s addresses form a Euclidean subset of the volume inferred from the hull.

The craft contains the access and maintenance relation, not necessarily the interior metric volume.

7. The circulation that would have to maintain it

The preceding account remains geometry until an actual circulation is named. POAMS therefore proposes a research form, not a completed engineering claim.

7.1 Required organization

A viable craft must maintain at least:

\[ Lk=Tw+Wr. \]

This identity does not derive the craft mechanism. It does, however, name a lawful way for one conserved topological grade to be partitioned among local internal turning and global winding. A candidate craft organization might maintain its exterior and interior address books through different partitions of one closed linking account, while the threshold controls conversion between partitions.

That possibility is explicitly conjectural. To promote it, one must identify:

  • the physical loop or linked circulation;

  • the control that changes the partition;
  • the predicted geometry change before observing it;
  • the balancing mechanical, thermal, electrical, and rate receipts.

Invoking \(Lk=Tw+Wr\) without those items would merely rename the mystery.

7.3 Rate organization

The Pope–Osborne orthogonal-turn premise states that internal count and projected motion are orthogonal, equally calibrated projections of one local turning budget:

\[ a^2+b^2=1,\qquad a=\frac{d\tau}{dt},\qquad b=\frac{|ds|}{c\,dt}. \]

This supplies an existing POAMS relation between internal count and spatial projection. It shows that spatial measure and internal rate are not independent ontological ingredients. But it does not by itself generate a large interior, choose the graph \(G_{\mathrm{in}}\), or derive the threshold map \(T\).

Its relevance is conditional: if the craft maintains different rate partitions on the two sides, clock and distance reconstructions may change together. The local segment book becomes

\[ d\tau^2=dt^2-\frac{ds^2}{c^2}, \]

or, solving for projected path per internal count,

\[ \frac{ds}{d\tau}=c\,\frac{b}{a}. \]

As \(a\) decreases relative to \(b\), a larger projected path can accrue per unit of the entrant’s internal count. This is a candidate rate-weighted expansion, distinct from pure adjacency expansion.

7.4 Two mechanisms must not be conflated

Adjacency expansion: \(G_{\mathrm{in}}\) contains more addresses or longer intrinsic paths while local clock and ruler calibrations remain ordinary. No universal internal/external clock offset is required.

Rate-weighted expansion: edge prices themselves differ because internal count and projected-path channels are repartitioned. Clocks, propagation comparisons, chemistry, decay, and traversals may show common rate effects.

A real specimen could instantiate either or both. Observations must decide; vocabulary must not.

8A. Strong conjecture: the craft as a fibered completion system

The graph-gluing construction proves possibility but says little about organization. A more powerful model treats the craft as a fibered completion system.

Let \(B\) be the exterior completion network—the compact address reconstructed as the hull and its surroundings. For each relevant exterior address \(x\in B\), let \(F_x\) be the set of internal completion states maintained over that address. The complete relational state is

\[ E=\bigsqcup_{x\in B}F_x, \]

with projection

\[ \pi:E\rightarrow B, \qquad \pi(y)=x\quad\text{for }y\in F_x. \]

The symbol \(E\) is not a higher-dimensional container. It is the full address book. The projection \(\pi\) states which distinctions remain visible to an exterior observer. Many internally distinct states may share the same exterior address:

\[ y_1\neq y_2,\qquad \pi(y_1)=\pi(y_2)=x. \]

An exterior survey resolves \(x\) and nearby base relations. It does not thereby resolve the intrinsic metric, address census, or topology of \(F_x\). The hull can therefore remain compact in \(B\) while the fiber \(F_x\) supports an extensive interior geometry.

This is the sharper answer to “where is the room?” The room is not elsewhere in an ambient space. It is the family of completion distinctions suppressed by the exterior projection and made available after threshold enrollment.

8A.1 Local product without global container

Near a stable threshold, the relational book may locally factor as

\[ E|_U \cong U\times F, \]

where \(U\subset B\) is a small exterior neighborhood and \(F\) is a representative internal completion network. This is a local bookkeeping factorization. It says that the craft maintains both exterior relations and internal relations in that operating neighborhood. It does not assert that \(F\) is geometrically stacked beside \(U\) in a larger stage.

The internal metric \(d_F\) is independent of the exterior base metric \(d_B\) unless a constitutive law couples them. Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{diam}_{d_B}(U)\ll \operatorname{diam}_{d_F}(F) \]

is entirely admissible.

8A.2 External compactness as projection degeneracy

Define the exterior distinguishability relation

\[ y_1\sim_{\mathrm{out}}y_2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi(y_1)=\pi(y_2). \]

The exterior craft is the quotient description

\[ B\cong E/{\sim_{\mathrm{out}}}. \]

The apparent smallness is therefore not necessarily compression of internal distances. It may be projection degeneracy: many internal distinctions belong to one exterior equivalence class because exterior transactions do not close with them separately.

That yields a concrete question for recovered hardware: does the hull’s exterior response remain nearly invariant while internal state, occupancy, or layout changes? If so, exterior observation is quotienting internal distinctions rather than faithfully embedding them.

8B. Threshold connection and closure-preserving transport

A fiber family is not enough. A body needs a rule for moving from base-addressed custody into fiber-addressed custody.

8B.1 Protected closure lattice

Let the entering body’s protected whole-turn organization be represented by an integer closure vector

\[ \mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_k)^T\in\mathbb Z^k, \]

with a positive comparison form \(Q\) that prices departures from its retained organization:

\[ \|\mathbf n\|_Q^2=\mathbf n^TQ\mathbf n. \]

A non-destructive threshold map on the protected closure book must act by an integer automorphism

\[ M_T\in GL(k,\mathbb Z) \]

so complete turns remain complete. If the crossing preserves the protected comparison tariff, then

\[ M_T^TQM_T=Q. \]

This is a demanding restriction. The threshold cannot use an arbitrary continuous transformation that maps whole closures into fractional debris. It must preserve the closure lattice, and—on protected channels—the relevant norm.

The minimal identity crossing has \(M_T=I\). A nontrivial crossing may permute closure channels, reverse paired senses, or add whole winding subject to the same preservation law. Any claimed mechanism must name which case occurs.

8B.2 Phase connection

Let \(\boldsymbol\phi\in\mathbb T^k\) be the vector of closure phases, with \(\mathbb T^k\) the \(k\)-torus of phase angles modulo \(2\pi\), and define the embedded phase state

\[ \mathbf z= (e^{i\phi_1},\ldots,e^{i\phi_k})^T. \]

Along a crossing parameter \(s\), let a connection \(A_s\) act on this state in the smooth representation:

\[ \frac{d\mathbf z}{ds}=-A_s\mathbf z. \]

The transported phase state is

\[ \mathbf z(s_1)= \mathcal P\exp\!\left(-\int_{s_0}^{s_1}A_s\,ds\right) \mathbf z(s_0), \]

where \(\mathcal P\) records ordering when successive threshold operations do not commute.

POAMS reads this equation operationally: the threshold must advance and compare phases in an order that returns each protected circulation to an admitted closure address. The connection is not a field inhabiting the doorway. It is the rule implemented by the doorway’s organized circulation.

8B.3 Continuity criterion

Let \(U_T\) denote the total threshold transport on the body’s protected relational state \(\Psi_B\). A successful crossing requires

\[ \langle U_T\Psi_B,U_T\Psi_B\rangle_Q = \langle \Psi_B,\Psi_B\rangle_Q, \]

and

\[ U_T\Lambda_B=\Lambda_B, \]

where \(\Lambda_B\) is the body’s allowed whole-closure lattice. These conditions formalize “the same retained body exits the map.” They do not require imported quantum ontology; they require preservation of an organized comparison norm and integer closure set.

8B.4 Mismatch cost

If the actual threshold map is \(\widetilde U_T=U_T+\delta U\), define a mismatch functional

\[ \mathcal E_{\mathrm{mis}} = \kappa \left\| (I-P_{\Lambda})\widetilde U_T\Psi_B \right\|_Q^2, \]

where \(P_{\Lambda}\) projects onto admitted retained closures and \(\kappa\) is a measured tariff converting mismatch into an apparatus receipt. A viable threshold demands

\[ \mathcal E_{\mathrm{mis}}<\mathcal E_{\mathrm{crit}} \]

for every protected subsystem. Heating, disorientation, material damage, memory discontinuity, or failure to cross may be manifestations of mismatch, but no particular symptom follows until \(\kappa\) and the affected channels are measured.

8C. Holonomy: the threshold should leave a geometrical receipt

If transport depends on the path or sequence through the threshold, a closed operational loop need not return every orientation and phase comparison to its initial reading even when the traveler returns to the same exterior address.

For a closed route \(\gamma\) that enters, traverses the interior, exits, and closes externally, define the holonomy

\[ H(\gamma)= \mathcal P\exp\!\left(-\oint_{\gamma}A\right). \]

If

\[ H(\gamma)\neq I, \]

the joined geometry is anholonomic: a protected comparison returns rotated, phase-shifted, permuted, or otherwise rebooked after the closed circuit.

This is not decorative mathematics. It yields direct searches:

  • carry a triad of mechanical orientations around a closed entry–exit route;
  • compare independent optical, inertial, spin, and clock phases;
  • reverse the route and entry order;
  • test whether \(H(\gamma^{-1})=H(\gamma)^{-1}\);
  • compose two routes and test whether

\[ H(\gamma_2\circ\gamma_1) =H(\gamma_2)H(\gamma_1). \]

A reproducible non-identity holonomy would show that the threshold is doing more than opening a visually unusual room. It would identify the ordered geometrical operation imposed on retained comparisons.

The simplest adjacency-only construction permits \(H=I\). The stronger circulation conjecture expects nontrivial holonomy when threshold phase-matching, orientation conversion, or rate partition is active.

8D. The cut-capacity theorem: a large interior owes a bottleneck account

A small threshold can access a large internal network, but access capacity is not automatically unlimited.

Let \(S\subset V\) be the interior address set and \(\partial S\) the threshold cut: edges with one endpoint inside and one outside. Assign each threshold edge a finite completion capacity \(c_e\)—whole admitted transactions per exterior count. The cut capacity is

\[ C_{\partial S}=\sum_{e\in\partial S}c_e. \]

For any conserved account routed through the declared threshold, the max-flow/min-cut bound gives

\[ \Phi_{\mathrm{in}\leftrightarrow\mathrm{out}} \le C_{\partial S}. \]

This is a major consequence. Arbitrarily large intrinsic extent does not imply arbitrarily large exchange rate with the exterior. Unless the craft supplies additional boundary channels that exterior geometry fails to resolve, the doorway is a bandwidth bottleneck.

8D.1 Observable implications

  • Material crossing rate may saturate even when the interior appears empty.
  • Communication bandwidth may depend on doorway state rather than internal separation.
  • Added occupants or apparatus may increase threshold noise, latency, power, or phase error.
  • Simultaneous crossings may interfere even without mechanical crowding.
  • Closing the doorway may sharply reduce exterior coupling while leaving internal relations intact.

8D.2 The hidden-cut alternative

If measured exchange exceeds the visible doorway’s capacity, then at least one of the following is true:

  1. the capacity assigned to visible channels is wrong;
  2. the craft maintains additional threshold edges not resolved by exterior instruments;
  3. the relevant account does not cross the visible cut during operation because it is already retained internally;
  4. the declared system boundary is incomplete.

No explanation may simply declare infinite portal capacity. The excess must identify its additional co-signing seats.

8D.3 Address volume versus throughput

Define internal address volume

\[ \operatorname{Vol}(S)=\sum_{v\in S}\mu(v), \]

where \(\mu(v)\) is the operational weight of an admitted internal address. The expansion ratio

\[ h(S)=\frac{C_{\partial S}}{\operatorname{Vol}(S)} \]

measures coupling capacity per internal address volume. A very large interior reached through a modest threshold has small \(h(S)\) unless hidden channels scale with the interior. This predicts increasing isolation as the interior census grows.

8E. Spectral stability: why the interior may require its own circulation

Let \(L_G=D-W\) be the weighted graph Laplacian of the maintained completion network, where \(W\) is the weighted adjacency matrix and \(D\) its weighted degree matrix. Its second eigenvalue

\[ \lambda_2(L_G) \]

is the algebraic connectivity: a measure of how strongly the network resists splitting into weakly joined sectors.

A large internal fiber connected to the exterior only through a narrow cut generally drives the complete network’s \(\lambda_2\) downward. The system becomes susceptible to phase drift, slow settlement, or separation between interior and exterior books. Cheeger-type bounds make the relationship explicit:

\[ \frac{h_G^2}{2d_{\max}} \lesssim \lambda_2(L_G) \lesssim 2h_G, \]

for an appropriate weighted conductance \(h_G\) and maximum weighted degree \(d_{\max}\).

The physical reading is important: a huge interior cannot plausibly remain coherent merely because a small doorway touches it. It likely requires a strong internal circulation and settlement network. The threshold coordinates two already maintained organizations; it does not individually service every internal address from outside.

8E.1 Phase-locking threshold

For a coarse phase model

\[ \dot\phi_i=\omega_i+ K\sum_j W_{ij}\sin(\phi_j-\phi_i), \]

stable collective ordering requires coupling \(K\) sufficient to overcome the dispersion of native rates \(\omega_i\). The exact threshold is architecture-dependent, but stability should improve with algebraic connectivity and degrade with rate spread. A schematic requirement is

\[ K\lambda_2(L_G)>\Delta\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}. \]

This is not claimed as the craft’s literal control law. It is a calculable family of stability conditions. If threshold accessibility or interior geometry changes with measured phase coherence, drive rate, thermal disorder, damage, or loading, the spectral model becomes testable.

8E.2 Failure morphology

The model predicts several distinct failures:

  • loss of threshold lock: interior persists but becomes inaccessible;
  • fiber fragmentation: one interior separates into mutually inaccessible sectors;
  • metric drift: adjacency survives but edge prices change;
  • holonomy drift: repeated closed routes return different phase/orientation receipts;
  • closure failure: protected organizations cannot cross safely.

Those are different events and should not be collapsed into “the portal failed.”

8F. Dynamic relational metric and control variables

Let \(\chi\) denote the measured craft state: circulation rates, phase order, twist/writhe partition, electrical and thermal accounts, material configuration, and threshold controls. The internal graph and its edge prices become

\[ G_{\mathrm{in}}=G_{\mathrm{in}}(\chi), \qquad w_e=w_e(\chi). \]

The operational internal distance is therefore

\[ d_{\chi}(u,v)= \min_{P:u\leadsto v} \sum_{e\in P}w_e(\chi). \]

8G. Finite capacity and the maintenance ledger

The mathematical fiber may be arbitrarily large. A physical fiber maintained by finite circulation cannot be assumed to be.

Shared circulation may make the observed scaling sublinear, while congestion, error correction, or phase matching may make it superlinear. The additive expression is therefore a candidate resolved model, not a universal premise.

Three scaling families should be distinguished experimentally:

\[ P_{\mathrm{maint}}\propto N, \qquad P_{\mathrm{maint}}\propto N^{2/3}, \qquad P_{\mathrm{maint}}\propto \log N, \]

15. Falsification conditions

The account fails in progressively stronger senses if:

  1. reported events reduce to hidden exterior extent, scale error, memory, or perception;
  2. independent instruments reconstruct one ordinary Euclidean interior;
  3. no stable internal adjacency or metric exists;
  4. geometry changes show no relation to any physical craft state or maintained organization;
  5. a claimed crossing produces unbalanced energy, momentum, or angular-momentum receipts after the complete boundary is measured;
  6. the proposed POAMS circulation variable is prospectively varied and the geometry does not respond as predicted;
  7. a simpler operational model explains every preserved record with fewer unsupported premises.

The graph theorem itself will remain true. What would fail is its identification with the craft and, more specifically, the POAMS maintenance proposal.
