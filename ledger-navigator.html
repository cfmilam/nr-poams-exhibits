Flying the Solar System on Measured Books

Philosophy first: there is no pull and no void — only one angular-momentum ledger, every entry measured. This calculator flies a vehicle between planets on any date you choose, with everything in motion, using no gravitational constant and no masses anywhere: the Sun's pair invariant is read off Earth's own orbit (μ☉ = 4π² AU³/yr² — the exactness theorem of The Resonance Network), planetary positions come from their measured elements, and the transfer arc is solved in the ledger. Pick two worlds and a day. The books do the rest.

Pick a route and solve.

What this is — and its honest tier

All measured, nothing fitted: planetary positions on any date from standard measured mean elements + secular rates (planning-grade table, valid ~1800–2050); the one invariant μ⊙ read from Earth's own orbit; the transfer solved by a Lambert pass in that ledger; Δv = the vector gap between the arc and each planet's own measured motion at the endpoints (heliocentric planning Δv — parking-orbit injection bookkeeping not included). Kinematic, not mechanistic: the arc is a bookkeeping solution, not a pulled trajectory.
Limits, stated: arcminute-grade ephemeris ⇒ mission-planning and visualization grade, not operations targeting; two-centre ledger per arc (planetary flyby dressing and resonance-channel kicks not applied); short horizons only — long-horizon single trajectories are refused on principle (the momenta are exact forever; individual paths are not).
