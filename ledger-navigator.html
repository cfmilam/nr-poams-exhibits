The Ledger Navigator

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian “Viv” Pope & Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

Flying the Solar System on Measured Books

Within Normal Realism / POAMS, the transfer is a relation in one angular-momentum ledger, not a pulled trajectory through a void. This calculator combines imported fitted planetary positions with a declared two-centre transfer model. No gravitational constant or masses are entered into its calculation. Choose a route, departure date, and flight time; a result is shown only if the model's time-of-flight and endpoint checks pass. This is an illustrative planning calculation, not a flown mission.

Pick a route and solve.

Inputs, calculation, and scope

Imported positions: all six element values and all six centennial rates from JPL SSD's approximate-position Table 1 are used. They are fitted coefficients, not raw measurements or an independent POAMS ephemeris. The Earth entry is the Earth–Moon barycenter, not Earth's centre. The displayed velocity is the analytic time derivative of that same position formula, including changing elements and orientation. Both departure and arrival must lie from 1800-01-01 through 2050-01-01. Calendar midnight is treated as the table's day coordinate; sub-minute time-scale conversion is not modeled.
Declared transfer ledger: the fixed reference is μ₀ = 4π² AU³/(Julian yr)². This chooses a one-AU, one-year reference ellipse; it is not an exact measured value for the Sun. In the adopted isolated inverse-square ellipse, μ = ℓ(vp + va)/2 = 4π²a³/T², with specific angular momentum ℓ = rpvp = rava. The identity's conditional scope is set out in The Resonance Network. The transfer uses the corresponding relation r″ = −μ₀r/|r|³ as kinematic bookkeeping, not a new force ontology. The imported planets are not re-integrated in that normalized two-centre model.
Computed, not assumed: a universal-variable Lambert calculation selects the zero-revolution prograde branch. A separate initial-value conic calculation checks the endpoint position and velocity and draws 241 equal-time samples; the destination is never substituted for the computed endpoint. The time residual must be ≤ 10−9 max(1, T/yr) yr, the position residual ≤ 10−7 max(1, |rarrival|/AU) AU, and the velocity residual ≤ 10−7 max(1, |varrival|/(AU/yr)) AU/yr. These are numerical acceptance tolerances, not ephemeris uncertainties. Near-collinear or unvalidated cases are declined, not declared physically impossible.
Limits: the source's nominal longitude errors vary by body (10–600 arcseconds), and its distance errors are much larger than a rendezvous tolerance. This is not operations targeting or mission feasibility: no parking-orbit injection/capture, propulsion budget, clearance, flyby dressing, resonance kicks, or multi-revolution search is included. Δv is only the sum of two heliocentric vector gaps. The scan holds departure fixed and chooses the lowest validated sample at 10-day intervals from 60 to 1400 days; it does not prove a continuous or global optimum. The supported single-flight range is 30–3000 days, subject to the date boundary. See The Navigation Engine for the wider research program.

The view is an XY projection of the three-dimensional calculation. Rings are snapshot ellipses, not future ephemeris samples. Flight playback spans eight seconds, preserves the solved inputs, and ends at arrival; changing any route input clears the old result.
