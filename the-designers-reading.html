A First Word from the Creator

Rules, not places · hitscan, not projectiles · ticks, not flow · a ledger, not forces · multiplayer, no server · and no console underneath

This essay is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian “Viv” Pope & Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).
The designer's frame — and the refusal of “simulation” that completes it — was set by Christopher F. Milam (2026). This page is the corpus's front door: no equations are required of the reader, every claim is taught rigorously elsewhere, and each section ends with the door into the full exhibit that proves it.

The fastest way to understand this corpus is to stop thinking like a physicist inheriting three centuries of stage machinery, and start thinking like a game designer with an empty file.

A designer does not begin by building a place. There is no warehouse where the level “is.” A designer writes rules: what states exist, what counts as an event, what is conserved, what happens when two things engage. The world the player experiences — the space, the objects, the motion — is those rules, read out and projected. Space is the interface. The rules are the reality.

Normal Realism's claim is that our world is written the same way. Reality is not a container with things in it. It is a set of rules about geometry applied in time — one conserved substance (angular momentum: circulation, turning, rotational time) and a book-keeping discipline that never breaks. Everything below is one of those rules, stated the way a designer would state it, with the measured record standing behind every line.

1 · Rules, not places

Open any cosmology textbook and the first illustration is a circle labelled “the universe.” Pope called this the Naked Emperor: draw a chalk boundary, stand outside it like a god, and start legislating for “everything.” But no such standpoint exists — to be the universe, the chalk circle would have to contain the man with the chalk. The designer's translation: the map screen is not the level. There is no level file called “the universe,” no world-coordinates array stored anywhere. What exists is the ruleset and the event log — every engagement between systems, booked. “Space” is how a participant renders the log from its own seat. Ask “how big is the whole world, right now?” and you have asked for a global variable the engine never declares.

The full argument, with the cosmology it demolishes and the one it builds: The Polyverse §1.

2 · Hitscan, not projectiles

Every shooter distinguishes two ways to implement a shot. A projectile weapon spawns an object that flies frame-by-frame across the map and may hit something at the end. A hitscan weapon computes the engagement at the moment of firing: source and target are connected by a rule, the hit is booked, and nothing ever travels. The tracer the player sees is drawn afterwards, for the eye.

Extant physics insists light is a projectile — a “photon” object cruising through space at speed c. A century of paradox follows: the flying thing must somehow be a wave and a particle, must pass through both slits, must be told after the fact which it was. POAMS says light is hitscan. Relativity itself supplies the receipt: along a light connection the proper time is zero — $\tau^2 = t^2 - (s/c)^2 = 0$ — so in the connection's own books, emission and absorption are one event. Nothing is en route; there is no passenger; the “photon in flight” is the tracer graphic mistaken for the bullet. What propagates at the finite speed c is the ordering of engagements in each observer's log — the ratio of distance-units to time-units, as Bondi put it — not a traveller's cruise.

The same physical event, two readings. Projectile: an object crosses the stage, aging as it goes — and inherits every paradox of being somewhere in between. Hitscan: the engagement completes as one event ($\tau = 0$); the emitter's ledger and the absorber's ledger book it together; the “flight” is a drawing. The census button fires one-at-a-time engagements at a two-slit wall: no single engagement shows a fringe — the pattern lives in the accumulating census, exactly as measured (Tonomura 1989, one electron at a time). demo; the measured record is in the Two-Slit exhibit

Every “quantum paradox” is a hitscan engine read as a projectile engine. Delayed choice: there is no “after the photon passed” because nothing passed. The quantum eraser: labels and erasers are part of the connection geometry the engagement runs through when it closes. Which-path detection: a detector is not a spectator, it is an absorber — a real engagement partner that takes the connection for itself.

3 · A tick engine, all the way down

No engine computes a continuum. The world updates in ticks — discrete state changes, integer-counted — and motion is what the eye makes of frames. The corpus's first principle is that reality is built the same way: discreteum, not continuum. The substance comes only in whole turns — action in units of $h$, one completed closure at a time; a fractional turn is not a smaller thing but no thing, because it does not close and cannot persist. The integers are primary. Continuity is the far-field appearance of the tick engine — the movie read as motion — and the calculus we drape over it is the smooth ramp drawn through an integer staircase.

And the frame-rate rule runs in both directions. Zoom in and the ticks show: single clicks, whole quanta, one engagement at a time. Zoom out and the staircase blurs into the classical world — not different physics, the same parity map ruled too fine to see. A designer would call it level of detail; the textbooks call it the correspondence principle. Same rule.

The frames, run live: The Quantum Cinematograph. The staircase under the ramp: the creed on the hub.

4 · No forces in the engine — only the ledger

Ask a designer what a “force” is in their engine and they will tell you: nothing. There is no force object in memory. There are invariants — quantities the update rules conserve — and there are state changes that respect them. “Force” is a word players use for the feel of the constraint.

POAMS reads gravity exactly so. Nothing pulls. A free body rides the natural path its angular momentum assigns it — weightless, which is why free fall feels like nothing. Weight is the bill for standing off your natural orbit: the constraint force the ground must supply, a ledger entry, not a pull across a gap. And the tell that the extant reading has it backwards is its own patch history: insist gravity is a central mass-pull, and galaxy rotation curves stop adding up — so a bug-fix called “dark matter” is invoiced, an invisible mass painted wherever the pull-model fails. Read the disk as one organized circulation keeping its books and the curves are just what circulation does. Every epicycle — dark matter, the photon, the field-in-a-void — is the receipt for insisting on a force where there is only bookkeeping.

The ledger, audited at every scale: Mass as Trapped Rate · Varying G · the dark centres as empty barycentres: The Polyverse §3.

5 · Multiplayer, no server

Here is the design problem that decides everything. A multiplayer world needs all players to agree — but the naive solution, one authoritative server holding the master state and the master clock, is exactly what reality does not have. There is no master clock anywhere in physics. There are only clocks, plural: every atom keeping its own time at its own rate, set by its own state of motion and orbit.

Reality is peer-to-peer, and the netcode is exchange itself. Seats reconcile pairwise, wherever engagements complete. And the corpus proved this year that pairwise reconciliation is enough: because bookings compose along chains of completed hand-offs, a common ordering exists — constructed, not decreed, exactly the way the GPS constellation shares nanosecond time with no master clock in it, by mutual calibration alone. No God's-eye instant is ever consulted, because none exists to consult.

Even the deepest event in cosmology obeys the no-server rule. At the floor of the cosmic contraction — where the extant model places its “Big Bang” singularity and this corpus places a conservation wall — the dynamics go chunk-local: each region passes its own floor on its own books. No global tick, no universal bounce-moment, no cosmic frame-zero. The engine never needed one.

The reconciliation theorem, the shared accumulation, and the spin-pinned floor: The Polyverse §§5–8. The clocks: GPS Clocks · The Rate of Time.

6 · Now delete the console

At this point a modern reader reaches for the fashionable conclusion: “so we live in a simulation.” That is the one move this page exists to refuse, and the refusal is where the ontology becomes serious.

“Simulation” assumes a simulator — hardware somewhere, a machine the rules run on — and a something-other: a base reality the simulation is a copy of. Both are the oldest error in the book, committed at the largest possible scale. This corpus has a name for it: inventing a bearer for the bookkeeping — a cat drawn around a grin. The rules do not need a computer to run on, for the same reason the momentum does not need a particle to ride in: the grin, closed into a whole turn, is the cat. Nothing bears; nothing is borne.

And the simulator is the ultimate unauditable posit — an assumption placed, as Hoyle said of the Big Bang, “out of sight where it can never be challenged by direct appeal to observation,” with an infinite regress invoiced immediately: who simulates the simulator? “Base reality” is the chalk circle drawn one level up, with the man holding the chalk now standing outside existence itself.

This is also precisely where POAMS parts company with the digital-physics tradition it superficially resembles — Wheeler's it-from-bit, cellular-automaton worlds. Those keep a substrate: a lattice, a grid, and above all an external clock the engine steps through. POAMS keeps none. The deepest disanalogy in the whole game metaphor is this one: a game's clock lives outside the level — frames tick, the world updates. Here there is no outside clock, because the applying of the rules is what time is. The substance is its own rate of turning ($L = dS/d\theta$); reality is not rules applied in time so much as rules whose application is time. Everything is time — that is one of the two pillars the whole corpus stands on.

The teaching sequence, complete. Think like a designer to understand it: rules, not places; hitscan, not projectiles; ticks, not flow; a ledger, not forces; peers, no server. Then delete the designer, the console, and the frame clock — and notice that nothing collapses. The ruleset does not lose its floor when the hardware is removed, because the substance is its own execution. A game needs a console. Reality is the one ruleset that doesn't. It is not a simulation of anything. It just is this.
