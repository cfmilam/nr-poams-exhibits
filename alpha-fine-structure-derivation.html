1. Accept the measurement; calculate the consequences

POAMS begins with the world we can observe and measure. We accept the fine-structure ratio as an empirical anchor. A derivation of why its numerical value is what it is is not required before using an angular-momentum account to calculate sizes, energies, spectra and material relationships.

$$\alpha=0.0072973525643(11),\qquad K_Q=\alpha\hbar c.$$

The measurement calibrates the elementary pair coefficient in units of $\hbar c$. The equations then supply linked consequences under their stated assumptions. The full Coulomb demonstration shows the energy account, calculated values, interactive scale explorer and application map.

Source: NIST 2022 CODATA. Standard uncertainty is $1.1\times10^{-12}$; 80-digit arithmetic does not turn this into an 80-digit measurement. $h$, $c$ and measured masses are named inputs, not quantities claimed to emerge from $\alpha$.

2. What the measured anchor gives us

For the stated two-ended circular scale model, $\mu=m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)$ and

$$E_n(r)=\frac{n^2\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2}-\frac{\alpha\hbar c}{r} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_n=\frac{n^2\hbar}{\mu c\alpha},\quad E_n=-\frac{\mu c^2\alpha^2}{2n^2}.$$

Both ends participate; no fixed central nucleus is inserted. The circular calculation establishes gross scales, not the full angular state or all resolved spectral effects. It uses both the energy relation and orbital-action condition; the radius law does not follow from action quantization alone.

3. The same structural role for gravity: a companion calibration

Nothing in the elementary pair account fixes the coefficient's numerical value or restricts it to the electromagnetic case. For any pair of measured masses $m_a$, $m_b$ the gravitational analog reads

$$K_G(m_a,m_b)=Gm_am_b,\qquad \alpha_G(m_a,m_b)\equiv\frac{K_G}{\hbar c}=\frac{Gm_am_b}{\hbar c}.$$

$\alpha_G$ is a dimensionless calibrated pair-coupling coefficient in the same structural role as $\alpha$. Both are measured, not derived. Nothing here changes what the origin section below concludes about either.

4. Geometric readings, with their conditions visible

The circular relation implies a dimensionless relative-motion ratio $\omega_1r_1/c=\alpha$. In a cone representation using $\sin\theta=\omega r/c$, the corresponding angle is $\arcsin\alpha=0.4181112144\ldots$ degrees. This is a geometrical reading of the calibrated state, not a separate determination of the number.

The exact spherical-cap expression is $2\pi(1-\sqrt{1-\alpha^2})$. With measured $\alpha$, it gives $1.67296291145\ldots\times10^{-4}$ steradians. Its leading expression $\pi\alpha^2$ is an approximation, not an equality. Neither this cap nor the formal plane/sphere ratio by itself derives elementary spin, an atomic doublet, or a universal macro/micro identity.

The legacy $N=2/\alpha^2=37557.7301412\ldots$ is a ratio, not an integer winding count. Integer closure of individual records and a noninteger ratio of scales are different statements. 6. The origin question is open, not an operational roadblock

We have not derived the numerical value of $\alpha$ from the examined source commitments. We have also not proved it fundamentally contingent, impossible to derive, or a consequence of a theological choice. Those were stronger statements than the tested models justified.

The source audit and later hypothetical networks, spectral constructions and observer/controller accounts narrowed which conditions were insufficient. They did not produce a fully source-derived microscopic interaction law. Active parameter-free model-building is parked. An independently justified future insight could reopen the question; phenomenological work does not need to wait for it.

Local no-goThe closure $L=\mu vr=\hbar$ has the scale symmetry $v\mapsto\lambda v$, $r\mapsto r/\lambda$: it fixes $vr$, not $v/c$. Action quantization alone therefore cannot select $\alpha$; an independent law or measured calibration is required. This result is local to the tested circular account, not a proof that $\alpha$ is fundamentally underivable. See the full derivation and status boundary.

Two legitimate activities, different claims: a parameter-free derivation must not insert the desired value; a measured-input calculation deliberately supplies it and must say so. The latter is what the current Coulomb exhibit does. Calling a calibrated result a prediction of its own calibration would be circular; using that calibration to compute further relationships is useful work.
