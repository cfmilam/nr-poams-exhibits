The Alpha Problem

An open front in Normal Realism / POAMS
MEASURED INPUT · DERIVATION OPEN

This page replaces the retired exhibit Spectra, Gravitation, and Fine Structure in Normal Realism. The replacement was ratified after a Tier-A cold review on 7 September 2026. The failed program remains available as an auditable research record.

The short answer

POAMS does not presently derive the fine-structure ratio. The measured ratio fixes the scale of the small spectral corrections, but neither its numerical value nor the sign-sensitive mechanism that splits one level into distinct branches has been obtained from the ontology without importing the answer.

This is not a cosmetic regrade. The former exhibit made several claims that did not survive its own mathematics. It has therefore been retired from the active exhibit sequence. The current scientific position is simple: alpha is presently an imported measured input, and deriving it remains an open problem.

What failed, plainly

  1. A scalar time-dilation factor cannot split a line. It is even in the circulation rate: reversing the sense gives the same scalar shift. The old drawing inserted the two opposite branches by hand.
  2. $L=\hbar$ fixes a product, not a unique orbit. It constrains the rate–radius combination; it does not independently produce both the orbital scale and the rate ratio. The old calculation imported the measured ratio and then presented the result as geometry.
  3. The proposed winding count is not an integer. The inferred $N=2/\alpha^2$ is approximately $37{,}557.73$. Forcing the nearest whole count moves the answer far outside the measurement uncertainty. The whole-turn interpretation is falsified.
  4. The spin-half step was circular. The factor one-half emerged because the chosen spin radius already contained one-half.
  5. The collective bootstrap did not prove contingency. Tree-like connectivity was assumed, not forced, and the numerical verifier's normalization installed the marginal eigenvalue it later reported. The valid conclusion is narrower: the tested model class did not determine alpha.
  6. The proposed running law was never quantitatively confronted. It remains a research idea, not a conditional theorem.

Update 2026-09-12 — Mechanism failures closed; scale failures unified as one open

The 2026-09-07 retirement listed structural failures of the old derivation. Walking through them with the ratified 2026-09-12 Landé g-Factor from Twist/Writhe Coupling derivation and the Receiver Keys §7 two-endedness formalization in hand: the failures separate cleanly into a mechanism group (all three now addressed) and a scale group (all three remain, unified as one open).

Mechanism failures — all closed

  • Failure 1 — “A scalar time-dilation factor cannot split a line.” Addressed. Călugăreanu $Lk = \pm 1$ for the elementary closure supplies the sign-sensitive machinery. Two chiralities of twist relative to a directed writhe give the two branches; the branching is topological, not scalar. See Landé §4.
  • Failure 3 — “The proposed winding count is not an integer.” Dissolved. The old derivation identified $N = 2/\alpha^2 \approx 37{,}558$ with a POAMS winding count and required it be integer. In the current framework the integer discipline is carried by Călugăreanu $Lk = \pm 1$ (per elementary closure), not by any hypothetical count at $\sim 37{,}558$. If $\alpha^2$ admits any POAMS-native expression, that expression is a dimensionless ratio — not a winding count — and does not require integer $N$. The specific “$N$ must be integer” objection no longer binds.
  • Failure 4 — “The spin-half step was circular.” Addressed. The old exhibit derived spin-half by inserting a factor $1/2$ into a chosen spin radius; the answer was smuggled in. The Landé derivation has no such step: it uses Călugăreanu $Lk = \pm 1$ for the elementary closure, integer by construction. The extant-side “$S = 1/2$” corresponds to POAMS-native $Lk = \pm 1$ via the units-mapping between POAMS's fundamental unit ($\hbar$, one whole turn) and extant's fundamental unit ($\hbar/2$, the SU(2) double-cover step). The factor $1/2$ is a units-convention artifact, not an inserted physical factor.

Scale failures — all remain, unified as one open

  • Failure 2 — “$L=\hbar$ fixes a product, not a unique orbit.” Remains. The topological constraint from Călugăreanu produces no independent constraints on orbital rate and radius separately. Producing them requires deriving the elementary-closure coupling scale to the electromagnetic-frequency sector from POAMS foundations.
  • Failure 5 — “The collective bootstrap did not prove contingency.” Remains. The old network model's normalization installed its own answer. Any new bootstrap approach requires forcing the connectivity from POAMS ontology rather than assuming it.
  • Failure 6 — “The running law was never quantitatively confronted.” Remains. Extant measurements show the effective ratio depends on interaction scale. Deriving that scale-dependence from POAMS foundations is unattempted.

The three scale failures reduce to one underlying open. Deriving the numerical value of the coupling that atomic and precision measurements register — equivalently, whatever POAMS derivation would produce the measured fine-structure ratio — is a single gap. It appears as Failure 2 (rate-radius product), Failure 5 (bootstrap value), and Failure 6 (scale-dependent behavior) because it manifests differently under different attempted derivations, but each reduces to the same question: from what native POAMS geometry does the measured scale emerge?

The same open surfaces in other exhibits:

  • The QED-order magnetic-moment correction $g_s - 2 \approx 0.00232$ (measured to 12 significant figures) — Landé §7.
  • The measured hydrogen 21cm line at 1420.405751 MHz and adjacent hyperfine spectra — Landé §7.
  • The numerical value of the elementary-closure coupling coefficient extant labels $\mu_B$ — Stern-Gerlach §3.

Closing this exhibit closes them all simultaneously.

The separation the old exhibit did not make

The retired program conflated deriving the mechanism with deriving the numerical scale. When the scale-derivation failed, the whole program was retired. But the two questions are orthogonal and can succeed or fail independently. With them separated:

  • The mechanism side is now closed: sign-sensitive splitting via Călugăreanu two-chirality (F1), integer discipline properly located at $Lk = \pm 1$ rather than at any bookkeeping-derived $N$ (F3), and spin-half as units-convention artifact rather than smuggled factor (F4).
  • The scale side remains open, unified as one gap across the corpus (F2, F5, F6 plus the corresponding fronts in Landé and Stern-Gerlach).

The status pill above (MEASURED INPUT · DERIVATION OPEN) remains accurate for the numerical scale — which is what the retired program tried and failed to produce, and what remains the unified open front. The mechanism question, orthogonal to the scale question, is now closed.

What would count as a solution

A future derivation must earn the number before seeing it. It must begin with circulating angular momentum keeping its own books and produce a sign-sensitive splitting law plus its scale without inserting $\alpha$, $1/\alpha$, a measured orbital rate, or an adjustable normalization.

  1. Derive the operator or closure rule that distinguishes the two phase senses. (Update 2026-09-12: now available via Călugăreanu $Lk = \pm 1$ per Landé exhibit.)
  2. Compute its dimensionless scale from native closure geometry, with no target value in the derivation.
  3. Preregister held-out spectral predictions and run them against the record only after the calculation is frozen.
  4. Explain why the measured effective ratio changes with resolution, or state the domain in which the derived value applies.

Until those gates pass, the value remains an input. A rigorous null is preferable to another attractive formula that lands near 137.
