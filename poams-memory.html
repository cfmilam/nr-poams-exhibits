1 · What a bit is

The extant books define a bit operationally — any system with two stable states — and leave open what the states are states of. POAMS answers with the only substance it has: a bit is a persistent organization of turning, and the two values are the substance's own oldest two-valued variable — the two senses of closure, $\oint d\varphi = \pm 2\pi n$. This is the same two-valuedness the corpus has already dissolved "charge" into see Electricity §1 · Periodic Table: not a property riding on a thing, but the direction the turning goes. Every magnetic bit on Earth stores exactly this — the orientation of an aligned population of circulation senses — under the extant label "magnetization."

Why digital won — the discreteum's own verdict. The corpus holds that reality is a discreteum: angular momentum comes in whole closures or not at all — a fractional turn is not a smaller thing but no thing, because it does not close and cannot persist see Aufbau as Closure · the Restatement. Persistence therefore belongs to completed organizations and to nothing else. An analog store asks a continuum to hold still; a digital store asks a closure to remain a closure — which is what closures do. The industry discovered by attrition (analog drift, analog noise, analog decay) what the ontology states in one line: only the discrete persists.

3 · Writing is torque — the industry's write equation is AM conservation

How does one write an orientation? The extant answer for modern MRAM is spin-transfer torque: a spin-polarized current exerts a torque on the free layer's magnetization. Strip the costume and read what the equation says. Torque is $d\mathbf L/dt$ — the rate of angular-momentum delivery. Each carrier in the write current hands the layer up to $\hbar/2$ of the one substance; the layer flips when it has been handed enough:

$$\frac{dL}{dt} \;=\; \eta\,\frac{\hbar}{2}\,\frac{I}{e} \qquad\qquad t_{\text{switch}} \;\approx\; \frac{N_{\text{spins}}\,(\hbar/2)}{\eta\,(\hbar/2)(I/e)} \;=\; \frac{N_{\text{spins}}\,e}{\eta\, I}$$

Worked, on public device numbers measured specs · arithmetic ours: a 40 nm × 1.3 nm free layer at $M_s = 10^6$ A/m holds a moment of $1.6\times10^{-18}$ A·m² — about $\boldsymbol{1.8\times10^{5}}$ units of $\mu_B$, i.e. $1.8\times10^{5}$ half-turn senses to be out-voted. A 50 µA write current delivers carriers at $I/e = 3.1\times10^{14}$ per second; at a transfer efficiency $\eta \approx 0.6$ that is $1.9\times10^{14}$ units of $\hbar/2$ per second. Divide: $t_{\text{switch}} \approx 0.9$ ns — the nanosecond class the datasheets report. Pure angular-momentum bookkeeping, run on the industry's own numbers, lands on the industry's own switching time. The write head does not persuade the bit; it hands it angular momentum until its books flip. That the extant art calls the delivered quantity "spin current" and the stored quantity "magnetization" changes nothing: the conservation arithmetic closes because both are the one substance.

4 · The toll and the lease — what memory costs and how long it keeps

The toll (writing's floor). $$\frac{kT\ln 2}{h}\Big|_{300\text{ K}} \;=\; 4.33\ \text{THz} \qquad\left(\frac{kT}{h} = 6.25\ \text{THz}\right)$$

The lease (keeping's clock). How long does an orientation store hold? The industry's own retention law is Arrhenius: a written grain re-organizes spontaneously at $\tau = \tau_0\, e^{KV/kT}$, with the attempt time $\tau_0 \sim 1$ ns and the barrier $KV$ spec'd in multiples of $kT$ measured · recording-industry design rule:

$$\tau = \tau_0\, e^{KV/kT}: \qquad 40\,kT \;\Rightarrow\; \tau \approx 7\ \text{years} \qquad 60\,kT \;\Rightarrow\; \tau \approx 3.6\times10^{9}\ \text{years}$$

— every $0.69\,kT$ of barrier (one factor of $\ln 2$) doubles the memory's life. Twenty $kT$ of barrier separate a bit that survives a product warranty from one that would outlive the Sun. Memory, natively read, is rented from the heat book at an exponential discount: the closure keeps itself; thermal traffic occasionally forces a re-organization; the engineer buys nines of persistence with linear spend on barrier rate.
