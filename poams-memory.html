1 · What a bit is

The extant books define a bit operationally — any system with two stable states — and leave open what the states are states of. POAMS answers with the only substance it has: a bit is a persistent organization of turning, and the two values are the substance's own oldest two-valued variable — the two senses of closure, $\oint d\varphi = \pm 2\pi n$. This is the same two-valuedness the corpus has already dissolved "charge" into see Electricity §1 · Periodic Table: not a property riding on a thing, but the direction the turning goes. Every magnetic bit on Earth stores exactly this — the orientation of an aligned population of circulation senses — under the extant label "magnetization."

Why digital won — the discreteum's own verdict. The corpus holds that reality is a discreteum: angular momentum comes in whole closures or not at all — a fractional turn is not a smaller thing but no thing, because it does not close and cannot persist see Aufbau as Closure · the Restatement. Persistence therefore belongs to completed organizations and to nothing else. An analog store asks a continuum to hold still; a digital store asks a closure to remain a closure — which is what closures do. The industry discovered by attrition (analog drift, analog noise, analog decay) what the ontology states in one line: only the discrete persists.

3 · Writing is torque — the industry's write equation is AM conservation

How does one write an orientation? The extant answer for modern MRAM is spin-transfer torque: a spin-polarized current exerts a torque on the free layer's magnetization. Natively, torque is $d\mathbf L/dt$ — a rate of angular-momentum delivery. If $N_\mu=\mu/\mu_B$ counts the layer's aligned magnetic-moment units and each transferred carrier contributes at most $\hbar/2$ with net efficiency $\eta$, a literal sign reversal changes the layer from $+N_\mu\hbar/2$ to $-N_\mu\hbar/2$. The signed receipt is therefore:

$$\left|\Delta L_{\rm flip}\right|=2N_\mu\frac{\hbar}{2}=N_\mu\hbar,\qquad \left|\frac{dL}{dt}\right|=\eta\frac{\hbar}{2}\frac{I}{e},\qquad t_{\rm full\,receipt}=\frac{2N_\mu e}{\eta I}.$$

Worked ideal receipt on declared public-scale inputs illustrative geometry · arithmetic ours: take a 40 nm diameter circular free layer, 1.3 nm thick, at $M_s=10^6$ A/m. Then $V=\pi(20\,\mathrm{nm})^2(1.3\,\mathrm{nm})=1.63\times10^{-24}$ m³, $\mu=M_sV=1.63\times10^{-18}$ A·m², and $N_\mu=\mu/\mu_B=1.76\times10^5$. A 50 µA current supplies $I/e=3.12\times10^{14}$ carriers/s; at $\eta=0.6$, the ideal signed-delivery rate is $1.87\times10^{14}$ units of $\hbar/2$ per second. A full reversal requires $2N_\mu=3.52\times10^5$ such units, giving $t_{\rm full\,receipt}=1.88$ ns. This is an ideal conservation timescale, not a complete switching-dynamics prediction: anisotropy, damping, thermal activation, nonuniform reversal, and angular momentum returned to the reaction system remain device-dependent. The corrected receipt still lands in the nanosecond class without pretending to derive a particular datasheet value.

4 · The toll and the lease — what memory costs and how long it keeps

The toll (writing's floor). Landauer's bound — imported by the extant record and kept by this corpus see Periodic Table §Landauer · imported extant — prices the erasure of one bit at no less than $kT\ln 2$ of energy, re-filed into the incoherent book see Heat & Coherence. Read natively, at room temperature:

$$\frac{kT\ln 2}{h}\Big|_{300\text{ K}} \;=\; 4.33\ \text{THz} \qquad\left(\frac{kT}{h} = 6.25\ \text{THz}\right)$$

— logically irreversible erasure carries a lower bound equivalent to 4.33 THz of turn rate at 300 K. A state-preserving read need not pay that erasure toll; this page does not calculate or claim zero device energy for reading.

The lease (keeping's clock). How long does an orientation store hold? The industry's own retention law is Arrhenius: a written grain re-organizes spontaneously at $\tau = \tau_0\, e^{KV/kT}$, with the attempt time $\tau_0 \sim 1$ ns and the barrier $KV$ spec'd in multiples of $kT$ measured · recording-industry design rule:

$$\tau = \tau_0\, e^{KV/kT}: \qquad 40\,kT \;\Rightarrow\; \tau \approx 7\ \text{years} \qquad 60\,kT \;\Rightarrow\; \tau \approx 3.6\times10^{9}\ \text{years}$$

— within this single-barrier model, every additional $0.69\,kT$ (one factor of $\ln 2$) doubles the mean waiting time. Twenty $kT$ changes that idealized mean by $e^{20}\approx4.85\times10^8$. Real products have barrier distributions and additional failure modes, so these are model examples rather than warranty predictions. DRAM's 64 ms figure is an all-row refresh requirement, not an exact bit-loss instant; without refresh, leakage eventually makes its bias unreadable. Flash demonstrates the independent persistence axis: its off-balance census is nonvolatile because a barrier suppresses escape. The storage examples audited here therefore occupy different points on a barrier-and-refresh ledger, not one universal Arrhenius ladder.
