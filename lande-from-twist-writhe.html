§1 — The Extant Starting Point

CONDITIONAL INTERPRETATION

This page is a cross-ontology recoupling reference. It imports the extant SU(2) state law, the labels $L,S,J,m_J$, the magneton scale, and the leading coefficients $c_W=1$ and $c_T=2$. Current POAMS does not derive those inputs from $Tw$, $Wr$, or $Lk=Tw+Wr$.

In standard atomic physics, an atomic state's magnetic moment along the field axis reads

$$\mu_z = g_J \, m_J \, \mu_B$$

where $m_J$ is the projection quantum number of total angular momentum $J$, and $g_J$ is the Landé g-factor:

$$g_J = 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$

The formula is derived in QM by applying the projection theorem to the operator identity $\hat\mu = g_L \hat L + g_S \hat S$, with distinct orbital and intrinsic response coefficients:

  • Orbital: $g_L = 1$ — the leading orbital magnetic-response coefficient
  • Spin: $g_S \approx 2$ — the leading electron-spin coefficient, with higher-order corrections measured and calculated separately

For the present comparison, the labels $L,S,J,m_J$, the SU(2) projection theorem, the response coefficients $g_L=1$ and $g_S\simeq2$, and the magneton scale are supplied inputs. The algebraic recoupling identity is exact once they are supplied; their physical origin is a separate question.

Writhe motivates an externally organized channel and twist motivates an internally organized channel. That distinction is useful, but static framed-curve receipts do not determine temporal angular-momentum generators or magnetic normalization.

§4 — Conditional Landé Recoupling

With the empirical magneton scale and the two response coefficients supplied, the axis response is written in standard angular-momentum notation:

$$\frac{\mu_z}{\mu_B}=c_W\frac{\langle L_z\rangle}{\hbar}+c_T\frac{\langle S_z\rangle}{\hbar},\qquad c_W=1,\quad c_T=2.$$
IMPORTED STATE LAW The operators $\hat L$, $\hat S$, and $\hat J=\hat L+\hat S$; the quantum numbers $L,S,J,m_J$; their SU(2) commutators and Casimir eigenvalues; and the projection theorem are standard quantum-mechanical inputs. §4A — Imported Angular Machinery and the Model Boundary

For the supplied extant state space, SU(2) gives $\hat J^2\to J(J+1)\hbar^2$ and the corresponding results for $L$ and $S$. The projection theorem then gives

$$\frac{\langle L_z\rangle}{\hbar}=m_J\frac{J(J+1)+L(L+1)-S(S+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$
$$\frac{\langle S_z\rangle}{\hbar}=m_J\frac{J(J+1)+S(S+1)-L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}.$$

The twist/writhe dictionary interprets these as intrinsic and orbital bookkeeping channels. It does not turn $Tw$ and $Wr$ into the operators above, derive their allowed quantum numbers, or select the spinor doublet.

Combining the imported projection formulas with $c_W=1$ and $c_T=2$:

$$\frac{\mu_z}{\mu_B}=m_J\frac{(1)[J(J+1)+L(L+1)-S(S+1)]+(2)[J(J+1)+S(S+1)-L(L+1)]}{2J(J+1)}$$

Simplifying the numerator: $(1+2)J(J+1) + (1-2)(L(L+1) - S(S+1)) = 3J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)$.

$$g_J = \frac{3J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)} \;=\; 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$

This is the leading Landé formula under the stated inputs. The result is an exact recoupling identity inside the imported SU(2) state law. The POAMS contribution on this page is the channel interpretation, not an independent derivation of the state space or coefficients.
