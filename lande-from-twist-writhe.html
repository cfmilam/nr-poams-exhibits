§1 — What Extant Postulates

CROSS-ONTOLOGY

The paragraphs below use extant quantum-mechanical vocabulary only for comparative purposes. Nothing in the POAMS derivation that follows depends on any of these terms.

In standard atomic physics, an atomic state's magnetic moment along the field axis reads

$$\mu_z = g_J \, m_J \, \mu_B$$

where $m_J$ is the projection quantum number of total angular momentum $J$, and $g_J$ is the Landé g-factor:

$$g_J = 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$

The formula is derived in QM by applying the projection theorem to the operator identity $\hat\mu = g_L \hat L + g_S \hat S$, with two separately postulated $g$-factors:

  • Orbital: $g_L = 1$ — the “classical” magnetic moment of a circulating charge
  • Spin: $g_S \approx 2$ — introduced by Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit (1925) as the empirically-required factor to reproduce anomalous Zeeman splittings, later “explained” by the Dirac equation as an unavoidable consequence of relativistic electron structure

These two coefficients are postulates of the extant framework: $g_L = 1$ is asserted by analogy to classical current loops, and $g_S = 2$ is imported either as an empirical fit or as a Dirac-equation artifact. Landé's formula is then a corollary once the two coefficients are in place. POAMS derives both coefficients from foundations, and Landé's formula follows.

§3 — Deriving $g_L = 1$ (Writhe is Externally Referenced)

Writhe is the coiling of a centerline in space. Each element of writhe corresponds to a geometric fact about how the centerline is embedded in the surrounding substance — where does this piece of the coil sit relative to other pieces, relative to the axis, relative to other closures.

Every writhe element is externally referenced: it describes the relationship between the closure and the substance around it. The reference points of the coiling are outside the closure itself. There is no self-reference; no element of writhe involves the closure reading itself.

In ledger terms, an externally-referenced element is a normal single-ended geometric fact. It participates in exchanges with its surround, and each of those exchanges is counted once (no double-counting).

Writhe coupling coefficient: $c_W = 1$. No factor-of-two premium; writhe is single-ended geometric information about the closure's embedding.

This matches the extant $g_L = 1$ for orbital angular momentum — the “classical current loop” picture, in POAMS ontology, is exactly the writhe-of-a-closed-circulation picture: how the centerline coils in space, single-ended, no self-reference.

§4 — Deriving $g_S = 2$ (Twist is Self-Referential)

Twist is the closure spinning on itself. Unlike writhe, twist is defined internally to the closure: it describes how the ribbon's own material rotates around its own centerline. Both endpoints of any twist element are on the same closure.

By the POAMS two-endedness principle (see The Receiver Keys the Exchange and GROUNDING §3 self-corollary): every exchange is co-signed at both endpoints, and each endpoint carries the book. For an externally-referenced quantity (writhe), the two endpoints are at different loci and the standard sum-over-pairs convention correctly halves to avoid double-counting. For a self-referential quantity (twist), both endpoints are at the same locus — both books are kept at the same closure — and the halving convention does not apply. Twist coupling is genuinely two-ended at a single closure, and the geometric coupling coefficient picks up a factor of 2 accordingly.

§5 — Landé's Formula from Călugăreanu Projection

With the two coupling coefficients derived, the atomic-state magnetic moment along the field axis is:

$$\mu_z = c_W \cdot Wr_z + c_T \cdot Tw_z = 1 \cdot Wr_z + 2 \cdot Tw_z$$

For a state characterized by definite total linking $J$ (identifying $Lk_{total} \leftrightarrow J$ across the extant/POAMS conversion), the individual $Wr$ and $Tw$ components have average projections onto the total-$Lk$ direction. Călugăreanu geometry gives these projections directly from the identity $Wr \cdot Lk = (Lk^2 + Wr^2 - Tw^2)/2$ (and similarly for $Tw$):

$$\langle Wr_z \rangle = m_J \cdot \frac{J(J+1) + L(L+1) - S(S+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$
$$\langle Tw_z \rangle = m_J \cdot \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$

Combining with $c_W = 1$ and $c_T = 2$:

$$\mu_z = m_J \cdot \frac{(1)(J(J+1) + L(L+1) - S(S+1)) + (2)(J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1))}{2J(J+1)}$$

Simplifying the numerator: $(1+2)J(J+1) + (1-2)(L(L+1) - S(S+1)) = 3J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)$.

$$g_J^{POAMS} = \frac{3J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)} \;=\; 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$

This is exactly Landé's formula. POAMS reproduces it in closed form from twist/writhe geometry plus the two-endedness principle. No adjustable parameters, no empirical fits. What extant QM postulates ($g_L$, $g_S$) and then combines via the projection theorem, POAMS derives from foundations.
