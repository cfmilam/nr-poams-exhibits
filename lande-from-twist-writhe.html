§1 — What Extant Postulates

CROSS-ONTOLOGY

The paragraphs below use extant quantum-mechanical vocabulary only for comparative purposes. Nothing in the POAMS derivation that follows depends on any of these terms.

In standard atomic physics, an atomic state's magnetic moment along the field axis reads

$$\mu_z = g_J \, m_J \, \mu_B$$

where $m_J$ is the projection quantum number of total angular momentum $J$, and $g_J$ is the Landé g-factor:

$$g_J = 1 + \frac{J(J+1) + S(S+1) - L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$$

The formula is derived in QM by applying the projection theorem to the operator identity $\hat\mu = g_L \hat L + g_S \hat S$, with two separately postulated $g$-factors:

  • Orbital: $g_L = 1$ — the “classical” magnetic moment of a circulating charge
  • Spin: $g_S \approx 2$ — introduced by Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit (1925) as the empirically-required factor to reproduce anomalous Zeeman splittings, later “explained” by the Dirac equation as an unavoidable consequence of relativistic electron structure

For the present comparison the leading coefficients are stated inputs to the angular projection. A derivation of their physical normalization is distinct from the Landé recoupling identity. The current POAMS account no longer claims that merely duplicating endpoint records derives both coefficients.

§5A — Formalization of Premise (b): SU(2) structure at closures from POAMS one-substance

The Casimir substitution used in the projection formulas below does not follow from the plain integer identity $Lk = Tw + Wr$ alone — a literal scalar reading would give $X^2$, not $X(X{+}1)$, and would numerically diverge for cases like H ²P₁/₂ ($g=0$ instead of $2/3$). Given those two labels, the derivation below is exact.

Step 1: POAMS one-substance. Everything is quantized angular momentum in $\hbar$ units, organized in three-dimensional space with normal rotational covariance (GROUNDING §1: "quantized angular momentum in $\hbar$ units... intrinsic spin and orbital organization kept distinct"). Closures are quantized angular-momentum organizations.

Step 2: SU(2) by Wigner classification. Any quantum system whose states carry a unitary irreducible representation of the 3D rotation group is (up to the double cover for half-integer spin) an SU(2) representation. This is a mathematical fact — Wigner's classification of unitary irreducible representations of the rotation group, not a physics assumption. POAMS's one-substance closures satisfy the classification premise: they are quantized angular-momentum organizations with normal 3D rotational covariance. Therefore each closure carries an SU(2) representation labeled by a non-negative integer or half-integer $j$.

Step 3: Operator structure. The generators of the SU(2) representation at a closure are angular-momentum operators satisfying

$$[\hat J_i,\ \hat J_j] \;=\; i\hbar\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat J_k, \qquad i,j,k \in \{x,y,z\}.$$

The Casimir operator $\hat{\vec{J}}^{\,2} = \hat J_x^2 + \hat J_y^2 + \hat J_z^2$ commutes with all $\hat J_i$ and, on the $j$-representation, has eigenvalue $j(j+1)\hbar^2$. This is the standard textbook result of SU(2) representation theory; nothing here is a new physics assumption.

Step 4: Lk, Tw, Wr as SU(2) labels at closures. The topological invariants at a closure label its SU(2) content:

  • $Lk$ is the total angular-momentum quantum number at the closure — the $j$ label of the SU(2) representation, and its $z$-projection is the eigenvalue $m_J$ of $\hat J_z^{\text{total}}$.
  • $Tw$ labels the self-referential (intrinsic) sub-representation of the total AM at the closure — the intrinsic ("spin") quantum number $s$.
  • $Wr$ labels the externally-referenced (orbital) sub-representation — the orbital quantum number $l$.
  • Vector decomposition at the operator level: $\hat{\vec{J}}^{\,\text{total}} = \hat{\vec{J}}^{\,\text{self}} + \hat{\vec{J}}^{\,\text{ext}}$, equivalently $\hat{\vec{Lk}} = \hat{\vec{Tw}} + \hat{\vec{Wr}}$ as an operator equation.
  • The Călugăreanu scalar identity $Lk = Tw + Wr$ is the $z$-projection statement $m_J^{\text{total}} = m_s + m_l$: it holds for the eigenvalues of the $z$-components on eigenstates. The identity is compatible with (indeed a consequence of) the operator equation above; it does not by itself commit to any operator algebra, but the operator algebra it is compatible with is fixed by Steps 1–3.

Step 5: Casimir eigenvalues (the "$+1$" structure). In an eigenstate of the total SU(2) representation labeled by $j = J$, the squared vector operator has eigenvalue

$$\langle \hat{\vec{Lk}}^{\,2} \rangle \;=\; J(J{+}1)\hbar^2,$$

and similarly $\langle \hat{\vec{Wr}}^{\,2} \rangle = L(L{+}1)\hbar^2$, $\langle \hat{\vec{Tw}}^{\,2} \rangle = S(S{+}1)\hbar^2$. The extra "$+1$" comes from the non-abelian structure of SU(2): $\hat J_x$ and $\hat J_y$ do not commute, so their squared expectation values contribute $\hbar^2 J/2$ each even when the state has maximal $\hat J_z$ eigenvalue. The scalar-square reading ($X^2$ rather than $X(X{+}1)$) is what one would get if the algebra were abelian; it is not.

Step 6: The projection theorem. For any vector operator $\hat{\vec{V}}$ acting within a $J$-eigenspace, the standard SU(2) projection theorem (Wigner–Eckart, vector case) gives

$$\langle J,m_J | \hat V_z | J,m_J \rangle \;=\; m_J \cdot \frac{\langle J |\, \hat{\vec{V}} \cdot \hat{\vec{J}}\, | J \rangle}{J(J+1)\hbar^2}.$$

Applied to $\hat{\vec{V}} = \hat{\vec{Wr}}$ with $\hat{\vec{Wr}} \cdot \hat{\vec{J}} = (\hat{\vec{J}}^{\,2} + \hat{\vec{Wr}}^{\,2} - \hat{\vec{Tw}}^{\,2})/2$ (from squaring $\hat{\vec{Tw}} = \hat{\vec{J}} - \hat{\vec{Wr}}$), and using the Casimir eigenvalues from Step 5:

$$\langle \hat{Wr}_z \rangle \;=\; m_J \cdot \frac{J(J+1) + L(L+1) - S(S+1)}{2\,J(J+1)},$$

and analogously $\langle \hat{Tw}_z \rangle = m_J \cdot [J(J{+}1) + S(S{+}1) - L(L{+}1)] / [2J(J{+}1)]$. These are the projection formulas used in the derivation immediately following, now grounded in POAMS one-substance rather than assumed.
