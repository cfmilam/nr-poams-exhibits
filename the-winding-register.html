1 · The premise the hardware never had

Normal Realism's ground floor is discrete: angular momentum comes only in whole turns, $\oint d\varphi = 2\pi n$, and a fractional turn is not a smaller thing but no thing — it does not close and cannot persist. The integers are primary; continuity is the far-field appearance of many turns read at once. Reality's own durable bookkeeping is integer-typed.

Now look at what our machines store. A DRAM cell holds an integer as a leaking census of charge, refreshed every few milliseconds extant record: JEDEC refresh, ~64 ms. A flash cell walls the census behind an oxide barrier. A disk grain holds one bit as an orientation on an Arrhenius barrier. In every case the integer is a representation imposed on an analog quantity by thresholds — the machine's state variable and the number it stores are different kinds of thing. The winding register closes that gap: it stores an integer as an integer — the count of completed closures of a physical phase. The durable state has the same type as reality's bookkeeping.

2 · The register

An accumulator is a resonant or delay structure whose phase is tracked modulo $2\pi$ together with a whole-turn counter: its persistent state is $n$, the winding number. Durability comes from the quantization itself — a half-turn cannot persist, so there is nothing between $n$ and $n{+}1$ for the state to decay into. Sub-turn drift is not protected against; it is discarded, at each scheduled commit, when the accumulated phase is resolved to the nearest whole turn. The parent exhibit derived the discipline and its design relation — the commit period that holds worst-case drift under the half-turn bound, the per-commit miscount, and the constant-overhead majority that replaces the extant art's growing error-correction tax (Computation as Completion, the commit theorem). The register is that discipline given a number to hold.

3 · Arithmetic that is native, not represented

Addition. Register values are expressed as phases, combined in an open interference stage, and the total is committed to the destination register. Subtraction is the same act in the opposite sense.

The modulus, free. Choose the register's per-turn quantum so the modulus corresponds to a whole number of turns, and the register wraps at the modulus by construction. There is no modulo circuit; residue arithmetic is what the hardware does when left alone. Modular exponentiation — the workhorse of period-finding — runs in the representation where reduction is the geometry.

The carry, physical. When an accumulator crosses a whole-turn boundary at commit, it emits one quantized pulse to the next register, which posts one turn. The carry is not a signal about arithmetic that a comparator deduces; it is a discrete physical event that is the arithmetic — countable, clockable, and either present or absent, never partial.

The radix, geometric. Two registers of differing per-turn quanta, coupled through an interference stage and committed, convert $N$ turns at base $a$ into the corresponding count at base $b$: base conversion as a native hardware operation — residue representation in, decimal readout out.

4 · Why interference alone was never enough

The record already tried computing with phase and no integers. Gauss-sum interference factoring — demonstrated in NMR, cold atoms, and shaped pulses — died by its own community's analysis: suppressing false (“ghost”) factor resonances demands a number of interfering terms that grows as a power of the target integer, exponential in the digit count extant record: the ghost-factor analyses. The failure mode is exactly sub-turn analog precision asked to survive an entire computation. The winding architecture inverts the burden: precision demands are confined to bounded-depth stages between commits, and everything persistent is an integer. And the scope is stated plainly, as the parent exhibit settled it: no Shor-class asymptotic speedup lives here — the merits are native modular arithmetic, commit-disciplined error, and the energy ledger in which the turns are free and the postings are tolled.

5 · The cousins, each holding a shard

Goto's parametron extant record: 1959 computed with two phase states $0/\pi$ — a winding register truncated to one bit, with no multi-turn count and no carry. Single-flux-quantum logic extant record: RSFQ, Likharev & Semenov 1991 already moves quantized whole-flux events as its working pulses — the art's own proof that computing with discrete closure events is fast and manufacturable — but spends its quanta as transients rather than accumulating them in registers with modular structure. Magnonic interferometer and majority logic extant record: Khitun et al. computes in phase without durable integers at all. Each cousin owns a shard: bistable phase, quantized events, wave interference. The register is what none of them built — the accumulated, multi-valued, modular integer as the machine's durable state.

6 · The bench that would kill it

Four gates, one register. (i) Retention: write $n$ counted pulses, hold, read $n$ — across hold times and power cycles of the drive. (ii) Carry integrity: the carry pulse fires at the whole-turn boundary and nowhere else — event statistics clean against drive noise. (iii) Modular exactness: the wrap at modulus $N$ is exact over arbitrarily many cycles — one lost or gained turn is a fail. (iv) Radix identity: conversion $a\!\rightarrow\!b$ matches integer arithmetic on every tested value. Room-temperature, garnet-class media, bench instruments throughout (substrate section). A machine that cannot pass gate (i) has no register; a reading that fails gate (iii) has no modulus. Failures are reported as failures.
