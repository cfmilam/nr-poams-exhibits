The Winding Register

Computation as Completion II · arithmetic where the integers are physical · the accumulator, the carry, the modulus, and the radix — in winding space

1 · The state must be named honestly

A phase observable is periodic: $e^{i(\phi+2\pi n)}=e^{i\phi}$. Its present angle therefore cannot reveal how many earlier turns occurred. A usable winding register must contain two different things: an open phase, measured modulo $2\pi$ for interference, and an explicit integer store that survives the commit. The integer may live in (A) an electronic up/down wrap counter, (B) a bank of finite residue channels with conventional recombination, or (C) a genuinely metastable topological sector of a specified medium. Species C is a research target, not an achieved device.

The loop condition $n=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint\nabla\phi\cdot d\ell$ can label a spatial winding sector when an order parameter, closed path, boundary condition, and nonzero amplitude are physically present. It does not make every temporal oscillator a memory. Quantization labels the allowed states; an energy barrier, noise environment, write path, and readout determine retention. That distinction is the register's starting point.

2 · The register and commit

In the buildable species-A embodiment, a resonator or delay structure carries the open phase, a reference oscillator supplies the comparison, a phase comparator and wrap detector observe signed crossings, and an up/down counter stores the integer. At commit, the controller combines the counted wraps with the rounded residual phase. If $x=n+\epsilon$ turns is the estimate, nearest-integer decoding is valid only inside the guard band $|\epsilon|<\tfrac12-\delta$. Boundary cases are re-sampled or flagged.

For zero-mean Gaussian phase error with standard deviation $\sigma$ turns, a raw nearest-integer decoder with no rejection band has finite single-commit miscount probability $p_{raw}=2Q(1/(2\sigma))$. A declared rejection half-width $\delta$ changes both the accepted-error probability and the re-sampling latency, so those quantities must be reported separately. Under independent raw commit errors, $P_{fail}=1-(1-p_{raw})^M\approx Mp_{raw}$ when $Mp_{raw}\ll1$; correlated drift requires its own model. Redundancy can reduce the effective error but does not make arbitrarily long computation free of accumulated risk. The commit schedule, redundancy, checkpointing, and confidence target must therefore be engineered for the declared operation count.

3 · Arithmetic the register must implement

Addition and subtraction. Open phases may combine in an interference stage, but their integer parts must be read from their stores and posted to the destination store. Interference returns a residue, not an unbounded sum. Subtraction additionally requires signed storage and a defined underflow rule.

Modulus is engineered. Integer winding gives $\mathbb Z$, not automatically $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$. A modulus-$N$ register needs a finite-state decoder or physical transition implementing $N-1\rightarrow0$, with the reset destination, energy, and error rate specified. In modular mode overflow is discarded; in positional mode it becomes carry.

Carry and borrow occur at the radix boundary. A carry is emitted only when the low register crosses $N-1\rightarrow0$, not at every whole-turn crossing; borrow is the signed reverse transition. Hysteresis and direction sensing suppress boundary chatter, duplicates, and missed pulses.

Radix conversion is arithmetic. Integer-ratio rescaling can use multiply/divide with a remainder register. General conversion requires quotient/remainder or a residue/CRT network. Differing phase scales alone do not convert digit representations.

4 · Why interference alone was never enough

Gauss-sum interference factoring demonstrated that elegant phase arithmetic does not by itself solve scaling: suppressing false (“ghost”) resonances requires resources that grow as a power of the target integer extant record: ghost-factor analyses. The proposed architecture confines analog phase to bounded stages and commits results into explicit integer stores. That may improve engineering locality, but it does not establish a complexity advantage. No Shor-class speedup is claimed. Nor are turns energetically free: drive, reference, sensing, commit, reset, carry transport, refresh, and correction all belong in the measured energy ledger.

5 · The cousins, each holding a shard

Parametron logic, single-flux-quantum logic, superconducting flux memories, ring counters, and magnonic interferometers each supply relevant pieces: phase-bistable logic, quantized transition events, stored flux states, event counting, or wave interference. The proposed combination is narrower: bounded open-phase processing plus scheduled commit into an explicit multi-valued integer store, with separately engineered modulus and carry control. Novelty and performance relative to those families require a dedicated prior-art search and fair benchmarks; this exhibit does not claim that no earlier machine accumulated quantized events.

6 · The bench that would kill it

Five finite gates, one proposed register. (0) State: demonstrate at least three independently distinguishable stored values with nondestructive readout, not inferred only from supplied write pulses. (i) Retention: specify the state carrier, write waveform, complete-power-cycle condition, hold-time/temperature matrix, trials, transition rate, and confidence interval. (ii) Carry: test signed $N-1\leftrightarrow0$ crossings near the boundary and report missed, duplicate, false, and mistimed events. (iii) Modulus: provide the physical transition graph and test a declared finite count of wraps and non-wrap increments. (iv) Radix: define input/output digit encodings, range, sign, overflow, quotient/remainder path, and compare every tested output with conventional arithmetic. A device that fails gate 0 has no physical register; one that fails gate iii has no implemented modulus.
