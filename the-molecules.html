1 · The Closure-Deficit Ledger

The outer census of atom \(i\) has capacity \(\kappa_i\), present occupancy \(o_i\), and deficit

\[d_i=\kappa_i-o_i.\]

For the first-row atoms used here: hydrogen has \(d_H=2-1=1\), carbon has \(d_C=8-4=4\), and oxygen has \(d_O=8-6=2\). Let \(b_{ij}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}\) count the independent closure channels shared by endpoints \(i\) and \(j\). A closed molecular graph obeys

\[\sum_j b_{ij}=d_i\quad\text{at every atom.}\]

This is not an electron-pair ontology in new clothes. The edge is one relation entered at both ends; no third object sits between the atoms. The integer equation derives the admissible bond-order ledger once the atomic census and connectivity are specified. It does not price which admissible graph is preferred.

The carbon result. Four is not guessed from methane. It is the unfilled half of carbon’s \(2s+2p\) outer capacity: \(8-4=4\). The old lesser-of-filled-and-unfilled p-only count gave two and could not derive tetravalence. The complete outer-census deficit supplies the correct integer, while the geometry below determines how four equivalent axes can coexist.

2 · Why Carbon Closes as 180°, 120°, or 109.471°

Let \(K\) equivalent axial coupling directions be unit vectors \(\mathbf u_a\). A completed local census has no leftover preferred direction, so \(\sum_a\mathbf u_a=0\). Equivalence makes every off-diagonal inner product the same number \(c\). Squaring the closure gives

\[0=\left|\sum_{a=1}^{K}\mathbf u_a\right|^2=K+K(K-1)c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c=-\frac1{K-1}.\]

Therefore the ideal angle is

\[\theta_K=\cos^{-1}\!\left(-\frac1{K-1}\right),\qquad 2\le K\le4.\]

The corresponding Gram matrix has \(G_{aa}=1\) and \(G_{ab}=-1/(K-1)\). It has one zero eigenvalue—the vector-sum closure—and rank \(K-1\), so the \(K=4\) solution fits exactly in three dimensions. No repulsive little objects are needed to manufacture the angle.

Boundary. These are ideal equal-handle angles. Different endpoint identities and nonbonding domains perturb them. Ethene’s measured \(117.6°/121.2°\) split is a comparison with the ideal \(120°\), not something this unweighted theorem calculates.

4 · Single, Double, Triple, and Ring Closure

Independent channels, not thicker sticks

A single bond uses one axial relation. A double bond adds one independent transverse winding relation; a triple adds the second linearly independent transverse direction around the same axis. The bond order \(b_{ij}\) is therefore a channel count. The visualization separates them as gold, cyan, and violet braids.

Twist/writhe guardrail. The atoms’ intrinsic outer phase senses belong to the twist account; the arrangement of a coupled circulation about multiple atomic barycenters belongs to its molecular writhe. \(Lk=Tw+Wr\) conserves the total but does not price the partition during bond formation. “Axial” and “transverse” here count independent relational channels; they do not yet assert that each channel carries a separately measured whole unit of either atomic twist or molecular writhe.

\[\sum_{e\in C}\Delta\phi_e=2\pi m,\qquad m\in\mathbb Z.\]

For six equivalent edges, \(\Delta\phi=m\pi/3\). The carbon ledger also has two alternating integer registries; the physical sixfold relation need not freeze either registry onto permanent alternating sticks. This derives the permitted phase family and the regular ring geometry. It does not derive the energetic aromatic discount or select its magnitude.
