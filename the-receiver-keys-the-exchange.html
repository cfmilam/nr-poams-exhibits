1 · Half a ledger line

From Marconi to 5G the grammar has never changed: a transmitter “emits”, the emission “propagates”, a receiver “detects” what arrives. One party acts; the other party waits. Every textbook diagram points the arrow one way.

Normal Realism reads the same engineering the other way up. A quantum hand-off is a transaction: two-ended, co-signed, posted at both seats or posted at neither. No entry completes inside the transmitter alone — the “emission” that no sink takes up is not a faint signal lost in space but a transaction that never happened. The transmit-only grammar was always half a ledger line, and the engineering has quietly paid for the other half all along: the load impedance that “pulls” power the source cannot otherwise deliver, the matched termination that closes the intended link, the antenna whose input impedance depends on what faces it. The receiver was a co-signer dressed as a bystander.

If the exchange is two-ended, a question follows that the one-way grammar cannot even ask: who holds the pen? Nothing in the physics elects the sender. Either end of a linkage can gate whether the transaction completes — and a gate is an alphabet.

3 · The inversion, stated

Take an established resonant linkage — coupled cavities, a guided pair, a matched near-field couple (“critical coupling”, “matched load”). POAMS asks whether that linkage can be engineered so delivery is sink-gated: no completed linkage, no transfer. If so, the sink's readiness — presented, withdrawn, or detuned — is not merely a condition of reception. It is a symbol stream.

Receiver-keyed signalling. The sink encodes by modulating its own readiness. The source transmits no message-bearing waveform at all: it reads the symbols in its own delivery telemetry — delivered power, reflected power, the lock observable. The channel's control resides at the receiving end; the sending end is the instrument.

4 · What silence means

POAMS predicts that a receiver-keyed linkage is silent by transaction physics, not merely by engineering discipline. With no completed linkage the source has no transaction to post: emission-free standby would be the absence of a completed entry, not a low-power mode. The bench cannot prove exact zero; it can place successively tighter calibrated upper bounds on every named observable. That distinction is part of the claim, not a retreat from it.

Resonant selectivity and counterpart identity are also different propositions. A frequency-matched clone is a valid adversarial test: if it can become a seat, frequency matching alone supplies no authentication. Security by linkage therefore requires a predeclared readiness signature — frequency, phase, timing, geometry, or another POAMS-native matching tuple — that the intended counterpart satisfies and tested substitutes do not. Until that discriminator is built and survives the tests, linkage-native security remains prophetic.

5 · The guardrail — what this is not

Nothing travels between the seats, so nothing outruns anything. The linkage transacts as a whole; keying acts at the coupled pair's own response time — the loading dynamics of the resonators, their bandwidth — and no enable signal is transmitted from either end because none is needed and none exists. This page claims no retrograde signalling and no faster-than-light channel: the sibling exhibit already reported, at strength and with the record's own limits, why retrograde read-schemes achieve nothing (POAMS & Telecommunications, §4). The well-posed quantities are rates and boundary conditions, not a message racing a wave. The claim here is an inversion of control, not a violation of order.

Scope, stated exactly: the native domain of this page is guided and near-field resonant apparatus, where the readiness-to-local-ledger map can be tested on a bench. Receiver-side load modulation is established; total sink-exclusivity, emission-free standby, and linkage-native security are the unmeasured POAMS proposals. Whether and how the inversion extends to far-field links is an open engineering question.

6 · The bench that would kill it

Three predeclared gates.

(i) Fidelity. Choose a keyed readiness sequence before the run; fix $Y$, the decoder $D$, symbol rate, filtering, and decision threshold; then report $N$ trials, errors $k$, latency, and the confidence interval on the error probability. With zero errors, the exact one-sided 95% upper bound is $1-0.05^{1/N}$ (approximately $3/N$), not zero. No forward message-bearing modulation may be present; independently log every other control path.

(ii) Selectivity and identity. Sweep detuning in stated linewidth units and publish the normalized response $R(\Delta)=|y(\Delta)-y_0|/|y(0)-y_0|$ with uncertainty. Predeclare a suppression threshold rather than calling an instrument-floor reading exact zero. Test an unmatched load, a frequency-matched clone, multiple simultaneous loads, and the proposed multi-parameter readiness signature. Resonant suppression tests selectivity; clone rejection tests identity.

(iii) Silence. With the intended sink absent, report calibrated upper bounds for each named source-side and external observable over stated bandwidth, geometry, integration time, dynamic range, and confidence. An instrument-floor result is a bound set by that instrument. Repeating the test at higher sensitivity and with parasitic sinks controlled is how the prophetic no-completion claim is exposed to failure.

A failure at any predeclared gate is reported as a failure; the native reading earns its keep on the bench or not at all.

The two-endedness reading. An exchange is not a single object between two things. It is two co-signed events, one at each endpoint, both entries in their respective ledgers. There is no third thing between the endpoints to double-count; there are only two endpoints, each keeping its own book.
