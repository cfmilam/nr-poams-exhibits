An Interactive Exploration of Time Dilation via Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis

🚀 Welcome to POAMS

Normal Realism supplies the philosophical setting; the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS) supplies the time ledger. Adopt Pope's orthogonal apportionment of observer time into a body's own count and relative distance-time. The clock ratio then follows by Pythagoras—without requiring a light-propagation mechanism in the calculation.

Scope: this exhibit computes an idealized two-leg journey in one declared Earth reference ledger. The Pythagorean apportionment is a POAMS premise, not a consequence of geometry without physical assignments. The controls illustrate its conditional arithmetic; they are not an experiment or a model of finite acceleration, Earth rotation, or orbital clock corrections.

Key POAMS Insights:

  • No Light Mechanism Required: The adopted time relation is geometric, not an optical transit model.
  • $c$ is a Conversion Factor: POAMS uses it to convert relative distance into time units.
  • Segment Geometry: $(\Delta t)^2=(\Delta\tau)^2+(\Delta s/c)^2$ applies to each constant-rate leg.
  • Natural Orbital Motion: In POAMS, the straight coasting legs are the unbounded-radius idealization of orbital motion; this triangle does not derive that ontology.
  • Reunited Counts: Add the two leg books. Reversing direction permits reunion; the rate difference already accumulates during coasting.

The kink is a discontinuity in direction, not position or accumulated age. A finite-duration turn would require its own sequence of rates and elapsed-time entries. The highlight button supplies no acceleration calculation. At $v=0$ both twins stay together and there is no turn.

🧮 The Segment-to-Journey Calculation

For a leg beginning with both increments set to zero, adopt the POAMS triangle and set $\Delta s=v\Delta t$ with constant $v$:

$$(\Delta t)^2=(\Delta\tau)^2+(\Delta s/c)^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\Delta\tau}{\Delta t}=\sqrt{1-\beta^2},\qquad \beta=\frac{v}{c},\quad |\beta|<1.$$

$\Delta t$ and $\Delta\tau$ are elapsed time intervals; $\Delta s$ is signed displacement on that leg; $c$ has units distance/time. Thus every term in the squared relation has units time². On a nonzero constant-rate segment $\gamma=\Delta t/\Delta\tau=1/\sqrt{1-\beta^2}$.

$$\tau_{\rm trip}=\sum_i\Delta t_i\sqrt{1-\beta_i^2},\qquad x_{\rm final}-x_{\rm initial}=c\sum_i\beta_i\Delta t_i.$$

For the chosen legs $(+\beta,5\,\mathrm{y})$ and $(-\beta,5\,\mathrm{y})$, displacement cancels but the proper-time increments add:

$$x_{\rm final}=x_{\rm initial},\qquad \tau_{\rm traveler}=10\,\mathrm{y}\sqrt{1-\beta^2},\qquad \tau_{\rm Earth}=10\,\mathrm{y}.$$

Do not put net round-trip displacement into one triangle. At reunion it is zero, but the traveler has accumulated two nonzero motion entries. Nor does a global triangle with total path length generally work for changing speed: 5 years at rest followed by 5 years at $0.8c$ gives $\tau=5+3=8$ years; a single triangle using $s/c=4$ years would instead give $\sqrt{84}=9.165\ldots$ years.

If sufficiently fine segments admit a smooth limit, the extension is $d\tau=dt\sqrt{1-\beta(t)^2}$ and $\tau=\int\sqrt{1-\beta(t)^2}\,dt$. This is a local accumulation rule, not differentiation of one global endpoint triangle for arbitrary motion.
