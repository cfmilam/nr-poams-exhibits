1 · The decade, honestly

Two computing programs ran through the same ten years. One of them refused irreversibility — it froze its state, shielded it, refrigerated it, and spent nearly all of its hardware defending an open phase against the world. The other one committed, billions of times per second per chip: every multiply-accumulate a thermodynamically irreversible, completed operation, cheap and final. The second program trained the large language models and took the decade. The first is still explaining why the breakthrough is close.

The record, stated at strength on both sides. The gate-model program has produced genuine laboratory triumphs — below-threshold error-corrected logical elements extant record: Google Willow, Dec 2024, a trapped-ion machine reaching roughly 2 physical elements per logical element in its regime extant record: Quantinuum Helios announcement, Nov 2025, and a factoring-resource estimate that fell from ~20 million to under 1 million physical elements in a decade of theory extant record: Gidney 2019→2025. The engineering is heroic and improving. And yet: superconducting platforms still run at 10–15 mK under multi-stage shielding; surface-code fault tolerance still budgets on the order of 10³–10⁴ physical elements per protected logical element across the useful range extant record; the advantage demonstrations have been repeatedly matched or spoofed by classical algorithms extant record: random-circuit & annealing rebuttals; and commercial applications at scale remain, honestly counted, at zero.

Meanwhile classical training compute scaled by roughly four orders of magnitude and simply took every workload that was supposed to wait for the quantum machines extant estimate: epoch-scaling analyses. The native reading of this asymmetry is the whole exhibit: the winning program computed with completions; the losing program pays everything it has to forbid them.

2 · The ontological audit

Take the four load-bearing entity-labels one at a time and ask what the bench actually has.

“Superposition” → one turning's phase. A “qubit in superposition” is, on the bench, a single coherent oscillatory excitation with a definite, continuous, single-valued phase — one thing turning, read in a chosen basis. The Bloch sphere is the projection geometry of one circulation's phase; nothing is “in two states at once.” What the extant grammar calls preparing a superposition is setting a phase; what it calls single-qubit gates are phase rotations. The bench never leaves the phase domain.

“Entanglement” → co-signing. A “maximally entangled pair” is one shared quantity of angular momentum booked across two seats — a co-signed exchange, in the corpus's standing grammar The Polyverse; POAMS & Memory. The closure is definite in magnitude — one whole ℏ, durable by whole-quantum bookkeeping — while its axis is not pre-set: the axis posts at readout, against whichever analyzer the seat chooses. That refinement is load-bearing — a pre-set axis read locally is precisely the model the Bell record falsifies (the derivation closes this section). The extant program’s entangled superpositions are open bookings that any stray completion re-writes; the co-signature is a completed one. The resource is real; the fragility is the grammar's, not the resource's.

“Measurement/collapse” → completion. Readout is a whole-quantum closure: an exchange completes, posts, and is final. The extant program treats this as a catastrophe to postpone; the mystery (“why does observation destroy the computation?”) is manufactured by the ontology. Natively there is nothing to explain — completion is the ledger's one primitive act, and a processor should use it.

“Decoherence” → untracked completions. The environment does not “collapse the wavefunction”; it keeps completing exchanges with the machine — every stray closure posts to the books and re-writes the phase story. Decoherence time is simply the mean time to the first untracked completion. Isolation engineering is an attempt to forbid the substance its one native act.

The dilution refrigerators, the shielding stack, the 10³–10⁴:1 correction overhead — that is not incidental engineering friction. The “Hilbert space” census

The exponential promise — “n qubits explore 2ⁿ states at once” — counts the possible joint bookings of n phases and their co-signatures. That is a census of describable outcomes, not an inventory of physical resources; the container grammar mistakes its own bookkeeping for hardware. What is physically present is n phases and their co-signing relations — a rich, real resource, linear in n, plus the combinatorics of how closures can land. Algorithms that win do so by interference arithmetic on phases — period-finding is winding arithmetic; amplitude amplification is phase geometry — never by touching 2ⁿ of anything.

Why small machines work anyway (honesty)

None of this says the laboratory record is fake. Interference algorithms are real phase arithmetic, and in the regime where the clamp approximately holds — small n, short depth, heroic isolation — the machines are phase engines, and they compute. The native claim is narrower and sharper: the resource was never the exponential state space; the ontology misdirects the hardware budget toward protecting a fiction; and the one application class that is native for free is completion-grammar emulation — what the extant program calls quantum simulation, which is its one commercially honest near-term lane extant record.

4 · The constructive doctrine: compute with the grammar

Stop fighting completion. Schedule it.

evolve → close → post → repeat

A phase-completion processor alternates two kinds of stage. In an open-phase stage, continuous phase evolves and combines by interference — the analog compute fluid, fast and cheap and perishable. In a commit stage, accumulated phase is deliberately, irreversibly closed: read out, resolved to the nearest whole turn, and posted to a winding register. The commit is the machine's clock tick, its readout, and its error philosophy in one primitive.

Closure-as-commit versus error correction

The extant program protects everything, always: thousands of physical elements per logical element, standing watch against the first stray completion. The native program protects nothing and digitizes instead: sub-turn drift accumulated during an open stage is discarded at each commit, because the posted quantity is the whole-turn count, not the continuous phase. Hold the inter-commit drift under half a turn and the posted integer is exact — error is quantized at each commit rather than accumulated across the computation. Where the extant machine pays a standing army, the native machine pays a metronome.

Winding registers: durable state that cannot drift a little

A winding register cannot be slightly wrong. Its state is an integer; the only possible error is a whole-turn miscount, which is discrete, detectable, and — under scheduled commits — rare by construction. Modular arithmetic is not computed on such a register; it is the register's geometry: turn count wraps at the modulus natively. Carry is a physical event — a deliberate whole-turn transition handed to the next register. Fig. 3 lets you operate one.

7 · The crux, stated falsifiably

The precision wall

Analog phase machines die, historically, on precision: continuous quantities must be resolved ever more finely as problems scale, and noise does not cooperate. Any proposal that keeps its arithmetic in continuous phase inherits the wall.

The ghost-factor wall (the named enemy)

The one serious extant attempt at interference factoring — Gauss-sum factoring, demonstrated in NMR, cold atoms, and shaped-pulse optics extant record, 2007–2012 — was killed by its own community's analysis: suppressing false (“ghost”) factor resonances requires the number of interfering terms to grow as a power of the target integer — exponential in the digit count extant record: Štefańák et al. 2007. Elegant interference, no scaling. That analysis is the standard this page's bet must beat, and we name it rather than hide it.

The bet, settled: winding arithmetic buys arithmetic — not asymptotics

The native architecture’s answer to both walls is structural — keep persistent arithmetic in winding space, spend continuous phase only inside bounded stages — and the question this page first published as OPEN has now been derived, the same night, against our own interest. The trichotomy: where could the exponential hide? (A) in sub-turn analog resolution — dead by the ghost-factor record. (C) in super-classical harvesting of the co-signed resource at bounded depth — grammatically forbidden: extracting the period requires phase relations that survive from stage to stage, and a commit is by definition the closure of open phase. You cannot post the turn and keep it open. Relax that — grow the depth, drop the commits — and you have not improved this machine; you have exited it, rebuilt the deep-coherent extant machine, and owe its clamp tax in full. What remains is (B): the winding period-finder is a classical-complexity-class analog co-processor. Stated plainly: no Shor-class asymptotic speedup lives in this architecture, and the derivation that says so is ours. What survives of the bet, honestly: modular arithmetic native in hardware, the commit error discipline, and whatever constant-factor throughput and energy-per-operation edge the substrate delivers — that quantification is the open remainder, and if it fails to materialize, this rung dies as a distinguished application. Rungs 1–3 and the correlation source stand unaffected either way.
