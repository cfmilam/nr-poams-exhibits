Every new exhibit must begin with:
1. the observed phenomenon and observer;
2. the authenticated source position;
3. the Normal Realist constraints;
4. the physical and mathematical premises being added;
5. competing approaches and why one is chosen;
6. the exact record that could defeat it;
7. independent review before construction.

That changes the workflow from invent → calculate → retrofit meaning to ground → choose → derive → test.

Three safeguards govern this method:
- The canon cannot judge the sources during reconciliation. Contrary passages must correct the canon, not be filtered out by it.
- The authors' holdings must remain separate from later program premises. Pope/Osborne source claims, our editorial synthesis, later ratified constitutive extensions, imported mathematics, and measurements each need their own status.
- Use the compact Core for orientation and read the relevant dossiers, source passages, known conflicts, and project-start questions before new work. The full Canon is the reference library, not the default payload for every session.

"Definitive" means controlled for a declared authenticated corpus and scope—not unrevisable. New source evidence or an explicit amendment can produce a new version.

The Canon is a matched set:
1. the full, quotation-backed Source-Grounded Canon;
2. a compact generated Canon Core;
3. a subject-specific Opening Context for each new exhibit;
4. the Project-Start Protocol;
5. hashes, source receipts, unresolved questions, and change control.
