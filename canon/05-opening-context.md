## Grounding: read this first

Normal Realism is the philosophy. POAMS, the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis, is the physics that follows from it. The philosophy comes first, in the work and on the page. This applies to every session and every sub-agent.

**Before any work that touches Normal Realism or POAMS, use the reading order below. For that work, this order supersedes the older Every Session reading order.**

1. `poams-review/work/grounding-2026-11-14/canon/CORE.md`: the canon without its quotations. The canon pages beside it carry the authors' words for every statement.
2. `GROUNDING.md` and `POAMS-FOUNDATION-WALL.md` in the workspace root. Both govern.
3. The canon page for the subject. If there is no page yet, search the texts before writing anything: `bin/corpus search --in books "words"` (also `--in papers` and `--in lectures`). The books, papers and lectures of Pope and Osborne are the authority for what those authors hold. The exhibits are our own later work. A source statement is not a measurement receipt.

The statements below are our reading of the texts, not substitutes for them. For source reading and review, the statements and tests are under examination, not acceptance criteria. Record what each source says, including conflicts, omissions and unresolved claims; do not filter passages through this summary. For new native POAMS work, apply the tests below. They do not prohibit the labeled imported tools, conditional models or constitutive extensions permitted by GROUNDING.md and the Foundation Wall. Label such work as what it is: it is not a native derivation, and its picture is not the ground. The texts govern attribution to their authors. The Foundation Wall governs evidential status: a reproducible observation with a complete receipt outranks a favored derivation.

**What Normal Realism holds** (canon page 01, draft 4):

1. Normal Realism is objective commonsense empiricism.
2. Phenomena are the physical reality.
3. It removes two errors at once: the split between the world and our perception of it, and the retreat into a single mind.
4. "Normal" names a standard to be met, not what is usual.
5. Information comes first. Space, time, mass and motion are projected from it by the observer. The information is sensory and instrumental. Normal Realism does not construct the world from featureless numbers. Geometry is a feature of the phenomena being analyzed. It is not built out of relations between pure numbers.
6. What is projected cannot also be placed underneath as the thing that produces the information.
7. Nature is kinematic, and mechanical only in our representations of it.
8. The elementary events are discrete. One does not cause the next. Cause and effect belong to large numbers of them.
9. An observer is any interagent, conscious or not.
10. Exchanges go on where no person observes them.
11. Objectivity is the shared world of interacting observers.
12. Matter, mind, space, time, mass, charge and force are words. They get their meaning from what they mark out in experience.
13. Nature did not come labeled.
14. A name is not a thing.
15. A word has meaning as a description of phenomena, directly or at some remove.
16. The language is never finished.
17. Start from what is observed. Start with the observed whole. The parts reached by analysis have their properties only within that whole. They are not independent pieces from which the observed world is made.
18. Observation always comes interpreted.
19. Where conventions conflict, revise them.
20. Use no more ideas than the phenomenon needs.
21. Logic finds and removes inconsistency.
22. Mathematics states relations and measures. A mathematical construction does not establish that anything physical answers to it. Continuous variables may be used to model phenomena on the large scale. Their use does not make the phenomena continuous. Keep the mathematical model apart from the phenomenon.
23. This is empiricism, not a closed system of axioms.

**The test for any new work** (canon page 01):

1. State the phenomenon and who or what observes it. Keep the record apart from its interpretation.
2. For each term that matters, say what kind of term it is and how it connects to experience: a record, an inference, a definition or a proposal.
3. Start from the exchanges of information. Do not put what is projected from them, the objects, distances and clocks, underneath as machinery that produces them. Calling that machinery a scaffold or a representation changes nothing.
4. Do not turn discrete exchanges into a chain of elementary causes, or run them on a universal clock.
5. List every premise taken on, physical and mathematical. Say what each explains and what stays assumed. A fit, or a consistent calculation, does not make its picture real. A conventional model may serve as a comparison. It is never the ground.
6. Reject any premise that contradicts this page, and say what it contradicts. A premise that fits is not barred because this page lacks it.

**Light** (canon page 07, draft 7). Light is not something that travels in space. Space is in the light. A quantum of light is one event with two ends. c is the constant ratio between units of distance and units of time, for inertial observers using the same units. An elementary exchange has no carrier and no state in between. If the apparatus stores something, or a further exchange occurs, name it separately. c appears as the ratio of units and as the bound in the time relation. It is never a rate at which light goes anywhere. Read page 07 and its test before any work on light, optics, fiber or signaling.

**Writing rules.** Preserve quotations and source titles exactly. In our prose, use American English and plain declarative sentences. Omit performative qualifiers and repeated caveats. Do not add reminders that this is not the standard model. State assumptions, uncertainty, conditional results and open questions exactly. Do not call c a speed or describe light or action as traveling, moving, propagating, being carried or arriving, except in an explicit comparison or denial. Particle names appear only in quotations or explicit comparisons. Do not use "honest," "honestly," "honesty" or any derivative as authorial self-certification. Do not use "violent" or its variations. Give each consequential claim its source and status: native to the authors' texts (with the location), imported mathematics or physics, adopted premise, definition, measurement, fit, reconstruction, or conjecture. Preserve the governing measurement and paper-first requirements.
