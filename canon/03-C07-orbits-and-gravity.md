# C07 — Orbits and gravity

## Holdings

**C07-H0001 — The account begins with observed orbital relations, not an unseen pull.** Normal Realism supplies the philosophical starting point. Osborne presents naturally orbital free motion as a physical postulate, not as a definition forced by the philosophy alone.

> "In particular, POAMS postulates that all ‘force-free’ motion (used here in the same sense as in GR) is naturally orbital, with no need to postulate any in vacuo ‘gravitational forces’ being responsible for these curvilinear motions." — immediate-distant-action:P1386

**C07-H0002 — A natural orbit is the motion a body would have without imposed restrictions.** The distinction is between a free orbit and a constrained one. Weight is treated through the latter, rather than as an invisible force already acting in the former. This is Osborne's account of the physical meaning of force.

> "The natural orbit of any particle may therefore be thought of as the path described by the particle when all restrictions on its motion are removed, that is, when the particle moves freely under the influence of nothing but its own angular momentum." — immediate-distant-action:P1388

> "In contrast, ‘real’ forces, i.e., forces which are actually experienced and measurable in some way, are very necessary in POAMS." — immediate-distant-action:P1387

**C07-H0003 — Conservation alone does not select the equation of motion.** Osborne explicitly retains mathematical elements of existing dynamics. An orbital calculation therefore needs its physical postulates, mathematical assumptions, initial conditions and empirical inputs stated separately. The joint Light-Speed book extends its time-dilation account to nonclosed, precessing orbits; its perihelion calculation expressly imports Møller’s standard derivation. The assumptions and reported comparison are retained in C07, Differences and open rules.

> "It is quite clear that any conservation principle does not automatically provide an equation of motion." — immediate-distant-action:P1392

> "Hence, for example, since observational and empirical evidence tells us that the planets follow nearly elliptical orbits, a (mathematically) Newtonian approach can be taken in POAMS when studying such orbits, so that the equation of motion is mathematically the same as the Newtonian equation of motion in this case." — immediate-distant-action:P1392

**C07-H0004 — The worked two-body construction is an idealization.** It is not the full many-body universe. The chapter starts with a much smaller orbiting mass and negligible spin; its time parameter is distinguished from discrete observational duration. In *Einstein's Lost Legacy*, the authors claim slower clocks nearer a central mass and an ideal remote zero of orbital time dilation called Deep Space Time (DST). Its satellite confirmation claim coexists with a numerical comparison reaching only approximately 60% of the cited difference. These are source reports under the stated idealization, not fresh measurements; C08 and C11 retain the DST reference-frame issues.

> "Of course, we readily acknowledge that such a pure, isolated system is not observable in practice." — immediate-distant-action:P1394

> "Here, t is used in its usual sense of the time parameter, which enters all the dynamical laws of physics, as opposed to time in the sense of observational time, or duration, which is discrete in POAMS" — immediate-distant-action:P1397

**C07-H0005 — The later joint book proposes spin-dependent orbital changes.** In its stated example, co-sense spin reduces the natural orbital radius and opposite spin increases it. These are source predictions. They are not measurements or a general transition law.

> "In particular, if a satellite spins in the same direction as its orbital motion, then it will orbit closer to the Earth than if it were not spinning. If it spins in the opposite direction to its orbital motion, then it will orbit further out than if it were not spinning." — einsteins-lost-legacy:P0641

> "A gyroscope resting on the Earth’s surface at the equator should therefore weigh somewhat more when spinning in the same direction as that of the Earth, and less when spinning in the opposite direction." — einsteins-lost-legacy:P0641

**C07-H0006 — The authors distinguish their prediction from disputed experimental reports.** The book questions both the magnitude and replication of the cited gyroscope results. Its hope for a decisive test remains prospective.

> "It is to be hoped, however, that this problem may sometime be solved, creating a means of decisively testing the POAMS prediction of the ‘levitation’ and ‘depression’ of spinning bodies." — einsteins-lost-legacy:P0641

## What the texts reject

They reject the necessity of an invisible force maintaining a free orbit. Osborne's wording is narrower than a declaration that every possible vacuum force has been disproved:

> "It should be noted here that POAMS does not imply that vacuum forces do not exist, only that they are superfluous" — immediate-distant-action:P1387

They do not reject measurable constraint forces. Nor does their synthesis reject every equation used in Newtonian dynamics or relativity: immediate-distant-action:P1381 explicitly retains selected mathematics while changing its interpretation. Substituting a familiar force name for an unexplained agency is rejected; explicitly labeled mathematical comparison is not.

## Differences and open rules

**Conservation versus dynamics.** The qualification in H0003 must accompany claims of derivation by conservation alone. The accepted L02 review, Q01–Q05, identifies additional assumptions in Lectures 14–17: inward direction, rotational symmetry, closed-orbit selection and observational shape comparison. It also retains the rectilinear limiting case. The claim that acceleration is parallel to a radius does not alone choose its inward sign. These are review findings about the argument, not additional author holdings.

**Nonclosed orbits and perihelion.** The closed-orbit requirement in the initial construction is not a postulate that every natural orbit closes. The joint book claims that conservation with time-dilation effects yields approximately elliptical, precessing orbits (light-speed-gravitation-and-quantum-instantaneity:P1427–P1428):

> "It is to be stressed that POAMS does not begin by postulating that all natural orbits are closed." — light-speed-gravitation-and-quantum-instantaneity:P1428
> "In this case, some of the natural orbits are approximately ellipses with a small perihelion shift and so are no longer closed." — light-speed-gravitation-and-quantum-instantaneity:P1428

The construction assumes a static, spherically symmetric central body, identifies natural circular orbits with circular geodesics, and requires the noncircular equation to approximate the Newtonian elliptical-orbit equation (P1392, P1401–P1408, P1422). The source restricts the resulting description to radii greater than three times MG/c² (P1424). The perihelion approximation treats 2MG/c² as small relative to orbital radii and drops terms above first order (P1474–P1478). These are source assumptions and mathematical choices, not consequences of conservation alone. The calculation is explicitly imported:

> "Since this result is fundamental, our derivation here expresses, in full, a standard derivation originally due to Møller [22]." — light-speed-gravitation-and-quantum-instantaneity:P1473

P1491 reports 43.03 seconds of arc per century for Mercury against a cited 43.11 ± 0.45, while explaining that planetary comparisons involve many-body influences and a residual beyond the Newtonian account. This is the book's claimed agreement, not a replay of observations or independent confirmation of its physical interpretation.

**Spin and orbit are not interchangeable accounts.** The accepted L02 review, Q06–Q09, keeps open the passage from conservation of total angular momentum to fixed orbital angular momentum, the proposed composition of energies, and the transition between circular solutions. Its inclination counterexamples prevent treating every orientation claim as settled. No new corrected spin law is supplied here.

**Different spin drafts remain different.** B2a records Pope's direction-independent contraction discussion in angular-momentum-conservation-spin-plus-orbit:P0017–P0020 and the expansion argument in further-deliberations-on-angular-momentum-experiment:P0005–P0030. The latter's original-page qualification is preserved in PC32–PC33. H0005 reports the later joint book; it does not silently reconcile these earlier assumptions or signs.

**Orbital clock rates and the reported shortfall.** *Einstein's Lost Legacy*, P0421–P0422, substitutes GM/r for the squared orbital speed in its time-dilation formula. It places the ideal zero at theoretically infinite radius, where orbital speed and orbital time dilation vanish, and calls that reference DST. Clocks nearer the central mass are predicted to run more slowly. C08 retains DST's inertial-reference role; C11 retains the universal-time disagreement. This ideal reference does not settle that disagreement.

> "In Normal Realism this ideal datum-zero on the scale of time dilations is called Deep Space Time (DST)." — einsteins-lost-legacy:P0421

P0423 extends the claim to straight radial motion, invokes Pound–Rebka, and calls the satellite comparison direct confirmation; P0424 supplies the authors' account of the reported resonance shifts. The satellite assertion is:

> "Discussions with these experts have disclosed that clocks on these satellites run quicker than clocks on the Earth’s surface, albeit slower than clocks further out in space, which is direct confirmation of our POAMS prediction." — einsteins-lost-legacy:P0423

P0425 then substitutes a free-orbiting surface-radius clock standard as an approximation to GMT, although terrestrial clocks are constrained rather than freely orbiting and the source limits standard G to free orbits. Its numerical comparison is explicitly short:

> "the satellite gains 22859 nanoseconds per day, which is approximately 60% of the reported average value of 38700 nanoseconds gained by those satellites as measured from the ground." — einsteins-lost-legacy:P0425
> "However, the values predicted by the formula, while qualitatively correct, are deduced for ideally circular orbits involving no more than just two bodies in a single instance" — einsteins-lost-legacy:P0425

The authors appeal to the actual many-body system to explain the limited accuracy; that appeal is not a completed correction of the shortfall. The confirmation wording and the approximately-60% comparison must both remain. These GPS and Pound–Rebka statements are source-reported support, not newly authenticated measurement receipts.

**Apparent light deflection.** C05, Differences and open rules, retains the Light-Speed book's material-refraction proposal and limiting Schwarzschild-geodesic comparison alongside Legacy's informational and orbital-geometrical formulation. The perihelion calculation does not supply a material-index law or establish the equivalence of those formulations.

**Orbital-time source defects and other experimental claims.** B1A-R09 records radical-placement differences in immediate-distant-action:P1893/P1895 and further original-page debts. B3c retains numerical, dimensional and signed-branch defects in Lectures 20–24. No affected equation or numerical prediction is promoted here. Lectures 22–24 remain attributed experimental reports, not complete measurement receipts; their preliminary conclusions and unstable signals remain material.

**Whole-system language.** Immediate-distant-action:P1384/P1389 uses every-body correlation language. The discrete, intransitive account in the later joint book does not provide a schedule connecting every pair at each event. C06 owns the general distinction; C08 owns inertia; C11 owns cosmic reference-time disagreements.

## Test for new work

*Editorial synthesis, not quotations or new physical premises.*

- **C07-T0001:** Identify the observed trajectory or constraint record before specifying its interpretation.
- **C07-T0002:** List the orbit law, symmetry, closure, mass hierarchy, initial conditions and empirical constants separately from conservation.
- **C07-T0003:** Distinguish orbital from spin angular momentum, total from component conservation, and a family of circular solutions from an actual transition between them.
- **C07-T0004:** State the constraint that makes a force measurable. Do not erase measured work by rejecting a vacuum-force picture.
- **C07-T0005:** Attach source version, orientation, fixed quantities and evidential status to every spin-effect claim. Do not resolve a source disagreement with a later program premise.
