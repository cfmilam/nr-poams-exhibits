The Census Flow — Why the Table Closes at Two Turns

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian (Viv) Pope and Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

Philosophy first: there is one substance — angular momentum — and a quantum is a completed turn. The order in which the elements build was computed from that premise in Aufbau as Closure. This exhibit shows why the computation had no choice: written in its natural variables, the self-consistent census is a single flowing curve in a plane, and an exact piece of algebra pins that flow to the closure value at the one point where the frontier is read. 1 · The frontier, restated as a flow

The census ledger of Aufbau as Closure is the standard self-consistent screening function $\chi(x)$ IMPORT (the statistical stand-in for the stacked closures — what extant physics calls Thomas–Fermi), obeying one equation: $\chi'' = \chi^{3/2}/\sqrt{x}$. Two bookkeeping variables turn that equation into something you can watch:

$$t = -\frac{d\ln\chi}{d\ln x} \quad\text{(the local steepness of the screen)},\qquad q = x^3\chi \quad\text{(the ledger weight at radius } x\text{)},$$ $$\frac{dt}{ds} = t + t^2 - \sqrt{q}, \qquad \frac{dq}{ds} = q\,(3-t), \qquad s = \ln x .$$

No approximation — the census equation is this autonomous plane flow. And the whole atom is one trajectory of it: it launches from the free eye $(t,q)=(0,0)$ (bare circulation, unscreened) and lands on the fixed point $(3,\,144)$ — which is exactly the Sommerfeld far field $\chi = 144/x^3$. Every neutral census, every $Z$, is this same curve: the shape is universal, only the scale changes.

Rates, not speeds (GROUNDING discipline): $t$ and $q$ are pure bookkeeping of the circulation ledger — a log-slope and a weighted count. Nothing here moves through a void; $s$ is log-radius, not time.

2 · The criticality line and the closure point

Two facts single out one point of the plane before any dynamics:

The criticality line $t=1$. On every zero-energy orbit, in any central ledger, the angle-averaged steepness is exactly $1$ (a one-line identity proved in the research ledger) — the frontier is a critical probe, always reading the marginal exponent. The frontier's edge — where the last winding barely binds — sits precisely where the ledger crosses $t=1$.

The closure value $\sqrt{q} = 3/2$. The apsidal slope at the edge obeys $k_{edge} = \sqrt{2/(2-\sqrt{q})}\,\big|_{t=1}$ (one line from the census equation). The Madelung double turn — $k=2$, the 720° closure that generates the $n{+}l$ families — corresponds to exactly $\sqrt{q} = 3/2$ at the crossing. Below it, $k<2$: the tie-break that opens every period with an s-orbit.

So the entire question "why does the table order itself by $n+l$, resolved downward?" compresses to: where does the one universal trajectory cross the line $t=1$?

3 · The one-way valve — an exact factorization

Consider the curve where the flow's climb rate equals the closure member's own peak rate, $\tfrac12$:

$$\mathcal{B}_{1/2}:\quad \sqrt{q} = t + t^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

Whether a trajectory can cross this curve reduces to a single polynomial condition — and that polynomial factors exactly:

$$5 - 3t - 4t^2 + 2t^3 \;=\; (t-1)\,(2t^2 - 2t - 5).$$

PROVED Valve/tangency theorem. For all $t \in (0,1)$ the curve $\mathcal{B}_{1/2}$ is a one-way valve: the census flow can cross it downward only, strictly. At $t = 1$ — the criticality line — the flow is exactly tangent to the curve, and the curve passes exactly through $\sqrt{q} = 3/2$ — the closure point. Beyond $t=1$ the valve flips (upward-crossable, until $t = \tfrac{1+\sqrt{11}}{2} \approx 2.158$). The number $3/2$ is not imported and not fitted: it is where $(t-1)$ divides the census field's crossing polynomial. The criticality point and the closure value are welded together by the algebra of the flow itself.
