The POAMS Spectral Formula

Level (term) energy. Appendix 3 derives, from time dilation plus the requirement that time is discrete (so t_R/t = n/N, integers), the standing-mode energy:

$$\nu = cR\left(1 - \frac{N^2}{n^2}\right)$$

With the ground mode as reference (N = 1) this is the level ladder En = mc²(1 − 1/n²), rising to the ionization limit mc² as n → ∞.

Observed spectral lines. Each line is a difference of two levels — a re-closure from one standing mode to another — giving the standard Balmer–Rydberg form:

$$\nu = cR\left(\frac{1}{N^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)$$

N is the lower mode, n the upper: Lyman (N = 1), Balmer (N = 2), Paschen (N = 3)… The level form and the line form coincide for transitions to the ground mode (N = 1), which is why N = 1 reproduces observed hydrogen directly. 7. The POAMS Mathematical Derivation

Here's how POAMS derives the Balmer-Rydberg formula from first principles using only angular momentum quantization and Pythagorean geometry:

Step 1: Angular Momentum Quantization

$$L = mvr = n\hbar$$

Angular momentum comes in discrete units of ℏ

Step 2: Energy from Angular Momentum

$$E = \frac{L^2}{2mr^2} = \frac{n^2\hbar^2}{2mr^2}$$

Kinetic energy of orbital angular momentum

Step 3: Time Dilation Relationship

$$E = mc^2\left[1 - \frac{\tau^2}{t^2}\right]$$

Where τ is proper time, t is coordinate time

Step 4: Level Energies, then the Spectral Lines

The time-relation gives the standing-mode level (term) energy:

$$\nu = cR\left(1 - \frac{N^2}{n^2}\right)$$

The observed lines are differences of these levels — the standard Balmer–Rydberg form:

$$\nu = cR\left(\frac{1}{N^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)$$

Directly derived from the angular-momentum–time relationship

The Result: The famous Balmer–Rydberg formula emerges naturally from angular-momentum quantization and relativistic time dilation: the standing modes fix the level energies, and each spectral line is a difference of two levels — a re-closure from one closed winding to another.
