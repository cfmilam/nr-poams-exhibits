3 · The isolated-channel criterion

When may two circulations exchange winding at all? Only through a beat whose phase accumulates slowly enough that transfer is coherent turn after turn — a phase-matched channel. Reducing the coupling term of the pair Lagrangian at a $j{:}(j{-}1)$ commensurability (ten lines, pendulum form) gives the channel its width:

$$W = 2(j{-}1)\,n\,\sqrt{3\,\alpha\,f'\,|f_d(\alpha)|\,e}\ , \qquad\text{channel open}\iff |p\,n' - q\,n| < W .$$

Here $n,n'$ are the measured rates, $\alpha$ is the geometry ratio, $f'$ the partner's measured partition fraction, $e$ the relevant eccentricity, and $f_d(\alpha)$ the cited first-order coefficient. Thus $W$ has units of rate. This displayed expression applies only to an isolated first-order pair channel. Higher-order pairs and three-centre combinations require their own coefficients and normal forms; their widths are not supplied by this formula. In an overlapping domain, no single scalar width is invariant. The operational gate is therefore: compute a correctly scoped width where isolation holds, otherwise test the registered critical angle directly.
