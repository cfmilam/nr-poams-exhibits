1 · One ledger, many centres

Bruns (1887) and Poincaré proved that an $n$-body system has no analytic integrals beyond the ten classical ones — energy, total momentum, total angular momentum, the centre-of-mass pair. For 140 years that theorem was read as defeat: the problem "cannot be solved." Read it in momenta and it inverts into a founding claim: the exactly conserved content of an $n$-body system is its momenta — nothing else. The theorem is not the obituary of celestial mechanics; it is the inventory of what is real. Trajectories are the map; the ledger is the territory.

What remains after the dissolution (see the Navigation Engine, §D) is structure: circulations couple where their rates lock in low-order ratios and stay effectively separate where the ratios are incommensurate. The working description of $n \ge 3$ is not a trajectory — it is a network of channels.

3 · The channel criterion — nothing fitted

When may two circulations exchange winding at all? Only through a beat whose phase accumulates slowly enough that transfer is coherent turn after turn — a phase-matched channel. Reducing the coupling term of the pair Lagrangian at a $j{:}(j{-}1)$ commensurability (ten lines, pendulum form) gives the channel its width:

$$W = 2(j{-}1)\,n\,\sqrt{3\,\alpha\,f'\,|f_d(\alpha)|\,e}\ , \qquad\text{channel open}\iff |p\,n' - q\,n| < W .$$

Rates, a geometry ratio $\alpha$, the partner's partition fraction $f'$, an eccentricity, and a computed coefficient $f_d$ (the classical expansion of the coupling — cited, and verified against the tabulated value to 0.6% before anything was scored). Higher-order channels carry coupling $\propto e^{|p-q|}$: widths collapse steeply with order, and that is what defeats the density of the rationals — every ratio in the sky is near some rational; almost none is near an open channel. House rule (the Wyler gate): no resonance may be named by numerology — every integer pair buys its place through a computed $W$, or not at all.
