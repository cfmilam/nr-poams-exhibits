Casimir and van der Waals Forces

Measured boundary-dependent forces, several calculational languages, and an ontology firewall against turning a successful formula into invisible substance.

QUEUE STATUS — DEVELOPMENTAL. Dispersion and Casimir–Lifshitz forces are measured. Their extant response theory is imported. POAMS may prefer a relational boundary account, but it has not derived polarizability, the Lifshitz kernel, attraction/repulsion, or a prospective deviation.

2. One family, several regimes

London / nonretarded

For nonoverlapping isotropic ground states,

$$U_{AB}(R)=-{C_6^{AB}\over R^6},\quad C_6^{AB}={3\hbar\over\pi}\int_0^\infty\alpha_A(i\xi)\alpha_B(i\xi)d\xi.$$

The imaginary-frequency integral is a transform of causal response—not an apparatus oscillating at imaginary frequency.

Retarded / Casimir–Polder

At sufficiently large separations relative to response wavelengths,

$$U_{AB}\to-{23\hbar c\over4\pi R^7}\alpha_A(0)\alpha_B(0).$$

The crossover is spectral and material-dependent, not a universal distance.

Macroscopic bodies

Lifshitz/scattering calculations use reflection response, temperature, geometry and an intervening medium. Suitable dielectric ordering across a liquid can produce repulsion; the family is not “always attractive.”

3. Ideal benchmark—and why it is not the experiment

For perfect parallel conductors,

$$ {E\over A}=-{\pi^2\hbar c\over720a^3},\qquad {F\over A}=-{\pi^2\hbar c\over240a^4}.$$

The ideal pressure magnitude is about 13.0 Pa at 100 nm and 1.30 mPa at 1 µm. Because $P\propto a^{-4}$, a 1 nm separation bias at 100 nm creates roughly a 4% pressure bias.

For a sphere above a plane, the proximity-force approximation gives

$$F_{PFA}=-{\pi^3\hbar cR_s\over360a^3},$$

with curvature corrections controlled by $a/R_s$. Exact scattering calculations are needed when the demanded uncertainty resolves those corrections.

4. Corrections and raw receipts

Model inputs

  • sample-specific optical response and low-frequency extrapolation
  • temperature and thermal contribution
  • geometry, edges and PFA error
  • roughness, coatings, anisotropy and substrate
  • medium composition and contamination

Raw apparatus channels

  • torsion angle, cantilever deflection or frequency shift
  • actuator voltage and separation sensor
  • surface potential and bias sweep
  • temperature, pressure, vibration and timing
  • topography and lateral interaction location
Calibration wall. Patch electrostatics, absolute gap, tilt, hydrodynamic drag, capillary forces, optical back-action and analysis flexibility can each imitate a residual. An ideal-law subtraction without this ledger is not an ontological discovery.

5. Ontology firewall

  1. Measured: specified bodies exhibit a reproducible force/gradient with controlled material and geometric dependence.
  2. Formalism: mode sums, stress tensors, scattering matrices, Lifshitz response or source diagrams calculate it.
  3. Ontology: one decomposition is literal substance in otherwise empty space.

The first is empirical; the second can be multiply represented; the third does not follow merely because prediction succeeds. Jaffe showed the force can be formulated without assigning physical reality to zero-point energy. Conversely, avoiding “vacuum stuff” does not itself derive a POAMS result.

6. POAMS interface and engineering relevance

The complete-apparatus discipline fits naturally: both surfaces, actuator, support, electrostatic calibration, medium and reset work belong to one account. Existing whole-atom response calculations can provide declared $C_6$ controls under imported operators. Still missing are a closure-derived polarizability, dynamic-response law, material map to $\epsilon(i\xi)$, attraction/repulsion rule and prospective deviation.

Engineering value requires no new ontology: MEMS stiction control, resonators, force metrology, liquid-mediated repulsion, material selection and searches for additional short-range interactions.
