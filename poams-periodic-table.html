Harmonic Mode Physics: What Is Derived, and What Is Assumed

Two logically separate things are in play, and intellectual honesty requires keeping them apart — claiming exactly what the mode structure forces, and no more.

Exact harmonic count; adopted doublet and exclusion rule. Each angular harmonic \(l\) supplies \(2l+1\) angular modes, so summing \(l=0,\ldots,n-1\) gives exactly \(n^2\) angular slots. The familiar maximum sub-capacities s = 2, p = 6, d = 10, f = 14 and totals \(2n^2=2,8,18,32,\ldots\) follow only after supplying both the observed/imported two-state spinor doublet and the imported exclusion rule of at most one occupant per complete state. The arithmetic is exact conditional on those two state laws; POAMS whole-turn counting has independently selected neither. Those totals are not yet the observed period lengths, which also require an ordering rule.

Assumed — an ordering postulate of exactly the same status as the conventional Madelung \( (n+l) \) rule. To turn those sub-capacities into the observed period lengths, POAMS assumes that mode energies order by increasing \( (n+l) \), ties broken by \( n \) — physically, that opening a new radial node at larger radius can cost less energy than completing a higher angular harmonic at smaller radius (e.g. the 4th radial mode filling before the smaller-radius d-harmonic is complete).

Given this ordering, the period lengths 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32 — including the characteristic doubling (8 and 8, 18 and 18, 32 and 32) — follow from the counting above with no further freedom: under the \( (n+l) \) ordering the sub-patterns fill in the sequence

\( 1s\,|\,2s\,2p\,|\,3s\,3p\,|\,4s\,3d\,4p\,|\,5s\,4d\,5p\,|\,6s\,4f\,5d\,6p\,|\,7s\,5f\,6d\,7p \)

where each bar marks a noble-gas closure. Reading off the capacity between successive closures gives the period lengths directly: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32.

The characteristic doubling — the repeats 8,8, 18,18, 32,32 — arises because each newly-available angular block first appears in one period and then recurs one radial step later:

  • the \( s\!+\!p \) blocks give \( 2s2p \) then \( 3s3p \) (the 8,8)
  • adding the \( d \) block gives \( 4s3d4p \) then \( 5s4d5p \) (the 18,18)
  • adding the \( f \) block gives \( 6s4f5d6p \) then \( 7s5f6d7p \) (the 32,32)

Each repeat is a block recurring at the next radius, which is exactly what the \( (n+l) \) ordering dictates.

So the doubling is a direct consequence of the ordering postulate, not a separate assumption — though, to be exact, each period is stitched from parts of two different \( (n+l) \) groups (the standard interleaving), not a single tidy group per period.
