2. The Philosophy

What is "Normal Realism"?

Normal Realism is Pope's term for a radical empiricist philosophy that:

  1. Rejects dualism: No fundamental split between "physical" and "mental" worlds—both are linguistic conventions for organizing experience.
  2. Embraces conventionalism: Physical concepts like "space," "time," "mass," "force" are human classificatory conventions, not features of external reality independent of observers.
  3. Maintains logical coherence: What makes these conventions "hang together" is logic, not correspondence to some external metaphysical reality.
  4. Preserves objectivity: Objectivity comes from the logical consistency and practical efficiency of our conventions, not from access to absolute external truth.
  5. Stays empirical: Only accept concepts that can be operationally defined through observation and measurement.

Use of Phenomenalism

POAMS draws heavily on the phenomenalist tradition:

Kant: Reality as we know it is structured by the forms of human cognition (space, time, causality). We cannot know "things-in-themselves" apart from our modes of experiencing them.

Mach: Time is known only through changes, space only through relations between objects. Physical concepts should be eliminable in favor of functional relationships between sensations.

Poincaré: Geometric and physical theories are conventions chosen for their mathematical convenience and logical consistency, not because they correspond to absolute truth.

Pope synthesizes these into "Normal Realism" - a position that is neither naive realism (external objects exist independently) nor idealism (only mind exists), but a sophisticated conventionalism where reality is the logical structure of our observational practices.

Observer-Centered Approach

Unlike classical physics with its "God's-eye-view," POAMS is rigorously observer-centered:

  1. No absolute simultaneity: "Now" is always relative to an observer's reference frame
  2. No absolute space: Spatial relationships are projected from informational patterns
  3. No view from nowhere: All physical knowledge is necessarily finite and perspectival
  4. Relational reality: Objects exist only in relationships, not as independent substances

This leads to the radical conclusion that "space is in the light" rather than "light in space"—we project spatial relationships from the informational content of optical phenomena rather than discovering pre-existing spatial containers.
