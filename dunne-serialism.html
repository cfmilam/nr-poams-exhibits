Normal Realism and J. W. Dunne's Serialism

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian (Viv) Pope and Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

Regression, observer-order, and what POAMS keeps from Dunne's serial universe.

Dunne is related work, not POAMS. This exhibit keeps his regression / observer-order insight, corrects the time-as-substance reading, and sets aside the immortality and universal-mind conclusions unless they can be derived from Normal Realism rather than asserted from serialism.

2 · Dunne’s regress engine

Dunne’s central move is simple and powerful: if we mark one state as now, the mark itself has a past, present, and future. To say where the now-mark is now, we need a wider ordering. Then that wider now-mark needs another ordering. The regress is not added later; it is implicit in treating any state as present.

Dunne’s L–M–N example: the first-term row is not enough to make M present. The “presentness” of the mark has to be marked in a higher row, and so on.

3 · Normal Realism translation

Normal Realism does not need Dunne’s extra time-stuff. It keeps the discipline of the regress and changes the ontology: time is not a container through which a self travels. In POAMS terms, time is accumulated action/angular order — $L=dS/d\theta$ — and a “now” is the local cut an observer/instrument takes through that ordered whole.

Dunne: $$\text{state} + \text{now-mark} \Rightarrow \text{higher-order now-mark} \Rightarrow \cdots$$
NR/POAMS: $$\text{observation} = \text{finite cut through accumulated order, not a primitive present}$$

4 · Physics contact points

Relativity

Dunne treats Minkowski’s map as a travelling-observer map, not the real world itself. He rotates second-term time into first-term time with $\sqrt{-1}$ and recovers a Minkowski-like interval. We keep this as history and diagram logic, not as a POAMS proof.

Quantum theory

Dunne’s strongest physics overlap is his refusal to turn uncertainty into object-world indeterminacy. His line is: the trap determines the aspect caught. POAMS can use that instinct, but must ground it in action/angular-momentum bookkeeping.

Nature review, 1934

The contemporary review explicitly reads Dunne as applying regress to relativity/Minkowski imaginary time and to Heisenberg uncertainty, locating uncertainty in instruments of observation rather than the world observed. That confirms the exhibit’s framing: Dunne belongs in the “related mathematical/philosophical pressure on physics” shelf, not the occult shelf.

6 · Viv Pope to Penny Gower — the wall comes down

Between 2005 and 2011 Viv Pope carried on a long email correspondence on religion with Penny Gower — a practising Christian — with a contribution from Alison Pope, later edited into Theological Discussion. It is the natural bridge from Dunne’s serialism to Normal Realism, because it is exactly where Viv lets the regress touch God, the soul, and judgement — and it is where he names Dunne as an independent discoverer of the same serial-time idea he had reached himself: the dimensional regress “that was conceived independently by J.W. Dunne and myself.”

Two moves matter here. First, Viv dismantles the God’s-eye view — the same absolute, observer-free vantage POAMS refuses in physics. There is no God’s-eye view and no God’s time; as mere observers “we cannot see the whole picture,” and some things, he is explicit, we simply cannot know. (An NR corollary: there may be quantities — a clean first-principles value of the fine-structure constant, say — that are constitutively not ours to derive, only to measure.) Second, he relocates the divine. Not a “God of empty spaces” floating among the stars, but a logos — the rationality “that has to exist before any project is undertaken,” the Prime Mover — expressed within creation and within us. In his words, if “God and Devil exist anywhere, they have to be within ourselves.” A god within, not without; the power and the glory working through all things, not imposed upon them from outside.

From this he draws the line that ties straight into the Time-Rope: “What is done is done, stays done and can never be undone.” The past is not gone — it is indelibly wound into the system of things, objectively accessible in retrospect. Viv argues that a purely one-way, perishable time makes nihilism unavoidable (“nothing matters,” and psychopathy becomes the norm), whereas an indelible past leaves logical room for a retrospective — never prospective — Final Judgement: in some real sense we are still “there to receive it.” A world with no judgement beyond this realm is a world ripe for violence and lawlessness. The god he is left with is the one Viv found in Voltaire:

“He is present everywhere … but everywhere where there is something, not where there is not. To be present in nothing seems to me a contradiction in terms, an absurdity … I will not set up the throne of God in imaginary spaces.”
— Voltaire, Selected Works (trans. J. McCabe, 1935), as quoted by Viv Pope in The Eye of the Beholder.

POAMS extrapolation — marked, not a derivation

Wind this around the Time-Rope and a disciplined picture of the dead follows. Because what has been cannot be un-happened, the departed persist — their souls, if you will — but only in the time prior to their death, never after; the future is not yet, and there is no travel to or from it (only relativistic time-slides). This is why apparitions, in every culture’s vocabulary of ghosts and revenants, are reported in places and times of the past, never the future. Black holes are metaphysics dressed as physics; the indelible past, the soul-as-history, and judgement are the reverse — ordinary consequences of taking time seriously. Viv himself held the bare survival question open (his mirror-reflected-in-mirror analogy cuts the other way). We therefore mark this as an NR-consistent extension, not a POAMS proof.

Source: Viv Pope & Penny Gower, Theological Discussion (email correspondence, 2005–2011), published at nr-poams-papers/theological-discussion.html.

7 · Michael Levin — the Platonic space (related, non-POAMS)

A modern resonance worth flagging: the developmental biologist Dr. Michael Levin (thoughtforms.life) argues that living matter navigates a Platonic space of forms — “variable-agency patterns that in-form physics, biology, computer science, and cognitive science.” Mind is not a late accident bolted onto dead mechanism; it is continuous, present in degrees all the way down — cells, tissues, and morphogenetic collectives solving problems in anatomical and physiological space. Forms of life, forms of mind.

This is Raphael’s School of Athens in one image: Aristotle’s hand pressing down toward the things of this world, Plato’s hand pointing up toward the forms above — two sides of one coin. Normal Realism thins the coin almost to nothing. If the “physical” is, as POAMS holds, nothing more (or less) than time — accumulated angular order — then the boundary between the physical and the formal, the material and the “spiritual,” is razor-thin, if it is a line at all. Levin approaches it from biology and information; POAMS from angular momentum and time; Dunne and Pope from the observer regress. Different doors, one threshold.
