Stern-Gerlach as Two Chiralities

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian “Viv” Pope & Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

The 1922 Two-Spot Deflection Read from Elementary Closure Topology

§1 — What Was Measured

In 1922 Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach sprayed a beam of silver atoms through an inhomogeneous magnetic field and photographed the deflection pattern on a detector plate. The classical prediction was a smooth line: silver's magnetic moment can take any orientation in the beam, so its projection along the field gradient should give a continuous distribution of deflections.

The plate showed two discrete spots. Not a smooth line, not three spots, not a distribution — two.

The result stood as the first direct measurement of quantized angular momentum in atoms and remains the most-cited experimental anchor for atomic-scale angular-momentum quantization. Its interpretation has since been folded into the standard quantum-mechanical postulate of intrinsic “electron spin” (cross-ontology label; see §2). This exhibit reads the same measurement through POAMS ontology, arriving at the same numerics from a different (and, we argue, cleaner) starting point.

§2 — The Extant Reading (Cross-Ontology Comparison)

CROSS-ONTOLOGY

The paragraphs below use extant quantum-mechanical vocabulary only for comparative purposes. Nothing in the POAMS derivation that follows depends on any of these terms.

In standard QM the two-spot result is explained by postulating that the silver atom's outer unpaired electron carries an intrinsic spin of one-half in units of $\hbar$. The associated magnetic moment is

$$\mu_z = g_s \, m_s \, \mu_B, \qquad m_s = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad g_s \approx 2$$

giving two discrete projections of magnitude $\mu_B$ (one Bohr magneton) along the field axis. The two spots correspond to $m_s = +\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $m_s = -\tfrac{1}{2}$.

The QM story requires spin to be introduced as a new metaphysical degree of freedom not derivable from the position and momentum operators of the theory. The Stern-Gerlach measurement is then evidence that this degree of freedom is quantized: only two projections, not a continuum.

POAMS gives the two-spot result without postulating a new degree of freedom. The two projections drop out of the topology of a single closure.

The elementary closure and its two admissible chiralities

An incomplete outer closure is a single elementary loop carrying stored angular momentum $L = \hbar$ (per GROUNDING §1: the elementary event is one closed cycle with $\oint L\, d\theta = 2\pi\hbar = h$). By Călugăreanu's theorem, a closed ribbon in three-space admits a signed integer linking number $Lk = Tw + Wr$. For an elementary closure — one that has not yet completed additional windings — the accessible linking values are exactly:

$$Lk \in \{+1, -1\}$$

These are the two admissible chiralities. Not a postulate — a geometric fact about a closed loop in three-space. A closed loop has exactly two orientations relative to any chosen axis; there is no third option because there is no continuous deformation from one orientation to the other without cutting the loop.

The two-spot result is topological, not new-postulate. The two admissible chiralities of the atom's single incomplete outer closure are the source of the two projections. Nothing else is required: not intrinsic spin as a new quantum number, not a two-valued spinor as a distinct algebraic object. The closure has two orientations because a closed loop in three-space has two orientations.
