Stern-Gerlach as Two Axis-Response Branches

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian “Viv” Pope & Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

The 1922 two-spot deflection with the measured state law stated explicitly

§1 — What Was Measured

In 1922 Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach sprayed a beam of silver atoms through an inhomogeneous magnetic field and photographed the deflection pattern on a detector plate. The classical prediction was a smooth line: silver's magnetic moment can take any orientation in the beam, so its projection along the field gradient should give a continuous distribution of deflections.

The plate showed two discrete spots. Not a smooth line, not three spots, not a distribution — two.

The result stood as the first direct measurement of quantized angular momentum in atoms and remains the most-cited experimental anchor for atomic-scale angular-momentum quantization. Its interpretation has since been folded into the standard quantum-mechanical postulate of intrinsic “electron spin” (cross-ontology label; see §2). This exhibit rebooks the same measurement through POAMS ontology while stating the decisive limit plainly: the two-valued magnetic state response is measured and matched here, not derived from closure topology.

§2 — The Extant Reading (Cross-Ontology Comparison)

CROSS-ONTOLOGY

The paragraphs below use extant quantum-mechanical vocabulary for comparison and to state the measured response law explicitly. The POAMS account that follows imports that law; it does not derive the spinor sector from closure topology.

In standard QM the two-spot result is explained by postulating that the silver atom's outer unpaired electron carries an intrinsic spin of one-half in units of $\hbar$. The associated magnetic moment is

$$\mu_z = -g_s \, m_s \, \mu_B, \qquad m_s = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad g_s \approx 2$$

giving two opposite magnetic responses of magnitude approximately $\mu_B$ (one Bohr magneton) along the field axis. The two spots correspond to $m_s = +\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $m_s = -\tfrac{1}{2}$; which detector side receives which branch depends on the axis/sign convention.

In nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, spin is an independent internal degree of freedom rather than an orbital variable constructed from position and momentum. The Stern–Gerlach measurement supports its two-valued axis response: two projections, not a classical continuum.

POAMS does not yet derive the spinor sector, its two projection values, or the magnetic coefficient. It can give the measured branches a POAMS-native reading — two axis-response records of the same species — and carry that specified response through a complete apparatus ledger without adding a second substance.
