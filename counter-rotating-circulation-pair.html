The September 2026 inquiry closes the automatic-amplification shortcut: an integer linking change does not set an energy quantum; phase agreement does not make every closure change; and internal reorganization does not give the whole apparatus an unbalanced impulse. §1 — Grounding: what the calculation uses

One substance: angular momentum. Circulation is the happening, not an object moving through a container. The angular-momentum Nexus is the whole relation; there is no aether, empty-space medium, or travelling intermediary. A localized change is a reorganization within that Nexus, not a disturbance launched into a second substance.

Discrete closures; energy as rate. The corpus reads a completed elementary closure as one $\hbar$ entry. For a mode with angular rate $\omega$, its energy entry is $\hbar\omega=h\nu$, where $\omega=2\pi\nu$. Throughout this page, $\nu$ is cycles per second (Hz), $\omega$ is radians per second, and $R$ is the number of actual events per second. These are not interchangeable counts.

Weight is the constraint bill. A free body follows its natural orbit. A support records the constraint needed to hold it off that orbit. A changed support reading, a recoil impulse and an internal torque are different observables; one is not proof of another. See Mass as Trapped Rate and The Navigation Engine.

The topology has to name its circulation. For a specified closed, non-self-intersecting framed ribbon,

$$Lk=Tw+Wr,\qquad Lk\in\mathbb Z.$$

$Lk$ links the ribbon's two edges. Twist and writhe are geometric contributions; they are not generally integers separately. Their smooth-chart variation does not introduce fractional elementary closures. The integer invariant survives deformations that preserve the ribbon's embedding and closure. A topology-changing event must leave those conditions, or exchange the relevant winding through a boundary. This is the mathematical content of Călugăreanu's theorem (Dennis & Hannay), not a transition-energy law.

Keep three books distinct: the self-linking of a framed circulation, the mutual linking of two different centerlines, and the phase-winding count of a physical circulation. A proposed device must identify which one changes and how it maps to the counted angular momentum. They cannot all be called “the body's $Lk$” without that bridge.

§4 — Threshold and domain: the finite transition ledger

1. An integer is a state label, not a trigger

A preserved $Lk$ does not creep through non-integer values until it “hits” the next integer. It remains the same integer throughout an admissible deformation. A genuine rejoining event can connect different topological sectors, but the linking identity supplies neither the drive value at which that happens nor a rule that every drive cycle must contain such an event.

Write $C$ for a finite configuration of circulation entries and $u$ for the imposed drive setting. Let $\mathcal B(C;u)$ be its action-rate cost in energy units, and let $\mathcal A(C;u)$ list the allowed re-filings. In the quasistatic, cost-minimizing case, local stability requires

$$\mathcal B(C';u)-\mathcal B(C;u)\geq0 \quad\text{for each }C'\in\mathcal A(C;u).$$

A lower-cost state alone does not guarantee immediate switching: an actual route, any barrier, and the driven state history must also be specified. This is the same finite-re-filing logic used in the Molecule. The finite angular model in §4A below supplies one explicit $\mathcal B$ and exchange graph; it does not yet supply the proposed apparatus's topological rejoining, drive and reset rule.

A short proof that topology cannot select the drive threshold

Consider integer-labelled states with costs $\mathcal B_k(u)=A(k-u/u_0)^2$, where $A>0$ and $u_0>0$. Adjacent costs cross at $u=(k+\tfrac12)u_0$. Every member of this family has the same integer labels, yet changing $u_0$ changes the crossing and changing $A$ changes its energy scale. A barrier can change the actual switching point further. These are counterexamples to uniqueness from integer labels alone, not fitted candidate laws for the device. A POAMS-specific cost and transition rule is what could select one member or exclude the entire family.

2. Price the change of rate, not the name of the topology

For counted modes whose energies add as $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{modes}}=\hbar\sum_i n_i\omega_i$, the exact finite change is

$$\Delta\mathcal B_{\mathrm{modes}} =\hbar\sum_i\left[\omega_i\Delta n_i+(n_i+\Delta n_i)\Delta\omega_i\right].$$

Any additional organization or pair term must be included once in the full cost. Released energy also depends on the work done by the drive during the transition. The special value $\hbar\omega_d=h\nu_d$ follows only when an actual single counted mode-step changes at that fixed rate, with the other terms accounted for. A change in a geometric linking integer does not establish those conditions or even require a nonzero change in the net AM vector.

The earlier assertion “every integer-$Lk$ crossing releases one $\hbar\omega_d$” is replaced by this conditional rate ledger. Quantized organization remains; a universal transition energy has not been derived.

3. Phase agreement is not the number of changing entries

A domain can be phase-aligned while only one entry changes, many entries change, or equal-and-opposite re-filings cancel in a particular observable. Define the active set from the actual transition:

$$\mathcal P=\{i:\text{entry }i\text{ changes in }C\to C'\}, \qquad N_{\mathrm{act}}=|\mathcal P|.$$

The former $N_{\mathrm{local}}\hbar\omega_d$ estimate is recovered only if all $N_{\mathrm{act}}$ participating entries release the same one-step energy at the same rate, and no other cost changes. Then $E_{\mathrm{event}}=N_{\mathrm{act}}h\nu_d$. Coherence can organize a collective event; it does not establish that counting rule. The former $10^6$–$10^{20}$ domain assignments were examples, not derived populations of the proposed devices.

A closure target, not three independent knobs. Supply one explicit driven transition: its before/after circulation census, permitted re-filing, action-rate cost and recovery path. That record determines which entries participate and the event energy. It must also name the external co-signer before it can predict a whole-apparatus impulse. None of these quantities may be chosen to hit a force target.

§5B — Whole-apparatus impulse: the counterpart and power budget

Internal reorganization versus external exchange

Include the complete test assembly in the device ledger. An exchange wholly inside that boundary has cancelling endpoint momentum entries. For an event that changes the device's net momentum, the balancing entry is external:

$$\Delta\vec p_{\mathrm{device}}+ \sum_b\Delta\vec p_{\mathrm{counterpart},b}=0.$$

The counterpart might be a support, the drive apparatus, another body, or a specified exchange with the wider Nexus. Naming it is not adding a medium or a traveller. “The Nexus” is not a numerical momentum law: its receiving circulation and the signed entry still have to be derived or measured. A balanced, isolated internal cycle supplies no net device impulse. A cycle exchanging momentum externally is a different, open ledger.

If the entire driven configuration and its environment are symmetric under reversal of the measurement axis, the corresponding mean directional impulse is zero. Opposite circulation senses alone do not establish an axial imbalance. An asymmetric drive or external partner can break that symmetry; its effect is then part of the calculation, not a freely assigned fraction.

The particular one-way exchange channel proposed earlier

Mass as Trapped Rate uses the one-way transaction bill $p=E/c$. Conditional on that channel, let $E_b\geq0$ be the energy transferred to external counterpart $b$, and $\hat n_b$ its momentum direction in the observer's chart. For the measured axis $\hat z$,

$$I_z=-\frac1c\sum_bE_b\,\hat n_b\cdot\hat z =\eta\frac{E_{\mathrm{out}}}{c}, \qquad |\eta|\leq1.$$

$\eta$ is the signed directional imbalance of the outgoing exchange, not simply the fraction of energy that is “not heat.” Equal opposite exchanges give $\eta=0$ even when the entire domain acts together. Here $c$ converts energy and momentum units; the calculation does not introduce travelling light.

Over a recorded interval $T$, sum the actual event impulses. For repeated equal events with mean rate $R$ and fixed $\eta$,

$$\overline F_z=\frac1T\sum_e I_{z,e} =R\eta\frac{E_{\mathrm{event}}}{c} =\eta\frac{P_{\mathrm{out}}}{c}.$$

The force here is this channel's contribution. Incoming drive momentum, supports and other exchanges must be added before calling it a net measured force. Mechanical transfer to a support, massive-counterpart exchange and a changed natural-orbit constraint need their own laws: $P/c$ is not a universal upper bound on all forces or all POAMS weight effects.

Collectivity at fixed power and directionality

Even granting the favorable equal-gap assumption,

$$E_{\mathrm{event}}=q\,h\nu_d,\qquad R=\frac{P_{\mathrm{out}}}{q\,h\nu_d} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \overline F_z=\eta\frac{P_{\mathrm{out}}}{c}.$$

$q$ counts actual released equal-gap steps; it is not automatically the domain population. Increasing $q$ at fixed exchanged power and $\eta$ increases each impulse and decreases its repetition rate in inverse proportion. It can improve a detector's ability to resolve an event, but cannot supply an extra factor in the mean momentum budget. Coherence could also change the actual directionality $\eta$; that would be a separately derived change in the angular distribution, not an additional domain multiplier.

The finite energy book must also close:

$$E_{\mathrm{out},T}+E_{\mathrm{other},T} =W_{\mathrm{in},T}+\mathcal B_{\mathrm{start}}-\mathcal B_{\mathrm{end}}.$$

All external energy inputs belong in $W_{\mathrm{in},T}$, and all other outputs in $E_{\mathrm{other},T}$. A transient can spend stored rate-energy. Repeated sustained operation must restore it: for a cycle returning to the same state, outward power in this channel cannot exceed total supplied power. Coherence is organization of that book, not an extra energy entry.

A concrete construction, with its boundary stated. Put a deliberately weak joining segment in each counter-wound superconducting loop. A driven loop can then leave one persistent-circulation branch through that segment and settle onto another. The calculation below supplies a loss-of-stability threshold, the participating phase redistribution, released energy, a reset cycle and a reciprocal load. It is a conditional effective material model, not a first-principles derivation of superconductivity from POAMS. The material's stiffness, joining law and relaxation are additional constitutive inputs. None is fitted here to a desired force.

Here a phase-slip event means a change between loop-circulation branches through the weak joining segment. It is not the smooth phase-advance rate sometimes called “slip” elsewhere in the corpus; nor is it automatically a change of a spatial ribbon's Călugăreanu linking number or of an elementary closure's spin sign.

The finite participation calculation

Use a finite set of actual phase relations along a ring. Let $\delta_a$ be a small mismatch on relation $a$, with a positive material cost $k_a\delta_a^2/2$. The joining coordinate $q$ and imposed relational phase bias $u$ require $\sum_a\delta_a=q-u$. Minimizing that finite sum gives

$$K=\left(\sum_a k_a^{-1}\right)^{-1},\qquad \delta_a=\frac{K}{k_a}(q-u),\qquad \mathcal B_{\mathrm{ring}}=\frac K2(q-u)^2.$$

This explicitly names participation: the phase redistribution is weighted by the links' compliances, not by an assumed count of all coherent closures. Once $K$ has been constructed from those entries, multiplying the answer by that count again would duplicate them. The quadratic local cost is the material's small-mismatch approximation, not a consequence of integer closure. These continuous phase coordinates describe the collective organization of the discrete turns; they do not introduce a continuous substance underneath them.

A joining law, and the condition for both cores to switch

A reciprocal $2\pi$-periodic joining cost can contain many harmonics. Choose the simplest explicitly specified case, $V(q)=E_b(1-\cos q)$, with $E_b>0$. Retaining only this first harmonic is an assumption about the weak link; periodicity alone does not select it. For two identical rings use the positive stiffness matrix

$$\mathbf K=K\begin{pmatrix}1&\zeta\\\zeta&1\end{pmatrix},\quad |\zeta|<1, \qquad \mathcal B=\frac12(\mathbf q-\mathbf u)^T\mathbf K(\mathbf q-\mathbf u) +E_b\sum_{i=A,B}(1-\cos q_i).$$

$\zeta$ is a signed material coupling, not a new universal constant. It has not been derived from the weak $G$-scaled kernel. Opposite controls and identical preparation admit $\mathbf u=(u,-u)$, $\mathbf q=(q,-q)$. Define $K_-=K(1-\zeta)$, $K_+=K(1+\zeta)$ and $b=E_b/K_-$. Then

$$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{opp}}=2K_-\,v(q,u),\qquad v(q,u)=\frac12(q-u)^2+b(1-\cos q).$$

The two local stability eigenvalues are $K_-+E_b\cos q$ for opposite phase changes and $K_++E_b\cos q$ for common phase changes. At the opposite-mode threshold, the other eigenvalue is $2K\zeta$. Thus $\zeta>0$ makes the opposite mode the first local instability; $\zeta=0$ gives two independent, degenerate switches; $\zeta<0$ allows a common-mode instability first. Phase agreement alone did not prove a collective event. This calculation supplies its local stability condition, under equal rings, opposite drive and the stated material matrix; it is not a proof of synchronization for a mismatched apparatus.

A stronger whole-transition condition. With equal positive damping, let $\delta=(q_A+q_B)/2$ measure departure from opposite motion and $w=(q_A-q_B)/2$. The full two-coordinate equations give $\gamma\dot\delta=-K_+\delta-E_b\cos w\sin\delta$. Since $|\sin\delta|\le|\delta|$, departures contract throughout the motion if

$$K_+>E_b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \zeta>\frac{b-1}{b+1},\qquad |\delta(t)|\le|\delta(0)|e^{-(K_+-E_b)t/\gamma}.$$

This is a sufficient, not necessary, condition for the identical opposed-drive model. It makes both cores' participation a calculated stable mode rather than an imposed domain multiplier. It still needs the material to supply that coupling and sufficiently matched controls. Merely having $\zeta>0$ guarantees the first local instability, not contraction throughout the whole switch.

Trigger: loss of a local minimum, not an integer crossing alone

The stationary branch and its stability follow from the same cost:

$$q-u+b\sin q=0,\qquad 1+b\cos q>0.$$

If $b\le1$, the opposed one-coordinate branch has no hysteretic fold. If $b>1$, increasing the drive along that branch near $q=0$ reaches the fold

$$q_c=\arccos(-1/b),\qquad u_\uparrow=q_c+b\sin q_c,\qquad u_\downarrow=2\pi-u_\uparrow.$$

The last expression is the reverse fold for the neighboring branch near $2\pi$. These are deterministic, quasistatic stability limits. Noise can switch earlier; a finite sweep can switch later. At the exact fold, a perfectly prepared noiseless state does not start moving by itself. The geometry supplies the loss of stability, not an arbitrary GHz event rate.

Energy and reset: a complete cycle

At fixed bias just beyond the forward fold, overdamped relaxation selects the first stable minimum to its right. In the zero-overdrive limit call this $q_+$, satisfying $q_+-u_\uparrow+b\sin q_+=0$. If no other work is extracted during this held-control relaxation, the heat delivered to the weak link's material thermal receiver is

$$Q_\uparrow=2K_-\,[v(q_c,u_\uparrow)-v(q_+,u_\uparrow)].$$

To make the recovery law explicit, assume a positive material phase-relaxation coefficient $\gamma$ (to be independently derived or measured) and write $\gamma\dot q=-K_-\partial_qv$. This is a reduced dissipative law with the mounting lattice/thermal receiver included in the energy account, not a disappearance of energy from the nexus. It gives

$$\frac{d\mathcal B_{\mathrm{opp}}}{dt} =-2\gamma\dot q^2-2K_-(q-u)\dot u,\qquad P_{\mathrm{drive}}=-2K_-(q-u)\dot u.$$

Reverse the drive past $u_\downarrow$ and the neighboring branch loses stability in turn. For a closed slow cycle with one forward and one reverse switch, the final state is the initial state and

$$W_{\mathrm{cycle}}=Q_\uparrow+Q_\downarrow. \qquad Q_\downarrow=Q_\uparrow\quad\text{for the symmetric example.}$$

Reset is therefore not free. If the drive is left inside the bistable interval, the switched ring stays latched rather than automatically returning. A new event requires a specified recovery/control cycle.

Why even the switching time needs a material law

The overdamped clock unit is $\gamma/K_-$. Near the forward fold set $y=q-q_c$, $\epsilon=u-u_\uparrow>0$ and $s=K_-t/\gamma$. To leading order, $dy/ds=\epsilon+\tfrac12\sqrt{b^2-1}\,y^2$. Passage through the full local bottleneck scales as $\pi\sqrt{2/(\sqrt{b^2-1}\,\epsilon)}$ in these units; starting exactly at its center gives half that leading passage time. The divergence as $\epsilon\to0$ is why an exact stability boundary is not a finite event period. Inertial dynamics, noise, heating and delayed recovery require their own material terms. The verification integrates a finite overdrive and independently counts its released heat.

The external counterpart must appear in that same cost

Let $R=z_D-z_C$ be the device's position relative to a separate drive/receiver circulation $C$, with both held at stated controls. If the material matrix and imposed phase relations are $\mathbf K(R)$ and $\mathbf u(R,t)$, and the joining cost has no additional explicit $R$ dependence, write $\mathbf x=\mathbf q-\mathbf u$. Its reciprocal interaction contribution is

$$F_D=-\frac12\mathbf x^T\mathbf K'(R)\mathbf x +\mathbf x^T\mathbf K(R)\,\partial_R\mathbf u, \qquad F_C=-F_D.$$

The derivatives are taken at fixed instantaneous phase state and the specified external controls. Drive-source and support terms must also be booked; a hidden ideal source cannot be treated as free. For constant $K_-$ and opposite phase bias $u(R)$, the branch-change contribution simplifies to

$$\Delta F_D=2K_-u'(R)(q_+-q_c),\qquad \Delta F_C=-\Delta F_D.$$

This derives the response law, not the missing physical function $u(R)$. The sign comes from the actual receiver geometry and coupling. If that positional coupling is absent, this contribution vanishes. An energy pulse by itself supplies neither $u'(R)$ nor a direction. An impulse additionally requires the time history, $I_D=\int F_D(t)\,dt$, including reset; the stationary step is not itself an impulse.

If the only external coupling retained is instead §5C's $G$-scaled angular book, a fast material switch merely changes its actual angular entries:

$$\Delta F_{D,z}=\frac{6GJ_C}{c^2} \left(\frac{\Delta J_A}{r_{AC}^4}+\frac{\Delta J_B}{r_{BC}^4}\right).$$

This expression assumes collinear signed angular entries and a receiver held above both cores. The material's map from $q_i$ to the physical $J_i$ is supplied for a calibrated case in §5E: a phase coordinate is not automatically a body's angular momentum. The factor $G/c^2$ does not disappear because switching is fast. Balanced opposite changes cancel at equal receiver distances and are further suppressed for a distant receiver. Stronger material drive/receiver coupling would be a different, explicitly characterized exchange channel, not an enhancement silently attached to this one.

What cannot be selected by the current POAMS premises

There is a direct non-uniqueness test. Multiply $K$ and $E_b$ together by any positive factor: the integer branches, $b$, and switching biases stay the same, while every released energy changes by that factor. Change $\gamma$ independently and the reset time changes. Change the receiver's position dependence and the load changes, or vanishes. Higher joining harmonics can change the threshold itself without violating $2\pi$ closure. The existing premises do not select among these cases.

Result. We have a worked switch, a conditional collective-instability criterion, a positive energy release, a paid reset cycle and an explicit reciprocal load law. We do not have an apparatus-specific, parameter-free POAMS derivation of their values. That requires the actual finite material stiffness/joining book, relaxation, phase-to-angular-momentum map and external coupling. Calling those supplied functions “derived” would put the missing physics into the assumptions.

A biased weak link can release energy stored by its drive when a small perturbation tips it across a threshold. That is a possible powered readout, not proof that the perturbation's own force has grown. Noise and stability determine whether it can resolve a small input. No weight-modification claim follows from the switch-and-reset calculation alone.

§5E — From a phase switch to a support reading

Three different quantities, now connected without identifying them. A joining-phase change is not itself an angular-momentum quantum. Local angular momentum is not itself an increment of orbital angular momentum about Earth. And a reciprocal load from a separate drive is not, by itself, a changed natural-orbit constraint.

Fix two identical, thin, planar circular rings of radius $a$, with centers at $z_D-d/2$ and $z_D+d/2$, and a common upward axis. Each has one weak joining segment; their controls and circulation states are opposite. The centerlines, mount and cross-section framing remain fixed in the chosen model. We continue §5D's material law and explicitly supply the additional rotational calibration. No microscopic spin re-filing is assumed. A real device that also changes its spin census or deforms its circulation needs those additional state variables.

1. The missing conversion is a response to actual rotation

Angular momentum is the quantity conjugate to a physical rotation, not to an arbitrary phase label. If $\Omega_i$ is a small mechanical rotation probe about ring $i$'s axis, the rotating-frame energy changes by $-\Omega_i J_i$. Suppose independent calibration shows that, to first order, this probe shifts the ring's bias by $\sigma_i\Omega_i$ while leaving the stiffness and joining cost unchanged. Then, with $\mathbf x=\mathbf q-\mathbf u$,

$$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{rot}}=\mathcal B(\mathbf q,\mathbf u+\boldsymbol\sigma\boldsymbol\Omega), \qquad J_i=-\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal B_{\mathrm{rot}}}{\partial\Omega_i}\right|_0 =\sigma_i[\mathbf K\mathbf x]_i.$$

Here $\boldsymbol\sigma$ is diagonal and $\sigma_i$ has units of seconds. Thus $J_i$ has units of J·s. By contrast, $\partial\mathcal B/\partial q_i$ has units of energy: calling it angular momentum would be a dimensional error. Other rotation-dependent material terms would add their own contributions; the bias-shift law is a stated constitutive case, not something integer closure determines.

For matched rings, $\sigma_A=\sigma_B=\sigma$ and $\mathbf x=(q-u,-q+u)$, so the complete endpoint map is

$$J_A=\sigma K_-(q-u),\quad J_B=-J_A, \qquad \Delta J_A=\sigma K_-\Delta q,\quad \Delta J_B=-\Delta J_A.$$

The last two expressions connect the relaxed endpoints of a switch at fixed drive bias. During a quasistatic ramp the full differential is $dJ_A=\sigma K_-(dq-du)$; omitting the $du$ term would omit the drive's share of the angular budget. A finite-time weak-link interruption can require additional transient circulation and mounting variables; this endpoint map does not calculate them. The calibration already includes the participating circulation. There is no additional whole-domain multiplier.

How circuit and rotation measurements can supply this calibration

For this particular flux-biased weak-link realization, use the conventional instrument labels $\boldsymbol\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}=s_0\mathbf u$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal L}=s_0^2\mathbf K^{-1}$. The phase-to-flux unit $s_0$ and the complete positive inductance matrix are calibrated inputs. Positive current is oriented about the common upward axis. The same fixed-source effective energy gives

$$\mathbf I=-\frac{\partial\mathcal B}{\partial\boldsymbol\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}} =\frac{\mathbf K(\mathbf q-\mathbf u)}{s_0},\qquad \mu_i=A I_i,\quad A=\pi a^2.$$

If an independently established, signed orbital gyromagnetic conversion is $\mu_i=\gamma_{\mathrm{orb}}J_i$, then $\sigma=A/(\gamma_{\mathrm{orb}}s_0)$. A current or moment measurement alone does not determine $J$ unless that conversion is known. The correspondence assumes the measured response is the transport circulation, with other spin, screening and mounting responses removed. In an inhomogeneous ring the spatial current distribution and rotational response must replace the single-area approximation.

Current, flux, inductance and magnetic moment here are laboratory calibration labels. They do not add an aether, a field-bearing void or an elementary charge-thing to the POAMS derivation. Nor does restating this familiar constitutive model make its coefficients POAMS predictions. No derivation of $\alpha$ is attempted. A change of winding-sector label is not a universal mechanical $\hbar$ jump.

3. Which angular momentum changed?

For a finite set of circulating entries about a ring center, $\mathbf J_{\mathrm{ring}}=\sum_a\boldsymbol\rho_a\times\mathbf p_a$ can be nonzero while $\sum_a\mathbf p_a=0$. This is local orbital circulation. It is not the intrinsic rotation of each constituent about its own axis.

A planar circle has geometric writhe $Wr=0$: in the writhe integral, the cross product of two tangents is perpendicular to the plane while their separation lies in the plane, so their scalar product vanishes. Choose a constant normal framing and its ribbon twist is also zero. Changing the amount or sense of circulation on that same fixed curve changes $J_{\mathrm{ring}}$ without changing either geometric invariant. These are the ring's explicitly named centerline and framing, not a claim that every microscopic circulation has zero linking. The joining phase $q$ has not supplied a microscopic ribbon frame.

$$\mathbf J_{\mathrm{about\ Earth}} =\mathbf R_D\times\mathbf P_D +\mathbf J_{\mathrm{local\ orbital}}+\mathbf S_{\mathrm{intrinsic}}.$$

At fixed device center and translational momentum, its Earth-orbital term $\mathbf R_D\times\mathbf P_D$ has not changed. Our chosen transport branch also leaves the intrinsic spin census fixed. Consequently we may not put $\Delta J_{\mathrm{ring}}$ straight into either the Earth-orbital numerator or the intrinsic-spin varying-$G$ term. A real exchange between those organizations needs its own derived coupling and reciprocal angular entry. Calling every orbital circulation “writhe” does not identify its reference orbit.

Each ring's angular change is balanced in the other ring, mounting body, drive or external receiver. If one ring exchanges only with its local mount, that mount receives angular impulse $-\Delta J_i$. An axial torque may move an individual support sensor; it is not automatically a change in the summed vertical force. The ideal matched pair has no net angular increment to store after either switch.

4. One complete support ledger, with the control convention fixed

Let $N$ denote the upward support force needed to hold the device center fixed, and let $\mathcal C_E$ be its Earth-relative constraint energy in a specified orbital chart. Include the material, independently supported drive/receiver and their appropriate fixed-control source work in one effective book:

$$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{tot}}=\mathcal C_E(z,\text{state}) +\mathcal B_{\mathrm{mat}}(\mathbf q,\mathbf u(z,t)) +V_C(\mathbf q,z), \qquad N=\left.\partial_z\mathcal B_{\mathrm{tot}}\right|_{\mathbf q,\ \mathrm{controls}}.$$

This is the fixed-center reaction; acceleration, elastic ringing and the instrument's transfer function require additional terms. On an actually stationary coupled branch, the envelope theorem permits this same partial derivative. Differentiating only a stored-energy fragment while leaving the drive source out gives the wrong force convention.

For the separate phase-bias receiver, constant stiffness and $\mathbf u_z=(k,-k)$ give

$$F_{D,z}^{\mathrm{drive}}=2K_-k(q-u),\quad F_{C,z}^{\mathrm{drive}}=-F_{D,z}^{\mathrm{drive}}, \qquad \Delta N_{\mathrm{drive}}=-2K_-k\Delta q.$$

For the illustrative ring, $\Delta N_{\mathrm{drive}}=-(2.37793\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm J)\,k$, with $k$ in m$^{-1}$. Positive $k$ gives an upward force increment on the device and a reduced required support force. This is a signed, dimensioned response per independently measured phase-position gradient, not a derived value of that gradient. If $k=0$, it vanishes. If the source and its reaction are both on the same balance, their internal contribution to the summed load cancels; any off-balance leads or counterpart remain external entries.

The weak $G$ receiver — and a derivative that must not be omitted

For a separate fixed axial circulation $J_C$ above both rings, keep the carried kernel $V_C=-(2GJ_C/c^2)\sum_i J_i/r_i^3$. If the bias has no positional dependence and the angular state is held fixed during the positional derivative,

$$\Delta F_{D,z}^{G}=\frac{6GJ_C}{c^2}\Delta J_A \left[\frac1{(R+d/2)^4}-\frac1{(R-d/2)^4}\right],\qquad \Delta N_G=-\Delta F_{D,z}^{G}.$$

In the example, $R=0.1$ m and an assumed $J_C=1$ J·s give $\Delta F_{D,z}^{G}=+5.75\times10^{-47}$ N. The receiver gets the opposite reaction. The assumed external circulation is not a demonstrated device. For $R\gg d$, the bracket is $-4d/R^5+O(d^3/R^7)$: balance introduces further cancellation, not amplification.

If the same displacement also changes the phase bias, $J_i(\mathbf q,\mathbf u(z))$ is not fixed even at fixed $\mathbf q$. The complete partial derivative instead includes

$$F_{D,z}^{G}=\frac{2GJ_C}{c^2}\sum_i \left[\frac{3J_i}{r_i^4}+\frac{\partial_z J_i}{r_i^3}\right],\qquad \partial_z J_i=-\sigma_i[\mathbf K\mathbf u_z]_i.$$

The drive contribution must be included too, and the stationary phases must solve the full coupled book if back-reaction is retained. Our verification checks this derivative both at fixed phase and along a re-equilibrated branch. The simple example above deliberately sets $\mathbf u_z=0$ for this separate $G$ channel; it does not silently add forces computed under incompatible controls.

5. Switch, dwell, reverse drive, reset

Take §5D's quasistatic lower-branch ramp from $u_\downarrow$ to $u_\uparrow$, forward switch, upper-branch return ramp, and reverse switch. With equal ramp durations and reflection-matched controls, $x=q-u$ on the second half is the negative of its value on the first. For a fixed phase-position gradient $k$,

$$\oint dJ_A=\oint dJ_B=0,\qquad \int_0^T F_{D,z}^{\mathrm{drive}}dt=0,\qquad W_{\mathrm{cycle}}=Q_\uparrow+Q_\downarrow>0.$$

The first result includes the ramps as well as the two jumps. The force cancellation uses the stated temporal symmetry; merely returning to the same state does not imply zero impulse when there is an external counterpart. Adding a dwell $T_h$ at the upper landing gives an extra $I_D=2K_-k(q_+-u_\uparrow)T_h$, with $I_C=-I_D$. Fixed supports balance those impulses. It is a reciprocal sustained interaction during the dwell, not stored thrust or an automatic weight change. A finite-time cycle or a back-reacting receiver must use its actual state history.

In a simple fixed-angular-momentum radial chart, $\mathcal C_E=-\mathcal G M_E m/r+L_E^2/(2mr^2)$ gives

This states the POAMS constraint bill in that chart; it is not permission to substitute a local ring circulation for $L_E$. Why a local switching calculation cannot determine the missing radial derivative

At the operating radius $r_0$, a term $A(r-r_0)\sum_i(1-\cos q_i)$ vanishes along with all its phase derivatives. Positive, zero or negative $A$ all leave that local data unchanged. This is a non-uniqueness witness, not an adopted force law. It shows exactly which information a new Earth-coupling derivation must provide; it is not a claim that such a derivation is impossible.

§5F — The Earth-relative constraint, worked through

The conditional relation is now explicit. The mixed angular-momentum energy already proposed in §5 can be completed into a held-on-Earth support calculation. It does not require identifying local circulation with orbital momentum. But the mixed interaction, and its application to local ring circulation, remain physical assumptions: the calculation below derives their consequences, not their necessity from closure.

For the fixed, horizontal, counter-wound rings of §5E, this leading mixed load change is zero. The local axes are perpendicular to the actual Earth-relative orbital angular momentum; independently, the matched ring changes sum to zero. Those are specific results for this construction and this channel, not an impossibility theorem for every POAMS device.

1. Name the Earth orbit and the participating circulation

Use a spherical Earth reference and one rigid device of total retained mass $M$, at radius $r$ and geographic latitude $\lambda$. Its mount maintains Earth's angular rate $\Omega_E$; latitude and orientation are held fixed during a radial displacement. Let $\hat z_E$ be Earth's rotation axis and $\hat n$ local north. Write $s=\cos\lambda$. The actual co-rotating orbit has, to zeroth order in the added coupling,

$$\vec L_{\mathrm{kin}}=M\Omega_Er^2 [\hat z_E-(\hat z_E\cdot\hat r)\hat r] =M\Omega_Er^2s\,\hat n.$$

This is not the freely orbiting reference $M\sqrt{GM_Er}$. It is also not generally parallel to Earth's rotation axis. A horizontal ring has its angular-momentum vector along $\hat r$, so its dot product with this orbital vector vanishes at every latitude.

Let $\vec J_\star$ denote the total internal angular momentum to which the mixed ansatz is assumed to apply, including any changing mount or onboard drive contribution. If that ansatz applies only to microscopic intrinsic spin, our spin-unchanged branch gives $\Delta\vec J_\star=0$ immediately. The more permissive body-level extension counts the calibrated local ring circulation from §5E. Work that extension through explicitly:

$$\vec J_\star(\mathbf q)= \sum_i\sigma_i[\mathbf K(\mathbf q-\mathbf u)]_i\hat n_i +\vec J_{\mathrm{mount/drive}},\qquad \Xi=\vec J_\star\cdot[\hat z_E-(\hat z_E\cdot\hat r)\hat r] =sJ_n.$$

$J_n=\vec J_\star\cdot\hat n$. This is an identification within the candidate interaction, not a claim that a current reversal changed the microscopic spin census or the ribbon's writhe. The rotation calibration is already included; another coherent-domain multiplier would count it twice.

2. Complete the mixed energy before differentiating it

Define $a_G=2G/c^2$, $\mu_E=GM_E$, and let $p$ be the canonical action conjugate to the device's azimuth about Earth. At a nonpolar latitude the orbital inertia is $I=M r^2s^2$. One explicit first-order Hamiltonian completion of §5 is

$$\mathcal H_E(r,p;\mathbf q)= \frac{p^2}{2Mr^2s^2}-\frac{\mu_EM}{r} -\frac{a_GJ_n(\mathbf q)p}{s r^3}.$$

At zero coupling, the full orbital AM is $(p/s)\hat n$, so the last term is exactly the carried dot-product energy. At nonzero coupling, $p$ must not silently be identified with the mechanical $Mr^2s^2\Omega_E$:

$$\Omega_E=\frac{\partial\mathcal H_E}{\partial p} =\frac pI-\frac{a_GJ_n}{s r^3},\qquad p_\star=I\left(\Omega_E+\frac{a_GJ_n}{s r^3}\right).$$

The Earth-locked mount maintains a rate, not a fixed orbital action. Its rotational work is included by using $\mathcal H_E-\Omega_Ep$ and eliminating $p$ at this stationary value. For the stipulated Hamiltonian the result is

$$\mathcal C_{E,\Omega}= -\frac{\mu_EM}{r} -\frac12Mr^2s^2\left(\Omega_E+\frac{a_GJ_n}{s r^3}\right)^2 =-\frac{\mu_EM}{r}-\frac12Mr^2s^2\Omega_E^2 -\frac{a_GM\Omega_E\Xi(\mathbf q)}{r} +O(a_G^2).$$

This Legendre transform is a change of constraint bookkeeping, not a new substance or a spacetime model. The displayed completion is exact algebra for the stipulated Hamiltonian; its square does not establish the unknown second-order physics of a first-order ansatz. Keep the prediction at the carried order. The local rotating-material book from §5E remains separate; its $-\boldsymbol\Omega_E\cdot\vec J$ response must be included once, not added a second time as another radial force.
