5 · The hazard chase

The proper-time relation changes the argument of a survival function; by itself it cannot choose the function. This experiment is the simplest Receipt-Local Dynamics case—one retained predictive state with one absorbing completion edge.

S(n+m)=S(n)S(m),   S(n)=qn=(1−p)n.

10 · Observable-modulus identifiability theorem

A finite hidden census does not automatically produce a recurrence, beat or sideband. Condition the surviving kernel as P=Q/(1−g/NA) and let f be any address-sensitive readout with zero mean under π. Its survived-turn correlation is

Cf(k)=π diag(f)Pkf.

For the uniform-renewal family in §9, every row of P is uniform. Therefore Pf=0 and Cf(k)=0 for every k≥1, for every N. The exact finite modulus is then silent outside the completion tariff: no recurrence or sideband is forced.

State-splitting no-go

Take any hidden-address model and replace each address by r observationally identical clones. Split every preparation and transition weight equally among the clones and give every clone the same readout response. The hidden census is multiplied by r, but every output sequence, survived correlation and completion record is unchanged. Hidden cardinality is therefore not identifiable from observations that fail to separate the clones.

A second observable is necessary but not sufficient. A beat can reveal an eigenmode of P; it does not by itself reveal the matrix dimension or prove that its period equals NA. Census closure requires an address-complete witness: N operationally distinguishable preparation/readout classes, a receipted action carrying each class to the next, closure after N applications, and failure of closure for every smaller positive divisor. The count and transition graph must be frozen before lifetime comparison.

11 · Finite-precision modulus no-go

A compatibility lattice cannot be obtained merely by measuring two frequencies and declaring their ratio rational. Every nonzero uncertainty interval contains irrational numbers and reduced rationals with arbitrarily large denominators. Finite phase or frequency precision therefore cannot prove an exact equivalence n∼n+N.

Rational reconstruction becomes unique only after an independent denominator ceiling Q is supplied. A sufficient separation condition is

|x̂−p/q| < 1/(2Q²),   q≤Q.

The ceiling is not delivered by the measured ratio; it is the missing finite-state physics in another guise. Using the lifetime-calibrated central target only to show scale, Q≈5.6129×1016 would require absolute ratio uncertainty below about 1.59×10−34 even to isolate one rational among denominators up to Q. No finite interval can exclude an irrational neighbor.

What survives. A ratio measurement can test a modulus already fixed by a discrete winding count, topological sector, explicit register or directly counted closure. It cannot create that modulus. Continued fractions are a search tool after the physical bound is earned, not a physical derivation of the bound.
