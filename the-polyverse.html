1 · The chalk circle and the missing instant

Philosophy first, because the philosophy decides what the physics is even about. Every Big-Bang narrative begins with the same quiet move: a boundary is drawn around “everything,” and the narrator steps outside it.

“Something which it is all too easy for a theoretical physicist or cosmologist to do is to draw, on a blackboard, say, a large circle or square to represent ‘the universe’. In this enclosed domain, the audience, like the theoretician, can fancy that they are some kind of ‘God’ who can see everything all over, everywhere within those chalk boundaries… To be truly a universe, that chalk boundary would have to include the man with the chalk, the audience, the room… in fact everything there is. But, of course, it doesn’t.”Pope, The Eye of the Beholder (2004), §VII — “The Naked Emperor”

Normal Realism refuses the chalk circle. What exists, observably, is not a container-object called “the universe” but a plurality of observational systems — each body booking its own turning, each observer at the centre of its own view, every pair of books reconciling where they meet. Pope’s later name for this is exact:

“…we are presented, not with a ‘universe’ but an indeterminate ‘pluriverse’, or ‘polyverse’ — what we might call a logos rather than a physis — which is a Whole of a very different kind.”Pope, Wondering in the Wilderness (2009)

Two refusals live here, and this page keeps them distinct because the sky treats them differently (§6):

  • The universe-object is refused. “The size of everything” is not a quantity; “everything is expanding” has no residual meaning (expanding into what? measured against what?). This is a grammar fact before it is a physics fact.
  • The God’s-eye instant is refused. There is no master simultaneity-slice on which “the whole universe, now” can be read. There are books, plural, reconciling pairwise wherever exchange happens — the GPS constellation is this fact shipped as engineering, closing to nanoseconds with no master clock anywhere in it.

What these refusals target in cosmology is precise: winding “the whole” back along one clock to one point. The wind-back needs the instant (one slice per tick) and the object (one size per slice) and the terminus (a first entry in a conserved ledger). 2 · The ledger has no zero

Now the physics. There is one substance — angular momentum, circulation, rotational time, $L = dS/d\theta$ — and it is conserved. Everything on this page follows from taking that seriously at both ends of the scale.

Nothing carrying finite angular momentum can reach $r = 0$. Circulation at zero radius would require an unbounded rate of turning: the books cannot be balanced at a point. This is the vortex’s oldest fact — the eye of the hurricane is empty, and its emptiness and its wall are the same entry read from two sides. A contracting circulation does not fall to its centre; it approaches a radial floor, spinning faster as it contracts (the skater’s arms), every step of the way paid for in the one currency. Pope & Osborne say it plainly of galaxies: bodies that radiate away energy — and therefore angular momentum — “simply get closer and closer to a lower radial limit” (Ch. 9). Not a sink. A wall.

A singularity claim, in either time direction, is therefore not an extreme physical claim but a bookkeeping impossibility dressed as one:

  • The black-hole singularity asks the substance to annihilate — finite circulation compressed to a point of infinite density, the whole ledger of a star written into a zero.
  • The Big-Bang singularity is the same zero run backwards — it asks the substance to begin, all the angular momentum of everything emerging from a point that has no capacity to hold any. A conserved substance does not begin. That is what “conserved” means.

The extant model knows where these zeros come from. The map ran off the table; that is not news about the territory.

The two refusals are one refusal. No zero in the books, forwards or backwards. Black-hole interiors and primeval peas are the same arithmetic error — dividing the one substance by zero radius — committed in opposite time directions, and both are placed, by construction, where no observation can ever audit them. “If the ‘Big Bang’ is accepted as a fact, then there is the even more bizarre question of what preceded it, to which of course, there can be no answer” (Ch. 9). 3 · ‘Black holes’: the empty barycentre

What is actually observed, at the centres of galaxies and in compact binaries, is this: matter in rapid orbit about centres that emit nothing. If the planets in our solar system were much larger than they are in comparison with our sun, the centre of rotation of the sun and planets might well lie outside the sun’s surface, somewhere in the void between the sun and all its planets. Observed from a great distance, it might seem that these bodies are rotating around an invisible centre of balance which, in itself, emits no radiation. To call this a ‘black hole’ may be picturesque but, unfortunately, lends itself to the sort of journalistic distortion that perverts the true course of science.”Pope, The Eye of the Beholder (2004), §VII.2

5.0 The scatter theorem — the kill that needs no thermometer

Before any microwave data, the mechanism fails on the shape of the redshift law itself. Write the booked shift for one galaxy moving randomly at speed $\beta$, angle $\theta$ to the sightline: $1+z = \gamma\,(1+\beta\cos\theta)$. Average over random directions and the first-order Doppler term cancels — the ensemble mean is the dilation shift $\gamma - 1 \approx \beta^2/2$, exactly as Ch. 9 §9.4 proposed. But the spread around that mean does not cancel: it is the first-order term itself, $\pm\beta$, both signs. The scatter-to-signal ratio is $2/\beta$ — greater than 2 at every depth, for any speed. A random-motion sky wears its velocities twice: once as a small second-order mean, once as a large first-order spread — and nearly half of all galaxies (every $\cos\theta < -\beta/2$) would arrive blueshifted. The observed sky: a redshift–distance law tight to a few percent, and essentially no blueshifts beyond the Local Group extant record, from Hubble’s own tables onward. Isotropy blocks the only exit — random orbits about a distributed barycentre still put $\langle v_r^2\rangle = v^2/3$ along every sightline; no arrangement makes everyone’s motion transverse to everyone’s view. The book’s “uniformly and without dispersion” answered band-broadening within one spectrum; the object-to-object scatter was never confronted, and it is fatal on its own. (The magnitude compounds it: the law extended to z ≈ 0.9 demands random speeds near $0.85c$, while every measured motion at those depths — line widths, kinematic SZ, the cold local flow — sits three to five orders of magnitude below.)

The strongest defence, pre-empted. “But time-dilation is direction-independent — the twins book the same slowing outbound and inbound — so shouldn’t all relative motion redden?” The direction-blindness is real and it belongs to $\gamma$ alone: the clock-rate booking, what a reunion compares. A spectrum is not a reunion. The observed shift is one booking with two factors — $1+z = \gamma(1+\beta\cos\theta)$ — and the first factor never erases the second: successive hand-offs from a closing separation book tighter arrival ordering (ledger geometry, no photon required), and that term carries a sign. The arithmetic is decisive: for head-on approach, $\gamma(1-\beta) = \sqrt{(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)} < 1$ at every speed — second order never beats first order; an approaching source is net blue at $\beta = 0.001$ and at $\beta = 0.99$ alike. The sky agrees wherever the test is available: Andromeda arrives blueshifted ($-300$ km/s); M86 in Virgo arrives blueshifted ($-244$ km/s) — blueshifts living exactly where virial speeds rival the still-small mean law, and vanishing at depth because peculiar speeds stay at a few hundred km/s while the law grows — the precise opposite of the $v \propto \sqrt{r}$ requirement; spectroscopic binaries swing their lines blue and red nightly in every observatory; and our own motion prints the floor’s dipole hotter ahead extant record throughout. So the mechanism’s mean was never the problem: averaged over random directions the first-order term cancels and the mean is the direction-blind $\gamma - 1$ — which is exactly why the class survived into its successor (§6). The sky, however, is measured per object, and each object wears its own $\pm\beta\cos\theta$ on top of the shared factor. A red mean cannot make every object red. Only shared accumulation can.
