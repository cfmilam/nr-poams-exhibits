2 · Do not confuse oscillation with completion

Completion kernel

A substochastic Q loses weight into absorbing classes. It describes termination and gives survival when πQ=qπ.

Oscillation kernel

A surviving preparation changes which terminal test it can satisfy while total retained weight remains available. Reversibility and phase memory matter.

An absorbing decay channel cannot be converted into a reversible label oscillation by renaming hidden addresses. Conversely, a reversible label change does not itself provide a completion probability at the detector.

3 · Two-state stochastic-kernel no-go

Let a surviving two-label Markov step be

P=[[1−a,a],[b,1−b]],   0≤a,b≤1.

Starting in label 1, the label-2 probability after n steps is

P1→2(n)=a/(a+b) · [1−(1−a−b)n].
  • If 0<a+b≤1, approach is monotone.
  • If 1<a+b<2, the labels alternate with a decaying envelope.
  • The only undamped nontrivial two-state case is a=b=1: a rigid every-step flip.

No continuous beat. A stochastic two-state kernel cannot generate a persistent sinusoid with continuously tunable phase. It relaxes, damp-alternates, or performs the trivial period-two permutation.

4 · Finite-state extension

For any finite stochastic P, observable sequences are sums of eigenmodes λrn. Modes with |λr|<1 decay. Unit-modulus eigenvalues of a finite nonnegative stochastic matrix belong to periodic communicating classes and are roots of unity. They yield exact discrete periods, not a continuously varying L/E phase.

Separation theorem. Persistent baseline-dependent oscillation requires additional phase-bearing structure beyond the classical address-renewal kernel: a deterministic continuous phase, coherent alternative-composition rule, or another explicitly reversible representation. Current POAMS bookkeeping does not force which one.
