Capture calculation

An autonomous Hamiltonian preserves phase-space volume and cannot make a resonant island an attractor. After a disc, tide, spin, impact, ring, or other reservoir supplies signed migration and damping, capture and survival frequencies are calculable over that registered history family. The eight-family audit registers published conditional calculations and preserves their incompatible priors rather than pooling them. The result is a network, not a ruler

The same closure grammar appears repeatedly, but it speaks different integers in different systems. An octave (2:1) is common because it is low order—not because every body lies on one powers-of-two line.

Pluto–Charon near-integer architecture

Styx · Nix · Kerberos · Hydra ≈ 3 · 4 · 5 · 6 Charon periods

The visual integer sequence is real, and published integrations identify a Styx–Nix–Hydra three-body resonance amid chaotic rotation. The individual near-integer ratios must not be promoted wholesale to pairwise locks.

Uranus + Mars nulls matter

no current universal low-order ladder

The five large Uranian moons do not form a present exact low-order chain; Phobos and Deimos are only near 4:1. A theory that turns every near-rational into music has no silence and therefore no test.

The asteroid belt: destructive closure written as absence

The belt extends the test from the phases of surviving bodies to the negative space of a population. “Solar destructive resonance” is close, but the operative phase partners are principally Jupiter and Saturn. The Sun supplies the central orbit; the planetary closures decide which heliocentric paths persist.

Clearing channels 4/4 forward-width gates pass

3:1 · 5:2 · 7:3 · 2:1 centres within 0.014%

At e=0.15, the kernel predicts half-widths 0.01436, 0.00917, 0.00535, and 0.07848 AU. JPL population gaps differ by factors 2.09, 1.64, 2.14, and 1.36—all inside the preregistered factor-three gate. An independent 1.25-million-body proper-orbit catalog puts 7:3 only factor 1.07 away. The ν6 secular channel separately helps define the inner edge. “Destructive” means loss from the belt, not destruction of matter.

Hilda control resonance can preserve

Jupiter 3:2 at 3.9717 AU → concentration, not gap

Numbered H≤15 asteroids in the registered Hilda core are 339 times denser per AU than equal-width sidebands. Integer closure alone therefore does not predict clearing. Coefficient geometry, overlap, and access to a crossing boundary decide storage or removal; the calculated Mars-crossing thresholds for the four scored gaps run from e=0.334 to 0.492.

The belt is also a migration recorder

Present-day perturbations account for the sharp resonant gaps, but not the full depletion pattern. Large-asteroid deficits just outside the 5:2, 7:3, and 2:1 gaps are reproduced by resonances sweeping across the belt while Jupiter and Saturn migrated. Published instability simulations can remove roughly 90% of a chosen primordial belt, but that percentage is model-conditioned. The robust finding is the spatially registered scar: changing giant-planet rates moved destructive channels through the belt.

What history survives in the published record?

Quite a lot—but at different grades. Astrometry measures present angular-momentum transfer; resonant angles, thermal states, surfaces, and neighboring populations constrain prior passages; formation simulations supply candidate reservoir paths. Together they define historical ensembles on which capture probabilities can be computed. They do not erase chaotic branching or model dependence.

The earned claim

The solar system is structured where coupled orbital phases close in integer combinations. POAMS supplies the whole-turn bookkeeping constraint; imported resonance dynamics supplies the tested phase-angle form. Which channels preserve, clear, or fail to bind is a dynamical and empirical question. The measured sky contains low-order pair locks, irreducible three-body locks, a high-order inclination lock, near-commensurabilities, clean non-locks, stable resonant populations, and destructive gaps. That full distribution—not a perfect regression—is the evidence.
