Orbital Time Dilation

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian “Viv” Pope & Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

One Paper, Worked Live

Philosophy first: a body in orbit is not being steered by a force through a void — it rides the natural path its angular momentum assigns, and its clock reads the Pythagorean time book along that path (the Cone Model). This page works Osborne & Pope's paper "Orbital Time Dilation" as five live panels in the paper's own order: the circular-orbit clock and its factor of three (Eqs 2.8, 2.9, 3.6) · the paper's natural-path matching condition for the three (Eq 3.5, a = 2) · the general ellipse where the dilation varies point by point (Eq 2.12) · the cubic term that advances a modeled bound orbit's axis (Eq 4.6, compared with Mercury's 43″ record) · and the engineering receipt: the GPS constellation, which pays these equations every day. 1 · The circular orbit and the factor of three — Eqs 2.8, 2.9, 3.6

2 · Why three: the natural-path condition — Eq 3.5, a = 2

The paper fixes its constant $a$ by matching its natural-path speed relation to its circular-orbit speed relation. Slide $a$ off 2 and the two stated equations no longer agree. At $a=2$, the adopted coefficient $1+a$ is 3: one part relative rate and two parts assigned by the paper to closed-path geometry. This panel checks that algebra; it does not independently derive Eq 3.5 or simulate off-match dynamics.

In the source paper the constant a is fixed by requiring that circular orbits be “geodesics” — the source's stage language. Read natively: the circular orbit is the force-free path, and carrying the time-cone around that closed path adds the path's own turning to the velocity term. The paper further places the Thomas, geodetic, and perihelion terms in the ratio \(1:3:6\) (\(\pi : 3\pi : 6\pi\)); that broader relation is a source-paper claim, not a result derived by this matching panel. Given Eqs 3.5 and 2.8, equality requires a = 2, and the adopted total \(1+a=3\).

About This Exhibit

POAMS (the Pope–Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis) reads the source paper's cubic term as a consequence of orbital time bookkeeping.

This page starts from the printed first integral, differentiates it to obtain $u''+u=\mathcal{M}c^2/\beta^2+3\mathcal{M}u^2$, and integrates the normalized form. It does not re-derive the first integral in this panel.

Move illustrative $\epsilon$ to zero for the closed baseline and increase it to inspect the equation's advancing axis. The Mercury line is computed separately at physical scale; $\epsilon=0.0100$ is roughly $3.8\times10^5$ times larger and is used only for visibility.

5 · The receipt: a simplified GPS clock budget

standard inputs · computed outputs Using the stated spherical-Earth constants, the leading clock budget is +45.72 μs/day from altitude and −7.21 μs/day from orbital motion, for +38.51 μs/day net. This panel is a simplified calculation, not the full operational GPS correction model.

Explanation

GPS operation requires clock-rate correction. This panel evaluates the paper's circular-orbit rate and a stationary ground reference using the constants printed in the code. POAMS supplies the page's ledger interpretation; the numerical comparison by itself does not choose an ontology.

Eq 2.9 (velocity only):   $\tau = \sqrt{1 - \frac{GM}{rc^2}}\; t$

Eq 3.6 (full orbital):   $\tau = \sqrt{1 - \frac{3GM}{rc^2}}\; t$

Given the page's adopted circular relation $v^2=GM/r$, the motion debit contributes one $GM/rc^2$ term while the altitude comparison contributes the other two. The component ledger is computed independently below and asserted to close to the net.
