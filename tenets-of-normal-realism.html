Normal Realism — The Tenets

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian “Viv” Pope & Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

The philosophy beneath every exhibit on this site — a reader's gate to sixty years of work, and to why it grants what the extant scientific enterprise cannot: a world in which your choices genuinely make the future.

Every exhibit in this series is physics — orbits, spectra, clocks, the periodic table, mass itself, all derived from one substance keeping its books. But the physics rests on a philosophy, and the philosophy came first, developed by Viv Pope over sixty years across seven major works. This page is the synopsis those works never had: the tenets stated plainly, each with a pointer into the book where Pope argues it in full. It cannot replace the books — in The Eye of the Beholder and Einstein's Lost Legacy every paragraph earns its place — but it can hand you the map before you walk the country.

1 · A lineman writes to Einstein

In March 1954, a seventy-five-year-old Albert Einstein, at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, answered a letter from a twenty-three-year-old telephone lineman working the hills of Wales. The lineman, Viv Pope, had asked about the "clock paradox" — the claim that relativity makes each of two separated twins end up younger than the other. Einstein's reply was short and exact:

"Your question concerns the wellknown 'clock-paradoxon' and is very well formulated. The answer is: Two observers B1 and B2, each at rest in an inertial system, can only encounter each other once. To make possible a second encounter one of the two observers… has to reverse his speed… This reversal distinguishes observer B1 in comparison to observer B2… This is the cause of the dissymmetry of the result with respect to the two observers." — Albert Einstein to N. V. Pope, 21 March 1954 (Swansea County Archives, ref. D/D NVP/1-17)

Einstein died the following year and the correspondence ended. But in grappling with that letter, Pope became convinced of two things: that there is no clock paradox — and that Einstein's second postulate, "light travels at a constant speed c in vacuo," is logically redundant. (There was a second letter, too: around the same time the young Pope independently conceived what J. W. Dunne had called serial time — time as a hierarchy of orthogonal dimensions — and wrote to Einstein again. No answer ever came; Einstein was already in his final illness. That unanswered thread waited fifty years, and resurfaces in §5 below.) Everything relativity actually delivers can be had without it. Pulling on that single thread, over the next six decades — later in mathematical collaboration with Dr. Anthony Osborne of Keele University — unravelled the whole fabric of field-forces, travelling photons, wave-particles, Big Bangs and dark matter, and rewove it into something radically simpler. Hermann Bondi reached the same reading of c independently and corresponded with Pope about it; Bondi took it as a teaching aid, and left it to Pope, with his blessing, to develop what it meant for philosophy. What it meant is Normal Realism.

The name is deliberate. Normal Realism claims to be nothing more than the default position of any sane, sensible observer not bedazzled by a theoretical belief system — the realism of ordinary perception, held to with uncommon consistency. Its heresy is only that it refuses to abandon that position when the textbooks demand it.

2 · The tenets

Stated as ten commitments. Each is argued, not asserted, in the works cited beneath it — the arguments are Pope's (and, in the mathematics, Pope & Osborne's); the compression is ours.

Tenet I

There is one nature, and science is the reading of it

The Eye of the Beholder opens: "There are many sciences and scientific theories but there is only one nature." The most basic definition of science that is possible is the rational interpretation of observational and instrumental information — and the ultimate reader of that information is ourselves, in communication with others like ourselves. Science is therefore relativistic at its root: whatever it describes is described and measured relative to observation. There is no "God's-eye view" from which nature is seen whole and at once; any physics that tacitly assumes one has left science for metaphysics.

Read: Eye of the Beholder, ch. I · Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 1 & 5
Tenet II

The information is discrete — and must be

For information to be information, its ultimate elements have to be discrete and unconnected. If laws determined which letter followed which, every sentence would be the same sentence and there would be no news. This is not an empirical accident confirmed by Planck — it is a logical requirement that Planck's quantum h happened to satisfy. The ultimate element of nature is the quantum of action: a whole, irreducible, probabilistic packet of energy-times-time that is simultaneously the ultimate bit. Reality is a discretum, not a continuum; continuity is only how many discrete events look from far away, as a movie's still frames read as motion. As CERN's founding director Weisskopf conceded to Pope: no experiment anywhere has ever succeeded in "splitting h."

Read: Eye of the Beholder, chs. I–II · Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 3–4 · Light-Speed, Gravitation and Quantum Instantaneity, ch. 5
Tenet III

c is not a speed

What Römer measured in 1676 was a constant ratio between distance-measure and time-measure: one second for every 300,000 kilometres, in any unmediated observational direction. All velocities are distances divided by times, but not all distances divided by times are velocities — "I can divide the length of my lawn by the time since the Kennedy assassination without that signifying any kind of velocity." Nothing is ever seen to travel: no one has observed light leave an object, cross a void, and arrive. Bondi said it exactly:

"Any attempt to measure the velocity of light is… not an attempt at measuring the velocity of light but an attempt at ascertaining the length of the standard metre in Paris in terms of time-units." — Hermann Bondi, Assumption and Myth in Physical Theory (1965)

So c is a conversion factor — like pounds to kilograms — telling us that observational distance and time were never two measures but one: distance-time. Lost Legacy ch. 6 gives ten independent logical proofs that c cannot be a velocity of anything, among them: a "photon," as one irreducible quantum, has no energy to spare to manifest itself anywhere between source and sink, and the one "speed" that is identical for all relatively moving observers is, by that very fact, not a speed.

Read: Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 3 & 6 · Eye of the Beholder, ch. I
Tenet IV

Space is in the light, not light in space

The full Copernican inversion. Light — the whole spectral range, from below infrared to beyond gamma — is not a travelling intermediary between things; it is the information itself, the matrix out of which every distance, duration, mass and motion is observationally projected, the way a viewer projects the depth of a video scenario out of patterns of flat screen pixels. A quantum transaction is proper-time-instantaneous: its two "ends" are one event, common to object and observer. The time-delay s/c lives not in the quantum but in the sequences of quanta — the "cinematic model": stills instantaneous, action time-delayed, no contradiction. This dissolves the EPR "paradox" outright: quantum immediacy and relativistic delay are complementary faces of the same time-formula, never rivals. And the felt connections are the same as the seen — Mach: "When the subway train jerks it is the fixed stars that throw you down."

Read: Eye of the Beholder, ch. VI · Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 4–5, 9 · Immediate Distant Action and Correlation in Modern Physics (the dedicated volume)
Tenet V

Relativity is Pythagoras — no light-speed axiom required

Draw a right triangle whose legs are the proper time t of a moving body and its distance-as-time s/c; the hypotenuse is the observer's time. School geometry then yields tR = t/√(1 − v²/c²) — Einstein's time dilation, derived with no second postulate, no ether history, no field theory (Pope & Osborne, Int. J. Math. Educ. Sci. Technol., 1987). Had Pythagoras thought to add a fourth dimension in the same units, he could have anticipated Einstein by three thousand years. The twins "paradox" dissolves exactly as Einstein's letter said — the reversal breaks the symmetry — and the same formula, with quantum-integer time-steps, yields the Balmer–Rydberg spectrum (Tenet VII); with orbital substitution it yields orbital time dilation as verified by GPS and Pound–Rebka. One formula; relativity and the quantum as two of its readings.

Read: Einstein's Lost Legacy, ch. 7 & App. 1 · Eye of the Beholder, chs. II & V · exhibits: Circular-Orbit Clocks, GPS Clocks, Orbital Time Dilation
Tenet VI

There is no gravity-force; angular momentum suffices

Newton gave two laws that cancel: a First Law decreeing force-free motion is straight, and a "universal gravitation" explaining why nothing ever moves that way. Aristotle had it right before either: uninterrupted natural motion is cyclic. All free motion is orbital; straight-line momentum is the ideal special case of an orbit of infinite radius. The single equation of orbital angular momentum, mvr = GmM/v, is sufficient by itself to give every parameter of free orbits — no pull crossing a void. Nothing is ever attracted; bodies simply ride the orbits their angular momentum assigns them, and the only real force is whatever prevents that — the twig holding the apple, the ground pressing up on your feet. Weight is the bill for standing off your natural orbit. That is why free fall feels like nothing.

Read: Eye of the Beholder, ch. III · Einstein's Lost Legacy, ch. 8 & App. 4 · exhibits: The Loop of Momentum, The Geodesic Proof, Angular-Momentum Calculator
Tenet VII

The atom turned inside-out

Democritus solved the Eleatic gridlock by putting substance inside the atom and void outside. Normal Realism takes the inverse, equally logical option he missed: void inside, substance outside. The atom is not a solid grain but a quantum accumulator — an irreducible angular-momentum system of paired, balanced masses about an empty barycentre, storing energy in onion-layered integer levels. "Charge" is not a thing: the coulombs cash out as the kinetic energy of spin in joules (after Uhlenbeck & Goudsmit), and the alleged separate "electric force" is the same restraint-against-natural-orbit as weight, with G promoted to a variable wherever spin dominates orbit. The spectral bar-code of Balmer and Rydberg falls out of the quantised time formula with no electrodynamics at all. "Atom-smashing" is a category mistake — the exotic "particles" are harmonics struck from angular momentum, bell-tones, not fragments of kernels.

Read: Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 9–10, Apps. 3 & 5 · Eye of the Beholder, ch. IV · exhibits: The Atom as Organized Phase, Balmer–Rydberg, Aufbau as Closure, The Periodic Table
Tenet VIII

Existence lives in language — guard the categories

"To exist" is not a metaphysical something-we-know-not-what; it is a function of language, bestowed on a subject by the verb to be — and there is more than one category of it. The Cheshire Cat exists in fiction; electrons exist in theory; ducks exist in the barnyard. Physics goes mad precisely where it conflates categories: calling an interaction a "particle," a barycentric void a "Black Hole," a probability function a travelling "wave," quantum-level talk and scenario-level talk thrown into one sentence. The repair tool is linguistic analysis plus Ockham's razor: every concept that cannot be cashed out in observation or coherent ordinary language is cut. Pope: since nature has no guile to spin theories of itself, the less theory in our interpretations, the closer we stand to nature as it is.

Read: Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 2 & 5, App. 7 · Wondering in the Wilderness (the collected shorter pieces)
Tenet IX

No universe — a polyverse

There is no single overall space, no single overall time, no universal "now" advancing like a cosmic guillotine. Time evolves at points — each observer, each atom, its own interactive present — a phalanx of thrusting spears, not one blade. Conservation is universal in the sense of holding in every case, never in the sense of everywhere at once: quantum connections are intransitive, so there is no Eleatic gridlock and no cosmic simultaneity. What the theoreticians draw on the blackboard and call "the universe" never includes the man with the chalk. From this one clearing follow the demolitions: the Hubble redshift is a time-dilation reading, not a recession — so no expansion, no "Big Bang," no "age of the universe"; "Black Holes" are the massless barycentres every angular-momentum system has, from bathtub vortex to galaxy; "dark matter" is the receipt for ignoring the spin term in the orbital equations. Every epicycle is the price of insisting on a force where there is only bookkeeping.

Read: Eye of the Beholder, chs. VII–VIII · Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 10–12 · exhibits: The Pioneer Anomaly, Census Flow
Tenet X

Mind matters — the sciences and the humanities are one subject

Dissolving Descartes' split does not demote matter to dream; it promotes mind to nature. In an information-based physics, "mind, in the sense of informational recognition and communication, is the most basic, most undeniable fact of nature" — not a Ghost in the Machine, because there is no Machine. Causality is strictly a macro-level, statistical affair; single quanta cause nothing. And the statistical laws are end-directed: entropy names one end-state on a sliding scale of probabilities, and life works the other end — negentropy, the leaf turning sunlight uphill, the hound computing the hare's futures. Aristotle's entelechies return as respectable physics: future states, held as probabilities, direct present ones. There is no chalk line in nature between physics and biology, between mechanism and intention — only positions on one scale. Deliberation, volition and responsibility are real, irreducible, and physical.

Read: Einstein's Lost Legacy, chs. 12.7–13 · Eye of the Beholder, chs. VIII–IX · exhibits: Computation as Completion, Memory, Dunne & Serialism

One sentence, if you must have it: Nature is one substance — angular momentum, which is organized time — read directly by a community of finite observers out of discrete quanta of information; everything else in physics is that reading's bookkeeping, and everything wrong in physics is a force, particle or container invented where bookkeeping was enough.

3 · Where the philosophy becomes physics

POAMS is what happens when the tenets are made to calculate. The one substance is angular momentum — the indissoluble union of mass, length and time, quantised in units of ℏ, observed directly in every orbit from electron to galaxy. On this site the derivations run as a connected series: the Cone Model builds observer space-time; the Loop of Momentum and Geodesic Proof retire the gravity-force; Balmer–Rydberg, the Atom as Organized Phase and Aufbau as Closure rebuild the atom and the periodic table; Mass as Trapped Rate and the Mass Ledger cash mass itself out as trapped turning; the α derivation, perihelion precession and GPS clocks take the same ledger to the tests. Every one of them passes through the same touchstone: if a result cannot be cashed out as circulating angular momentum keeping its books, it has floated off the real world — no matter how elegant.

5 · Theology, free will, and the making of the future

Pope never treated this as an appendix to the physics. In The Eye of the Beholder the "Theological and Social Implications" are a full chapter, and Lost Legacy closes on the "Humanitarian Implications" — because the deepest failure of the extant enterprise, in his reading, is not a wrong equation but a wrong ledger of meaning. What follows is his argument, compressed.

What the machine-world took away

A science whose ultimate reality is mindless matter in a void has no room for anything a life is made of. Democritus dismissed all of it as "convention"; Descartes exiled it to a ghost-realm; and every society built on that physics has inherited the same split — "'atheistic' Science on the one hand and Religion on the other, and never the twain shall meet." In a machine there can be no responsibility, hence no morality, hence no meaning: human values become mayfly-ephemera, and the culture knows it. Pope saw the wreckage plainly — the escapism, the nihilism, the young unable to find any rational compass-direction for their lives — and traced it to the physics: a society whose official picture of reality has no place for persons will not indefinitely produce people who believe their lives matter. The race away from spirituality was never forced by the evidence. It was forced by a seventeenth-century category mistake.

What an information-world gives back

Reverse the mistake — matter as a projection from information rather than information as an accident of matter — and the standing of every humane thing changes at once:

  • Truth is objective. "There is no truth, only opinion" refutes itself the moment it is asserted as true. We need not possess The Truth to know it exists: the very possibility of discovering error proves a reality that outruns convention.
  • Values are objective the way towers are. Every observer's perspective of the distant tower differs, yet the tower's height is a fact the community of perspectives converges on. Goodness, justice, beauty admit the same perspectival objectivity — ideals no society invented and no society can survive abandoning, criteria that judge institutions from beyond them. That is what superhuman literally means; and history's Socrates and Brunos died for exactly that surplus.
  • Faith is not optional — it is structural. Every perception beyond the present instant involves belief; to trust that there is something around the next corner is already faith. And Pope argues we are equally committed to "a residual class of intelligence in nature… superior not only to our own but to that of mankind as a whole" — knowable in existence, unknowable in nature. Reason cannot produce that intelligence, but it is reason that keeps belief in it from curdling into dogma: there are truths beyond logic, but never contrary to it.
  • Creation and evolution never contradicted each other. The cook creates the cake by intention and then entrusts it to the eminently random processes of the oven; the farmer creates the conditions and consigns the seed to the weather. Purpose sets the premium; randomness runs the mechanism; the outcome is both created and evolved. What is incoherent is not a creator but the alternative: life from pure chance in a world with nothing to recognise, value or keep a "success" — monkeys at typewriters with no one to read.
  • Prayer is no longer a category error. To an information-transacting nature, communication is not alien but fundamental. "It makes more sense, surely, to worship and pray to an information-transacting deity than to some cosmic automaton." Pope named nothing — he called the students' bluff both ways (describe to me the God you don't believe in) — but he demonstrated that an information-based science cannot be used as a basis for atheism, and quoted Voltaire for where any God must live: present everywhere where there is something — never enthroned in imaginary spaces. Pressed in private, he went further: "If hard pressed, I would have to admit I am a believer, and a devout one at that. But as soon as I try to rationalise that belief, I am stuck."

Free will: the physics of the open future

Here the tenets pay their deepest dividend. In the machine-world the future is a fixed reel already printed — "the first morning of Creation wrote what the last day of reckoning shall read." Normal Realism's future is neither that nor chaos. The holistic laws — conservation, entropy — govern probabilities, and probabilities form a live scale on which the future exists definitely but indeterminately: a field of weighted possibilities that genuinely directs the present (the hound reads the hare's futures; the seed holds the tree's), while leaving room that mechanism never had:

"In the overall angular momentum nexus there is nothing predetermined or pre-ordained. There is always room for actions which change the probabilities… signifying the freedom for any agent to 'change the world' by deliberation or design." — Einstein's Lost Legacy, ch. 8

And because there is no universal now — because time evolves at points, each observer the growing tip of its own world-line — there is no already-written whole for your choices to be an illusion inside. Each present moment is a place where the books of the world are still being written. Pope noticed how much this frightens us:

"We like to think that behind all our finite dealings with the world there is an absolutely permanent reality that is 'in God's hands' and which we can do nothing about. It scares us to think that much of the carving-out of the course of history that relativity entails may be in our hands. To be given that power of intervention places upon us, we may feel, far too great a burden of responsibility." — The Eye of the Beholder, ch. VI

The dread is the tell. The traditional comfort — a finished universe, a spectator God, a fate — was always also an alibi. Normal Realism cancels the alibi.

What each life is for

Put the pieces in one place. You are not a late accident in a dead machine, watching a reel that was printed before you were born. You are one of the points at which time actually advances — an encoder and decoder in the polyverse, a place where information becomes deliberation and deliberation becomes physical fact. The soldier at the field-gun deciding whether the tower stands; the mother creating the conditions of the cake; the heretic refusing the convention that would have kept the Earth flat — these are not metaphors of agency but instances of physics: choices selecting among the weighted futures, entering the conserved books of the world, co-signed and irreversible. Whatever has happened cannot be caused to unhappen — which means every genuine choice is a permanent contribution to what the world is. History, Pope wrote in his memoir, "can be nothing without" the actions of individuals forged by their own experience.

That is the meaning the extant enterprise could not supply and the tenets restore: not consolation bolted onto physics, but a consequence derived from it. A cosmology of mindless matter made every life a spectator's; a cosmology of information makes every life an author's. The future is real, open, and partly yours — and so, therefore, is the responsibility. Where there's life, there's hope; and where there's deliberation, there is a hand on the pen.

Six years at the edge: the Pope–Gower correspondence

Between 2005 and 2010, Viv Pope and Penny Gower — the NRIG organiser thanked in Eye of the Beholder's acknowledgements, a believer whose faith had nearly been destroyed by mechanistic science itself — conducted a 72,000-word email correspondence, with a contribution from Alison Pope, pressing Normal Realism as far into theology as honesty would let it go. It was never a conversion effort in either direction. It is the tenets under maximum load, and it produced arguments found nowhere in the published books:

  • Theology by elimination. A Normal Realist theology asks not what God is — every answer shrinks the Object into a tribal effigy — but what, logically, God cannot be, and holds "a deep and reverent silence in the face of such an Object of Contemplation." Yet the silence is not emptiness. What we know for certain is that whatever we know is never all there is: "that implies an absolutely undeniable remnant of knowledge that is forever unknown. And that is the most solid basis for a 'religion' that there can possibly be."
  • The past cannot be unhappened — and that is a theological fact. If the Sun exploded tomorrow and every record was gasified, would that unhappen what has happened here? No one can hold that it would. But then everything that has ever been done persists as real when every mundane trace of it is gone — which requires a dimension of reality beyond the ordinary ones, relative to something superhuman. Nihilism is not an option: to say that in reality nothing is real is a flat contradiction. "Given that we have once existed, nothing can possibly make us never to have existed." And on that ledger hangs conscience itself: if nothing we did could ever be evaluated, psychopathy would be the natural norm — the fact that it is not is the oldest evidence we have that the books are real and are kept.
  • The open door. The dimensional analysis — the serial time of that unanswered second letter to Einstein — ends not in proof but in a rigorously honest verdict: survival of death cannot be established, and cannot be logically excluded. Belief in it "is not necessarily nonsense" — which, from the strictest logician in Wales, was not a small concession. His summary of a lifetime's method: there are truths beyond reason, but never contrary to it.
  • Co-creating the Logos. Alison Pope drew the consequence that closes the circle of this whole exhibit: if the Logos is genuinely becoming — its future undetermined, awaiting the choices of the agents within it — "does it not follow that we are each responsible in part for what this God/Logos becomes?" Viv did not retreat from it. The free agent does not merely make the future; the future being made is the growing edge of the Logos. There is no stronger statement anywhere in the corpus of what an individual life is for.
  • The testimony. Penny, from the believing side: "If anything chipped away at belief for me, it was mechanistic science… there was a period in my life when I was hanging on by my fingertips. It was you who finally helped me to see that my problem should never really have been a problem — that mechanistic science was at odds with the physical world, let alone religious belief." The race away from spirituality, witnessed from inside one life — and reversed there by the tenets.
  • The vocation. And Viv, from the questioning side, finally set down the day the work began: a young lineman, wronged and raging, wandering into a small grey stone church by the sea on Gower; the stillness; and a sub-linguistic resolve — "I am here, with such talents as I have, to search for philosophical truth and reality and to carry it through to wherever it leads" — followed, through an improbable cascade of injustices and coincidences, by precisely the life that could keep that pact. He weighed the word "miracle" against that record and declined to rule. At eighty he closed the ledger with the 23rd Psalm — "I don't know how I could express it better in modern-day language" — and with this: "My life has been a test… and I'm glad that, so far at least, I have not been tested to destruction."

The correspondence survives in the POAMS source archive (Theological Discussion, with Penny's Response to "The Biggest Question" and the two Refutation of Solipsism letters) — unpublished, and as essential to the ending of this story as any of the books above.

The workshop's corollary. The exhibits on this site carry Pope's argument one ratified step further: exchanges are two-ended and co-signed, so no exchange happens within either endpoint — an observer is not a vessel holding its experiences but the census its closures maintain. You happen at the exchange. Seat, not container, from pebble to human to whatever reads this after us. That corollary is ours, not Pope's — stated here so the provenance stays honest — but it is the same inversion he taught: look for the substance in the relation, not the relic. 6 · Coda — the boy on the hill

In 1941, a Swansea evacuee sat on a milk-churn stand on a Carmarthenshire hilltop his family called God's country, looking at a distance he could not explain, wondering what light was, why it was coloured, what held him to the earth, and why he was here at all. The bombs that had driven him there were the machine-world at its most articulate. A dozen years later, the same boy — now a lineman with oil in his eye and an injustice in his chest — walked into a small grey church by the Gower sea and made the wordless pact that decided the rest. The questions outlived the bombs by seventy years and became the only modern physics in which the boy, the wondering, and the hilltop are all fully real — and in which what he chose to do about it changed, permanently, the books of the world. They are still open. So are yours.
