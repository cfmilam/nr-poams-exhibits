The rule. The empirical binding curve is famous — no registration can feign ignorance of the iron peak. So the discipline here is derivational: structure and coefficient ratios must be forced by ledger bookkeeping before any comparison; every free constant is a named import; fitting is the only sin. The classical five-term formula (comparative shadow: von Weizsäcker 1935) is cited throughout as the fitted silhouette of these entries — the claim of this page is the bookkeeping derivation, never the form's novelty.

Rates, not speeds (GROUNDING discipline): every quantity below is a count, a rate of angular change, or an energy of trapped rate. Nothing moves through a void; nothing pulls. The dense packing is contact of circulations, and the ledger only ever records meshing, filing, and strain.

Units. MeV — the mega-electronvolt, nuclear physics' conventional energy unit ($10^6$ electron-volts $\approx 1.602\times10^{-13}$ J); all measured masses below come from AME2020, which tabulates in keV. 3 · The second column: every quantum pays a localization tax

A one-column ledger dies on the three cleanest data. Per bond, the net discounts of the lightest packings run 2.22 → 2.83 → 4.72 MeV (labels: deuteron, triton, alpha) — spreading 2.1× and non-monotone in coordination: impossible for any single per-contact discount. The founding entity of the synthesis repairs it: the action quantum sets a floor — a circulation confined to size $d$ must carry relative rate. So the ledger has two columns:

$$B \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{\text{bonds}} \delta_{\text{gross}}}_{\text{contact discount}} \;-\; \underbrace{\sum_{\text{quanta}} \tau}_{\text{localization tax}}, \qquad \tau \ge \frac{9}{8}\frac{\hbar^2/m}{\langle r^2\rangle}\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\Big).$$

4 · The capacity law — and why packing saturates

Bulk matter breaks the flat line: per bond, the bulk gross discount is only $5.97$ MeV — the data force a saturation of ×2.5 (an unsaturated close packing would give $a_v \approx 70$ MeV per quantum against the observed $15.75$). The mechanism is a resource bound. A mesh is a phase lock over a contact patch — a cap of solid angle $\Omega_w$ on the quantum's circulation sphere. The sphere is finite: $z$ patches saturate it when $z\,\Omega_w > 4\pi$:

$$\delta_{\text{eff}}(z) = \delta_0\,\min\!\big(1,\; z_c/z\big),\qquad z_c = \frac{4\pi}{\Omega_w}, \qquad z_c = \frac{12 \times 5.97}{\delta_0} = 4.78 \pm 0.35 \;\;(\theta_w = 54.5^\circ,\; C = \tfrac{z_c}{2}\delta_0 = 35.9\text{ MeV}).$$

Partners beyond capacity share arc and add nothing ("saturation of nuclear forces" — comparative label — as solid-angle bookkeeping). Consistency with no dial: the alpha ($z=3$) and triton ($z=2$) sit below $z_c$, which is exactly why §3 found them at full strength.

THEOREM Saturation at capacity. Balancing tax against capped profit, the equilibrium density is pinned at the capacity point $z(\rho_0) = z_c$ — a kink minimum, robust for any contact exponent $\nu > 0.373$ and any clash cost including zero. No hard core is required: expansion loses profit faster than it saves tax; compression gains nothing (profit capped) and pays tax. Two independent routes meet with nothing tuned: geometric contact count from measured density and sizes gives $z = 4.5$–$5.3$; the energy ledger above gives $z_c = 4.78 \pm 0.35$. Prediction (kink signature): the interior density profile meets $\rho_0$ at finite depth — only the outer tail is exponential.
