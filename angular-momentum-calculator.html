Keren Li’s Angular-Momentum Calculator

This exhibit is an extrapolation of the foundational work of N. Vivian “Viv” Pope & Dr. Anthony D. Osborne, PhD, in the philosophy of Normal Realism (NR) and the mathematics of the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS).

A reconstruction of the Java/Excel equations, with their finite-body domain made explicit. For a fixed nonzero energy gap and geometrically similar bodies, shrinking the radius drives the required spin magnitude upward without bound.

Original archive: preserved intact Core result: mass cancels Correction: no point-body spin shortcut

Two layers, kept separate

Historical layer

Keren’s calculator compares a constrained orbit with a natural-orbit state under the adopted relations in the notes. The Java/Excel reconstruction stores the energy-gap magnitude and a separate branch sign:

v* = GME/(v rO)
KS = 1/2 m |v*² − v²|
ω  = sign(v*² − v²) √(2KS/I)
Premises behind v*

The handwritten positive-spin example adopts G*/v* = G/v and G* mME/rO² = m v*²/rO. Eliminating G* gives v* = GME/(v rO). Its energy relation is KO + KS = m v*²/2.

The Java/Excel negative branch instead records KS = KO − m v*²/2 ≥ 0 and a negative angular-velocity label. This is the recovered signed-gap convention, not a derivation of negative kinetic energy or a measured handedness effect.

Modern correction

For positive mass and radius, the model uses finite moment of inertia. Hold v, rO, G, ME and the shape factor C fixed while reducing R. If the energy gap is nonzero, the required magnitude diverges:

I = C m R²
ω² = |v*² − v²|/(C R²)
|v*² − v²| > 0, C > 0 fixed:
R → 0 ⇒ |ω| → ∞

If v² = GME/rO, the gap is zero and ω = 0 at every finite R. At R = 0 the original energy/inertia quotient is undefined. For a torus or hollow cylinder, fixed C means fixed a/R: both radii shrink together.

Historical equations, calculated

The presets reproduce the preserved examples. Modern input and precision checks prevent undefined outputs; blank fields are not silently replaced with Java defaults. All calculator outputs retain the historical mechanical units.

The sign branch is automatic: if the constrained orbital kinetic energy is larger than the natural-orbit kinetic energy, the calculator returns negative spin.

Why mass does not rescue it

Both the required spin energy and the rigid-body moment of inertia scale linearly with body mass. For any fixed geometry:

KS = 1/2 m |v*² − v²|
I  = C m R²
ω² = 2KS/I = |v*² − v²|/(C R²)

The mass disappears. This is the sharp result in Keren’s work: density changes do not fix the spin requirement when the size and shape stay fixed.

The preserved 2013 result

The handwritten pages use a 100 kg, 0.3 m-radius solid sphere with Earth-surface co-rotation values. Recomputing their inputs gives the following rounded result:

v* ≈ 134,658 m/s
KO ≈ 1.08 × 10⁷ J
KS ≈ 9.07 × 10¹¹ J
I  = 3.6 kg m²
ω  ≈ 7.10 × 10⁵ rad/s ≈ 1.13 × 10⁵ rev/s

Historical wording corrected: the notes say “geosynchronous,” but the numbers are Earth radius and equatorial surface speed. That means co-rotating at Earth angular rate at the surface, not a satellite at true geosynchronous radius.

Ledger bridge (rate ontology): the joules above are preserved historical mechanical units. With h = 6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J s, the native aggregate rate is KS/h ≈ 1.3683×10⁴⁵ turns/s. Bulk kinetic energy is distributed rate-increase, not a new substance. This calculator does not determine the occupant census, the distribution of that increment, or its fraction of each occupant’s baseline. Even an assumed census of 10²⁷ would supply only a mean increment of approximately 1.3683×10¹⁸ turns/s, not the claim that every increment is small.

Normal Realism / POAMS reading

Read historically, this is an early AM-first feasibility calculation: the supplied surface-co-rotation examples require very large rigid-body spin rates under their stated premises. That conclusion is not universal over all inputs: the equal-energy case requires no added spin.

Read through the current touchstone, it also marks a trap. Orbit and intrinsic spin are distinct channels. This finite rigid-body calculation cannot be extrapolated into a model of intrinsic twist by silently replacing its moment of inertia with a point body. Its conditional size limit does not impose a limit on every POAMS spin-control hypothesis.

What this does not prove: no Hayasaka-style handedness/sign claim is present in the notes. Sign belongs to the modern channel question—twist through variable-G versus writhe through orbital AM—not to a retroactive claim about Keren’s calculation.
