§2 — The Derivation

A body of tangled trapped rate is an ensemble of closed accumulator loops whose individual $Lk = \pm 1$ orientations sum to zero in any direction over the whole body. Each loop still continuously completes cycles — a closure cannot complete “in place” because completion is a hand-off — and each cycle-completion routes through the surrounding nexus. Aggregated across the body's $\Omega$ total turning rate, the total rate of nexus-completion demand is:

$$\dot{N}_M \;=\; \frac{Mc^2}{h}$$

This is the mass's tangled-trapped-rate sink rate — the rate at which the body's constituent closures collectively demand completion routing from the surrounding nexus. It is sign-agnostic and directionally isotropic (because the constituent orientations average to zero).

Depletion pattern in the nexus

The nexus supports completion propagation at $c$; the number-density of completions in the nexus is $\rho_N$ with a characteristic diffusivity $D$ (dimensions $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$; a shared property of the nexus, common to this derivation and to Coulomb). Around a spherically-symmetric tangled sink at the origin, in steady state, the completion-density distribution satisfies the spherical-shell balance: the completion flux crossing every sphere of radius $r$ must equal $\dot{N}_M$ (all completions leaving the sink must pass through that sphere).

$$\rho_N(r) \;=\; \rho_N(\infty) \;-\; \frac{\dot{N}_M}{4\pi D r}$$

The near-source completion count density is lower than the far-field value by a factor that scales as $1/r$. This is the geometry of any spherically-symmetric spherical-shell balance in three dimensions; $1/r$ is not chosen — it is the shape the geometry allows.

Coupling to a test body

A test body of tangled trapped-rate $m_{test}$ placed at radius $r$ has its own sink. Its closures preferentially route their completions where the nexus's local completion-density gradient makes the routing favourable — the natural configuration of the test body's closures shifts toward the source's depletion pocket (extant label: “gravitational attraction”). What extant physics reads as an attractive force is this ledger fact: the test body's natural configuration has it closer to the source; if held off that natural configuration by a scale or support, the support-force it must supply is the constraint bill (extant label: “weight”).

Working through the coupling of the test body's sink to the source's depletion gradient and identifying constants against SI:

$$g(r) \;=\; \left(\frac{Gh}{c^2}\right) \frac{\dot{N}_M}{r^2}$$

Substituting $\dot{N}_M = Mc^2/h$:

$$\boxed{\;g(r) \;=\; \frac{GM}{r^2}\;}$$

Newton's $1/r^2$ acceleration is the derived consequence. The $1/r^2$ shape is spherical-shell geometry (a $1/r$ potential gives a $1/r^2$ gradient). The proportionality to $M$ is the sink-rate proportionality $\dot{N}_M = Mc^2/h$. The coupling coefficient $Gh/c^2$ is fixed by the nexus's diffusivity and background completion density (see §4). Nothing is postulated as a force; nothing crosses a gap; a free test body rides the natural configuration the ledger assigns it, weightless. Weight, when a body is held off that configuration, is the constraint bill.

§3 — Cross-checks Against Known Behaviour

$F = m_{test} \cdot g(r) = GMm_{test}/r^2$, the classical Newtonian gravitational force expression. The test body's natural configuration is closer to the source; any support holding it off that natural configuration produces the constraint force conventionally called weight.

Reproduces superposition for many-body configurations

For an assembly of many mass sources, each contributes its own tangled-depletion pattern to the shared nexus. This reproduces the Newtonian superposition principle (Poisson's equation with source density $\rho_M$) without additional POAMS commitment. Long-range limit matches Newton exactly

The derivation gives the leading $1/r$ pair potential exactly. Bertrand-clean orbital geometry
