§2 — The Derivation

A body of tangled trapped rate is an ensemble of closed accumulator loops whose individual $Lk = \pm 1$ orientations sum to zero in any direction over the whole body. Each loop still continuously completes cycles — a closure cannot complete “in place” because completion is a hand-off — and each cycle-completion routes through the surrounding nexus. Aggregated across the body's $\Omega$ total turning rate, the total rate of nexus-completion demand is:

$$\dot{N}_M \;=\; \frac{Mc^2}{h}$$

This is the mass's tangled-trapped-rate sink rate — the rate at which the body's constituent closures collectively demand completion routing from the surrounding nexus. It is sign-agnostic and directionally isotropic (because the constituent orientations average to zero).

Depletion pattern in the nexus

The nexus supports completion propagation at $c$; the number-density of completions in the nexus is $\rho_N$ with a characteristic diffusivity $D$ (dimensions $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$; a shared property of the nexus, common to this derivation and to Coulomb). Around a spherically-symmetric tangled sink at the origin, in steady state, the completion-density distribution satisfies the spherical-shell balance: the completion flux crossing every sphere of radius $r$ must equal $\dot{N}_M$ (all completions leaving the sink must pass through that sphere).

$$\rho_N(r) \;=\; \rho_N(\infty) \;-\; \frac{\dot{N}_M}{4\pi D r}$$

The near-source completion count density is lower than the far-field value by a factor that scales as $1/r$. This is the geometry of any spherically-symmetric spherical-shell balance in three dimensions; $1/r$ is not chosen — it is the shape the geometry allows.

Coupling to a test body

A test body of tangled trapped-rate $m_{test}$ placed at radius $r$ has its own sink. Its closures preferentially route their completions where the nexus's local completion-density gradient makes the routing favourable — the natural configuration of the test body's closures shifts toward the source's depletion pocket (extant label: “gravitational attraction”). What extant physics reads as an attractive force is this ledger fact: the test body's natural configuration has it closer to the source; if held off that natural configuration by a scale or support, the support-force it must supply is the constraint bill (extant label: “weight”).

Working through the coupling of the test body's sink to the source's depletion gradient and identifying constants against SI:

$$g(r) \;=\; \left(\frac{Gh}{c^2}\right) \frac{\dot{N}_M}{r^2}$$

Substituting $\dot{N}_M = Mc^2/h$:

$$\boxed{\;g(r) \;=\; \frac{GM}{r^2}\;}$$

Newton's $1/r^2$ acceleration is the derived consequence. The $1/r^2$ shape is spherical-shell geometry (a $1/r$ potential gives a $1/r^2$ gradient). The proportionality to $M$ is the sink-rate proportionality $\dot{N}_M = Mc^2/h$. The coupling coefficient $Gh/c^2$ is fixed by the nexus's diffusivity and background completion density (see §4). Nothing is postulated as a force; nothing crosses a gap; a free test body rides the natural configuration the ledger assigns it, weightless. Weight, when a body is held off that configuration, is the constraint bill.

§3 — Cross-checks Against Known Behaviour

Reproduces the $1/r^2$ force expression

$F = m_{test} \cdot g(r) = GMm_{test}/r^2$, the classical Newtonian gravitational force expression. The test body's natural configuration is closer to the source; any support holding it off that natural configuration produces the constraint force conventionally called weight.

Reproduces superposition for many-body configurations

For an assembly of many mass sources, each contributes its own tangled-depletion pattern to the shared nexus. The pattern at any point is the sum of individual contributions (additive for small perturbations of $\rho_N$). This reproduces the Newtonian superposition principle (Poisson's equation with source density $\rho_M$) without additional POAMS commitment. Equivalently, Gauss's law for gravity falls out of the spherical-shell flux balance.

Two-endedness, not signal propagation

The pair coupling is a proper-time two-ended exchange between source and test-body closures, each reading the nexus's local completion-density gradient (per The Receiver Keys the Exchange §7). No graviton, no field carrier, no signal propagating through a container. The observer's apparent $r/c$ delay under specific measurement configurations is optical projection of the two-endedness onto their space-projection, not something travelling.

Long-range limit matches Newton exactly

The derivation gives the leading $1/r$ pair potential exactly. Corrections at subleading order — the Kerr-analog $1/r^3$ term from coherent angular momentum (per Counter-Rotating Vortex Cores §2) and higher-order geometric corrections yielding perihelion precession (per Perihelion Precession) — attach to this leading term additively without changing its form. Newton is the tangled-body long-range limit; POAMS reduces to Newton in that limit exactly.

Bertrand-clean orbital geometry

The derived $1/r$ potential is one of exactly two central-force potentials that produce closed orbits for arbitrary initial conditions (Bertrand's theorem). This closes the solar-system observational anchor: closed Keplerian orbits are what the derived $1/r$ potential produces, and what nature displays.

§4 — The Coupling Coefficient: What's Derived, What's Fitted, and What Would Close It

The §2 derivation of Newton's $1/r^2$ shape from spherical-shell nexus depletion is complete: the geometry is exact, the proportionality to $M$ comes from $\dot{N}_M = Mc^2/h$, and the coupling coefficient carries the value $Gh/c^2$ by identifying constants against SI. This section examines the — separate — question of whether $G$ itself is derived from POAMS or whether it enters as a fitted numerical input. The honest answer is that $G$ is currently a fitted input; the framework below names what an independent POAMS-native derivation would need to supply.

$$D \cdot \rho_N(\infty) \;=\; \frac{\dot{N}_M}{4\pi R \cdot (\Delta\tau/\tau)}\bigg|_{\text{Earth surface}}, \qquad \Delta\tau/\tau \approx 6.95 \times 10^{-10}$$
and then announced $G$ as "derived" from that product. This is circular: the input $\Delta\tau/\tau$ used to set the equation is numerically identical to $GM_\oplus/(R_\oplus c^2)$ — the standard weak-field expression that already contains $G$. Substituting $\Delta\tau/\tau = GM_\oplus/(R_\oplus c^2)$ back into the equation reduces it to the identity $G = G$, not a derivation. The exhibit retracts the prior "$G$ is derived" claim on this basis. The numerical consistency check below still holds (with $G$ treated as one fitted input): under the Planck-length commitment for $\lambda$, $D = c\lambda/3 \approx 1.6\times 10^{-27}$ m$^2$/s and $\rho_N(\infty) \sim 9 \times 10^{102}$ m$^{-3}$; under a nucleon-scale commitment the split shifts by many orders. The Planck-length commitment for $\lambda$ is a POAMS modeling choice, not an independently-anchored derivation.

Framework: what an independent POAMS derivation of $G$ would need

A POAMS-native derivation of Newton's coupling coefficient would supply the SI value of $G$ from POAMS-foundational quantities without using $G$-dependent measured inputs (like weak-field clock ratios or measured gravitational potentials). Three candidate paths, listed by decreasing dependence on external measurement:

  1. Direct derivation from POAMS primitives. Derive the nexus's $D \cdot \rho_N(\infty)$ product from first-principles POAMS quantities — the discreteum's characteristic length, the elementary $\hbar$ quantum, and $c$. If POAMS supplies a first-principles value for either $D$ or $\rho_N(\infty)$ (rather than a fitted product), then $G = (h/c^2) \times (\text{sink coupling strength}) / (D \cdot \rho_N(\infty))$ becomes a POAMS-native prediction. The current exhibit does not supply either factor from first principles; the "sink coupling strength" term itself is asserted, not derived.
  2. Ratio to $\alpha$ via the alpha-derivation family. If POAMS derives the fine-structure ratio $\alpha$ from first principles (per the corpus alpha-derivation program, see The Alpha Problem), and if POAMS gravitational coupling is related to $\alpha$ by a POAMS-computable ratio, then $G$ follows from $\alpha$. The relationship is not currently established; this is a research target that would close both open fronts simultaneously.
  3. Match to a $G$-independent observable. Pick a gravitational observable whose measurement does not itself use $G$ — e.g. a Cavendish-style laboratory torsion-balance measurement of the force between two known masses at known separation — then $G$ is fixed by the POAMS-native prediction of that force. This is not an independent derivation of $G$; it is honest empirical calibration of one POAMS parameter. Path (3) upgrades §2's "no adjustable parameter" claim to "one empirically-calibrated coupling, no additional adjustable parameters" — the strongest claim honestly available today.
